The three-ingredient peanut butter cookie is a recipe that
shouldn't work and does anyway. One cup of peanut butter. One cup
of sugar. One egg. That's it. No flour, no butter, no baking soda.
The peanut butter contributes the fat AND the protein structure;
the sugar dissolves into the egg as you mix; the egg holds it
together. The texture is dense and chewy, the flavour is
unrepentantly peanut-buttery, and the cookies are gluten-free by
default — which matters if you have a flour-allergic friend.

The fork-marks on top are mandatory. They're not decorative — they
flatten the dough (which won't spread on its own without flour),
and they're the visual signature of the recipe. The criss-cross
pattern is the cultural marker that says "this is a peanut butter
cookie" even from across the room.

The photo shows them on a mustard-yellow plate (a colour scheme
that's specifically right for these), seven cookies plus crumbs,
with a glass of milk that's been hit with a moment of foam from
pouring fresh. One cookie is broken at the corner showing the
dense, slightly molten interior. The crackled tops are golden-brown
on the high points where the fork pressed down; lighter in the
valleys.

A note on peanut butter: use natural peanut butter (just peanuts +
salt — the oil that separates and you stir back in). The processed
kind (Skippy, Jif) has added sugar and stabilisers that change the
texture. The cookies will still work; they'll just be sweeter and
slightly oilier.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup (250g) natural peanut butter (creamy or crunchy)
- 1 cup (200g) granulated sugar (plus 2 tbsp for rolling)
- 1 large egg
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- ½ tsp baking soda
- Pinch of salt (skip if peanut butter is salted)

**Optional add-ins:**
- ½ cup chocolate chips
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon
- 1 tsp espresso powder

## Method

### 01. Mix.
In a bowl, combine the peanut butter, 1 cup sugar, egg, vanilla,
baking soda, and salt. Stir with a wooden spoon until smooth and
fully combined — about 1 minute. The mixture should be thick and
slightly tacky.

### 02. Shape.
Cut a square of parchment to fit the air fryer basket. Scoop the
dough by the tablespoon and roll into balls between your palms.
You should get 12. Roll each ball in the extra 2 tbsp sugar.

### 03. Fork-mark.
Place 4–6 balls on the parchment, leaving space between (they
won't spread much but they swell). Press each one with the back
of a fork in one direction, then crosswise — making the classic
criss-cross pattern.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 320°F (160°C). Place the parchment with
cookies in the basket. Cook 7–9 minutes, until the edges are
firm and the tops are golden. The middles will look slightly
underdone — that's correct.

### 05. Cool on the parchment.
Don't try to move them right away — they're fragile straight out
of the air fryer. Let cool on the parchment for 5 minutes, then
transfer to a rack.

### 06. Repeat with the second batch.
Use a fresh piece of parchment for the second batch (the first
one will have some oil on it). Same time and temperature.

## Notes

**Critical detail**: don't crowd the basket. These need air
circulation to set properly. 4–6 per batch is the right number for
a standard 4-quart air fryer. They keep 5 days in an airtight
container at room temperature, and they freeze well (uncooked or
cooked). For a 5-minute chocolate variation, press a chocolate kiss
or a square of chocolate into the centre of each ball right after
they come out of the air fryer — the residual heat melts it. For
crunchy peanut butter texture, use crunchy peanut butter. Don't
double the recipe; do two batches.
