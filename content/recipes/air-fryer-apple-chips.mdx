The apple chip occupies a strange space in the snack landscape. It's
not a chip in any structural sense — it doesn't shatter like a kettle
chip, it doesn't have salt, it doesn't crunch the way a potato chip
crunches. What it is, is a slow-dehydrated apple slice with cinnamon
dusted on top, that ends up somewhere between a thin cracker and a
particularly aggressive raisin. Sweet, faintly chewy at first bite,
fully crisp after a minute on the counter.

The technique is more "low-and-slow dehydration" than "air frying."
You slice the apples paper-thin (a mandoline is non-optional unless
your knife skills are exceptional), dust with cinnamon, lay them out
in the basket, and cook at 300°F — not the usual 400°F — for about
twenty minutes. The lower heat allows moisture to evaporate without
scorching the surface. They go pale-tan as they dry, the edges curl
slightly, and they firm up to crisp as they cool on a rack.

This is a parent's snack. It feels healthful (it more or less is —
apple, cinnamon, no added sugar), it's safe to make with kids (no
oil, no high heat), and it produces something that satisfies a sweet
craving without being a dessert. The photo shows a glass bowl piled
with crisped apple slices, the cinnamon visible as a slight brown
dust on each piece, on a yellow gingham kitchen towel against a
wood cutting board. Pure cottagecore wholesomeness, executed in a
$60 appliance.

## Ingredients

- 2 large apples (Honeycrisp, Pink Lady, or Granny Smith), cored
  but unpeeled
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- Pinch of nutmeg (optional)
- 1 tsp powdered sugar (optional, just for sweet apples)
- Squeeze of lemon (optional, prevents browning during slicing)

## Method

### 01. Slice the apples.
Core the apples but don't peel — the skin holds the slices
together. Using a mandoline (set to about 1/8-inch / 3mm), slice
the apples crosswise into rounds. Aim for paper-thin and uniform.
If you don't have a mandoline, the sharpest knife you own, very
carefully.

### 02. Optional: lemon squeeze.
If you're not cooking them immediately, toss the slices with a
small squeeze of lemon juice to prevent browning. Skip if going
right into the air fryer.

### 03. Dust with cinnamon.
Spread the slices on a plate or sheet pan. Sprinkle evenly with
cinnamon (and nutmeg / powdered sugar if using). Toss gently to
distribute.

### 04. Air fry low and slow.
Heat the air fryer to 300°F (150°C) — lower than usual. Arrange
the slices in a single layer in the basket (work in 2 or 3
batches if needed — overlap a little is OK, they shrink). Cook
18–22 minutes, shaking the basket every 6 minutes, until the
edges curl up and the slices look mostly dry.

### 05. Cool on a rack.
Transfer to a wire rack as they come out. They'll firm up to
fully crisp within 3–5 minutes of cooling. If they're still bendy
after cooling, return to the basket for 3 minutes more.

### 06. Store.
Keep in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 5
days. If they soften, refresh in the air fryer for 2 minutes at
300°F.

## Notes

The variety of apple matters less than you'd think — sweeter ones
(Honeycrisp, Gala) need no added sugar, tarter ones (Granny Smith)
benefit from the optional powdered sugar dust. Don't peel — the
skin is the structural element. Don't try to do thicker slices —
they stay chewy, not crisp. For a savoury version, skip the
cinnamon and dust with sea salt + smoked paprika. Pear chips work
with exactly the same method (slightly faster — about 16 minutes).
