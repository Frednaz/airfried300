There is a class of pasta salad that gives the genre a bad name — the
sad bowl of overcooked rotini drowned in bottled Italian dressing,
served at every family barbecue from 1985 onward. This is the other
kind. The kind that has lemon zest in the dressing, fresh herbs you
chopped this morning, parmesan microplaned over the top so it tastes
like cheese instead of dust.

The trick is dressing the pasta while it's still warm. Hot pasta drinks
in the dressing in a way that cold pasta refuses. So you boil the
pasta, drain it, transfer it directly to a bowl with the olive oil and
lemon juice and let it cool there — it picks up the flavour as it
loses heat. By the time it's room temperature, every bowtie is
seasoned through.

The photo is the bowl — bowtie pasta visible, flecks of green from
parsley and dill, parmesan shaved on top, chicken pieces folded
through. Lemon wedges at the edge for additional squeezing. This is
the summer-lunch pasta salad, the one you bring to a picnic in a glass
bowl and watch disappear before the burgers are off the grill.

## Ingredients

**For the pasta salad:**
- 1 lb bowtie pasta (farfalle)
- 2 cups cooked, diced chicken (rotisserie or grilled)
- ¼ cup grated parmesan (plus more to finish)
- ¼ cup fresh parsley, chopped
- 2 tbsp fresh dill, chopped (or 2 tsp dried)
- 2 tbsp fresh chives, sliced
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper

**For the dressing:**
- ⅓ cup extra-virgin olive oil
- ¼ cup fresh lemon juice (about 1 large lemon)
- 1 tbsp lemon zest
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- ½ tsp kosher salt

## Method

### 01. Boil the pasta.
Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Cook the pasta to al
dente — usually 1 minute LESS than the package says. Drain (don't
rinse).

### 02. Whisk the dressing.
While the pasta cooks, whisk together the olive oil, lemon juice and
zest, garlic, Dijon, oregano, and salt until emulsified.

### 03. Combine while warm.
Transfer the drained pasta to a large bowl. Pour the dressing over the
warm pasta and toss — the warmth helps the pasta absorb the dressing.
Let cool for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.

### 04. Add the rest.
Once the pasta is just-warm (not steaming), add the diced chicken,
parmesan, parsley, dill, chives, salt, and pepper. Toss to combine.

### 05. Rest and serve.
Cover and refrigerate for at least 30 minutes (or eat at room
temperature). Before serving, taste — if it tastes flat, add another
squeeze of lemon. Finish with extra parmesan shaved over the top.

## Notes

This is better the next day — the herbs deepen, the lemon mellows. It
keeps 4 days refrigerated. For a sturdier version: add halved cherry
tomatoes, chunks of cucumber, kalamata olives, and crumbled feta —
it becomes a Greek-leaning pasta salad. For meal-prep, portion into
individual containers; the dressing doesn't separate badly. If you
must reheat (you really shouldn't), do it briefly and add a splash of
olive oil after.
