There is an entire category of recipe that exists because someone
realised they didn't have to make dough from scratch. The biscuit-
dough doughnut is the patron saint of that category. You pop open
a can of refrigerated biscuit dough — yes, the kind that makes
"buttermilk biscuits" — and using a cap or a small cookie cutter,
you cut a hole out of the centre of each one. The leftover dough
holes become doughnut holes; the rings become doughnuts. Air-fry,
toss in cinnamon sugar, serve warm with coffee.

The result is genuinely close to a fresh cake doughnut from a good
bakery. The interior is slightly denser than a yeasted doughnut but
softer than the standard "cake" doughnut at a chain. The crackled
cinnamon-sugar exterior shatters at the first bite, then gives way
to a steamy, slightly tangy biscuit interior that hits you with
that pleasant buttermilk note.

The photo shows the finish: three doughnuts in a parchment-lined
container, deeply coated in cinnamon sugar so the surface looks
almost gritty. The biscuit dough's natural texture shows through in
the slight irregularity of the rings — they're not perfectly round,
and that's good. It's homemade-doughnut-shaped, not factory-
doughnut-shaped.

A note on the biscuit dough: the recipe works with most refrigerated
biscuit doughs (Pillsbury Grands, Annie's, store brands), but
flakier biscuit doughs (the ones that pull apart in layers) work
slightly better than the smooth-domed ones — they give you more
crackle on the surface.

## Ingredients

- 1 can (16.3 oz / 462g) refrigerated biscuit dough (8 biscuits,
  the "Grands" size)
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
- ½ cup (100g) granulated sugar
- 1½ tbsp ground cinnamon
- ¼ tsp kosher salt

**For glazed version (optional, instead of cinnamon-sugar):**
- 1 cup powdered sugar
- 2 tbsp whole milk
- ½ tsp vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

## Method

### 01. Cut the doughnuts.
Lay the biscuit rounds on a board. Use a small round cutter (½-inch
to ¾-inch, or the wide end of a piping tip, or the cap of a
ketchup bottle — anything small enough) to punch a hole through
the centre of each one. Save the holes — they make excellent
doughnut holes.

### 02. Brush with butter.
Brush both sides of each doughnut and each hole lightly with
melted butter. This is what gives the surface its golden colour
and helps the cinnamon sugar stick.

### 03. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 350°F (175°C). Arrange the doughnut rings in
a single layer in the basket, leaving space between them (they
puff). Cook 5–6 minutes — flip at the 3-minute mark — until both
sides are deep golden. The holes only take 3–4 minutes total.

### 04. Make the cinnamon sugar.
While the doughnuts cook, combine the granulated sugar, cinnamon,
and salt in a shallow bowl.

### 05. Coat hot.
Pull the doughnuts from the basket and, while still warm and
slightly steamy, toss them in the cinnamon sugar to coat
generously. The moisture from the steam makes the sugar cling.
Repeat with the holes.

### 06. Serve immediately.
Eat while warm. These are best within 30 minutes of cooking — they
go from doughnut to dense biscuit-pastry as they cool.

## Notes

**For the classic glazed version**: whisk powdered sugar, milk, and
vanilla into a thin glaze. Dip the warm (not hot) doughnuts top-
down in the glaze and let drip on a rack for 1 minute before
serving. **For an apple cider variation**: replace the milk in the
glaze with apple cider, add an extra pinch of cinnamon. Reheating
leftover doughnuts in the air fryer for 90 seconds at 320°F mostly
brings them back to life. Don't try to make these with
crescent-roll dough — different texture, doesn't puff the same way.
