The twice-baked potato is the steakhouse side that nobody makes at home
because of its perceived effort. Bake the potato, scoop it out, mash
the inside, restuff it, bake it again — it does sound like a project.
But the project is mostly waiting, which the air fryer cuts in half.
First bake: 40 minutes at 380°F (vs. 60 in the oven). Second bake: 8
minutes. The total time is just over an hour, but only fifteen minutes
of that is hands-on, and the result is the most respectable potato
side dish there is.

The photo shows the finish: three halves on a gray plate, the filling
piled mountainous and topped with sharp cheddar that's melted into the
crevices, crispy bacon crumbles, fresh chives. The skin underneath is
still crisp from the first bake. You break through the cheese into the
soft mash and find streaks of bacon and onion — it's a baked potato
that decided to become a casserole.

A note on the potato: russets are non-negotiable for this. Yukon golds
turn waxy when twice-baked; sweet potatoes are a different recipe.
You want the dry, fluffy texture that russets give you when their
interior has been mashed with dairy fat.

## Ingredients

- 2 large russet potatoes (about 12 oz each), scrubbed
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp kosher salt (for the skins)
- 4 strips bacon, cooked and crumbled
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
- ⅓ cup sour cream
- ½ cup shredded sharp cheddar, divided
- 2 tbsp chives, chopped (divided, plus more to garnish)
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- 2 scallions, finely sliced (optional)

## Method

### 01. First bake.
Pierce each potato several times with a fork. Rub all over with olive
oil and sprinkle with salt — the salt crusts onto the skin. Heat the
air fryer to 380°F (193°C). Cook 38–42 minutes, until a fork slides
easily through to the center.

### 02. Cool slightly and halve.
Let the potatoes cool 10 minutes — long enough that you can handle
them. Cut each potato in half lengthwise.

### 03. Scoop the filling.
Using a spoon, scoop the soft interior into a bowl, leaving a ¼-inch
shell of potato attached to the skin so the boats hold their shape.

### 04. Mash the filling.
To the bowl, add the butter, sour cream, half the cheddar, half the
chives, salt, pepper, and most of the bacon (save some for the top).
Mash until smooth but with a little texture remaining.

### 05. Restuff.
Pile the filling back into the four potato shells, mounded high. Top
each with the remaining cheddar and the reserved bacon.

### 06. Second bake.
Place the stuffed potatoes back in the air fryer at 400°F (200°C) for
6–8 minutes, until the cheese is bubbling and the tops are golden in
spots.

### 07. Finish and serve.
Scatter the remaining chives (and scallions, if using) over the top
and serve immediately.

## Notes

To make ahead: complete through step 5, refrigerate up to 24 hours,
then second-bake for 12 minutes at 400°F (longer because they're cold).
For a freezer version: wrap individually after step 5, freeze up to a
month, cook from frozen for 18 minutes at 380°F. Variations: jalapeño
+ pepper jack instead of cheddar, ranch seasoning in the mash, smoked
gouda for a less obvious version. Serve as a side to roast chicken,
steak, or just dinner-by-itself with a green salad.
