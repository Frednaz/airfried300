The cookie pie is the dessert equivalent of upgrading your seat to
business class — same components as a regular cookie, slightly
absurd extra space, much more satisfying outcome. Instead of twelve
cookies you have one giant cookie. Instead of dough on the outside
and dough on the inside, you have dough on both sides and a layer of
melted chocolate-hazelnut spread in the middle, which oozes out the
moment you cut a slice.

The photo shows it mid-slice on a wooden round board, dusted with
powdered sugar, one triangle pulled out so you can see the molten
Nutella layer in the middle. This is the kind of dessert that takes
20 minutes to make and earns disproportionate gasps from anyone
who arrives at the table while it's still warm.

The air fryer is genuinely the right tool for this because of the
ceiling: it cooks from above, which sets the top of the cookie
quickly while the bottom and middle stay soft. You want the
exterior to crackle when cut, the interior to be barely-set, and
the chocolate layer to be fully molten. A regular oven works too,
but you'd need to pull it precisely at the moment when the centre
is still warm enough to ooze — and most home ovens don't bake that
evenly.

A note on the pan: this needs a small (6-inch / 15cm) round
cake-pan-sized vessel that fits in your air fryer basket. A
springform pan with a removable bottom is best, but a regular
cake tin lined with parchment works. The Nutella layer is the
secret weapon, but peanut butter or dulce de leche both work the
same way.

## Ingredients

**For the cookie dough:**
- ½ cup (115g) unsalted butter, softened
- ⅔ cup (135g) light brown sugar
- ¼ cup (50g) granulated sugar
- 1 large egg
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 1¼ cups (165g) all-purpose flour
- ½ tsp baking soda
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¾ cup (135g) semi-sweet chocolate chips

**For the filling:**
- ½ cup (140g) Nutella (or chocolate-hazelnut spread)

**To finish:**
- Powdered sugar, for dusting
- Vanilla ice cream, to serve (optional but excellent)

## Method

### 01. Make the dough.
Cream the softened butter with both sugars in a bowl with a wooden
spoon (or stand mixer) until pale and fluffy, about 2 minutes. Beat
in the egg and vanilla. Stir in flour, baking soda, and salt until
just combined. Fold in chocolate chips.

### 02. Layer half the dough.
Line a 6-inch round pan with parchment (let it overhang for easy
lifting). Press about ⅔ of the cookie dough evenly into the bottom
of the pan, going slightly up the sides to form a shallow well.

### 03. Spread the Nutella.
Spoon the Nutella into the well and spread evenly, leaving a
¼-inch border around the edge (so it doesn't ooze out the sides
while baking).

### 04. Top with remaining dough.
Flatten chunks of the remaining dough between your palms and lay
them over the Nutella, pressing to seal the edges with the bottom
layer. It's okay if the top is rustic and uneven — that's the look.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 320°F (160°C). Place the pan in the basket
and cook 22–26 minutes — the top should be deeply golden and just
set in the middle, but still soft when pressed. Pull when it's
slightly underdone; it firms up as it cools.

### 06. Cool briefly.
Let cool 10 minutes in the pan — the cookie firms up enough to
slice but the Nutella stays molten. Lift out using the parchment
overhang, dust with powdered sugar.

### 07. Cut and serve.
Slice into 8 wedges. Serve warm with vanilla ice cream — the
molten Nutella + cold ice cream is the entire point.

## Notes

If your air fryer is small and doesn't fit a 6-inch pan, use 4
smaller ramekins instead — divide the dough into 4, layer with
1 tablespoon of Nutella each, air-fry 18 minutes. The cookie pie
keeps 3 days in an airtight container at room temperature, but the
Nutella firms up as it cools — microwave a slice for 15 seconds to
get the ooze back. Swap Nutella for crunchy peanut butter (with a
pinch of salt) for a Reese's-style version.
