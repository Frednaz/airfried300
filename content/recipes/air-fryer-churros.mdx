The churro is one of the great Spanish-Mexican exports — choux-
style dough fried in long ridged tubes, rolled in cinnamon sugar,
eaten with thick hot chocolate for dipping. The "ridged" part is
critical, both visually and structurally: the ridges (made by
piping the dough through a star-tip nozzle) increase surface area,
which means more contact with the hot oil and more crackled
crust per inch of churro.

The deep-fried version is a project — boiling water for the
dough, heating a pot of oil to 375°F, frying batches with a
slotted spoon. The air-fryer version simplifies the front end
(you still make the dough on the stove) but eliminates the oil
bath. You pipe the dough directly into the air fryer basket and
cook at 380°F for 10 minutes. The result isn't quite as crackling
as a properly deep-fried churro (the air fryer's drier heat
creates a slightly tougher crust), but it's about 90% of the
way there, with much less drama.

The photo shows them in their festival-stand presentation: three
small galvanised metal cones lined with white parchment paper,
each holding 5–6 churros, generously coated in cinnamon sugar.
The ridges are pronounced. The colour is deep amber where the
ridges hit the hot air, lighter in the valleys. Nuestras churros,
hechos en casa.

A note on the dough: it's a choux paste (the same dough used for
éclairs and gougères) — flour cooked into boiling water and
butter, then eggs beaten in one at a time. Don't try to skip
this; rolled-pastry doughs don't pipe into proper churros.

## Ingredients

**For the dough:**
- ½ cup (115ml) water
- ½ cup (115ml) whole milk
- ¼ cup (55g) unsalted butter
- 2 tbsp granulated sugar
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- 1 cup (130g) all-purpose flour
- 2 large eggs
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- Olive oil spray

**For coating:**
- ½ cup (100g) granulated sugar
- 1½ tsp ground cinnamon

**For the chocolate dipping sauce:**
- 4 oz (115g) dark chocolate (60–70%), chopped
- ½ cup (115ml) heavy cream
- 2 tbsp light corn syrup (or honey)
- Pinch of salt

## Method

### 01. Make the dough.
In a saucepan over medium heat, combine water, milk, butter,
sugar, and salt. Bring to a boil, then immediately reduce to low.

### 02. Add flour.
Dump in the flour all at once. Stir vigorously with a wooden spoon
until the mixture pulls away from the sides of the pan and forms
a smooth dough ball, about 90 seconds. Off heat.

### 03. Let cool 3 minutes.
The dough needs to cool slightly so the eggs don't scramble when
added. Transfer to a bowl if it speeds the cooling.

### 04. Beat in the eggs.
Add the eggs one at a time, beating fully into the dough before
adding the next. Stir in the vanilla. The dough should be glossy,
smooth, and stiff enough to hold a shape.

### 05. Pipe into the basket.
Fit a piping bag with a large star tip (1M or similar). Fill the
bag with the dough. Heat the air fryer to 380°F (193°C). Cut a
piece of parchment to fit the basket. Pipe 4-inch lengths of
dough onto the parchment, leaving space between (they puff
slightly). Use kitchen scissors to cut each one as you finish
piping.

### 06. Spray and air fry.
Spritz the piped churros all over with olive oil. Slide the
parchment into the air fryer. Cook 9–11 minutes, until the
ridges are deep golden. No flipping needed.

### 07. Coat in cinnamon sugar.
While churros cook, mix the granulated sugar and cinnamon in a
shallow bowl. As soon as the churros come out, roll each one
in the cinnamon sugar — the residual oil makes it stick.

### 08. Make the chocolate sauce.
While churros cool slightly, heat the cream in a small saucepan
until it just starts to steam (don't boil). Off heat, add the
chocolate, corn syrup, and salt. Let sit 2 minutes, then whisk
to a smooth glossy sauce.

### 09. Serve.
Pile the churros into containers or onto a platter. Serve the
warm chocolate sauce alongside for dipping. Eat warm.

## Notes

For a more authentic crunchy texture, increase the temperature to
400°F and reduce the time to 8 minutes — slightly less puff but
more shatter. The dough can be made up to 4 hours ahead and kept
covered at room temperature. For a dulce-de-leche dip (also
traditional), use ½ cup of warmed dulce de leche thinned with 2
tablespoons of cream. Don't try to pipe the dough without a star
tip — round tips give you uniform sticks, not ridged churros.
