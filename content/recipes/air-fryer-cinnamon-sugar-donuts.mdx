The from-scratch yeast donut is a project — make the dough, first
rise, shape, second rise, fry in oil, glaze. It's a wonderful thing to
do on a Sunday morning if you have three hours. This recipe is the
opposite of that. Refrigerated biscuit dough (the Pillsbury kind, in a
can that pops open with satisfying violence), separated into rounds,
hole punched out of the centre with a small cookie cutter (or a shot
glass), air-fried at 350°F for five minutes, tumbled in melted butter
and cinnamon sugar. Done. Eight donuts plus the holes, in twelve
active minutes.

The result is genuinely surprising. Biscuit dough air-fries into
something approaching the texture of a yeast donut — slightly chewy,
soft inside, golden on the outside — and the cinnamon-sugar coating
is the same coating you'd put on a real donut. It's not a perfect
replica, but it's close enough that nobody will notice unless they're
a pastry chef.

The photo shows two formats: two large cinnamon-sugar rings (the
straight donut version), and a cluster of small chocolate-glazed
donut holes with rainbow sprinkles (the holes, dipped in melted
chocolate and topped). Both come from the same can of biscuit dough.
The double act is what makes this a small-event recipe — kids'
breakfast, brunch buffet, late-night-snack situation.

## Ingredients

**For the donuts:**
- 1 can (16.3 oz / 460g) refrigerated buttermilk biscuit dough
  (8 biscuits)
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, melted

**For the cinnamon sugar:**
- ½ cup (100g) granulated sugar
- 1½ tsp ground cinnamon
- Pinch of salt

**For the chocolate-glazed holes (optional):**
- ½ cup (90g) semi-sweet chocolate chips
- 1 tbsp coconut oil (or butter)
- Rainbow sprinkles, for topping

## Method

### 01. Cut the donuts.
Open the can of biscuit dough and separate into 8 rounds. Use a
1-inch cookie cutter (or a clean shot glass, or the wide end of a
piping tip) to punch a hole in the centre of each. Save the holes
for the chocolate-glazed version.

### 02. Mix the cinnamon sugar.
In a small shallow bowl, combine the sugar, cinnamon, and salt.

### 03. Air fry the donuts.
Heat the air fryer to 350°F (175°C). Place 4 donuts at a time in
the basket (don't crowd, they puff). Cook 5–6 minutes, flipping at
the 3-minute mark, until golden on both sides. Repeat with the
remaining 4 donuts and then with the donut holes (3–4 minutes for
holes).

### 04. Butter and sugar.
While still warm, brush each donut all over with melted butter,
then roll in the cinnamon sugar to coat. Set on a wire rack.

### 05. (Optional) Chocolate-glaze the holes.
Microwave the chocolate chips and coconut oil in 30-second bursts,
stirring between, until smooth. Dip the top of each donut hole
into the chocolate, then sprinkle immediately with rainbow
sprinkles. Place on a parchment-lined plate.

### 06. Serve.
Best eaten within an hour of cooking — they soften as they sit.

## Notes

For a glazed version (instead of cinnamon sugar): whisk 1 cup
powdered sugar with 2 tablespoons milk and ½ teaspoon vanilla.
Dip the warm donuts in the glaze, return to a rack to set. For a
pumpkin spice version, swap the cinnamon for pumpkin pie spice.
Refrigerated croissant dough also works (smaller, more delicate
donuts). The biscuit donuts don't keep well — they go stale in
hours. Make them right before serving.
