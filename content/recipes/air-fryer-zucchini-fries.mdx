The zucchini fry is the rare vegetable preparation that translates
fully into the kid-food vernacular. Cut into chip-shaped sticks,
breaded with panko and parmesan, fried until crisp, served with
red dipping sauce — the format is exactly the same as a chicken
finger or a mozzarella stick, which means kids who would otherwise
refuse the underlying vegetable will eat them happily. Adults, too.
It's not that the zucchini is hidden; it's that it's been put into
a structurally familiar context.

The technique is straightforward but has two critical steps. First,
salt the zucchini sticks for 10 minutes to draw out moisture (then
pat dry) — this prevents soggy fries. Second, the breading needs
to be heavy on parmesan, which adds umami AND provides the crisp
golden surface. Pre-grated parmesan (the green-can stuff) doesn't
work; use real Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated coarsely.

The dredge sequence: flour, then egg, then a generous mix of
panko + parmesan + Italian seasoning + garlic powder. Press
firmly into the breading; don't be gentle. Spray with olive oil.
Air-fry at 400°F for 10 minutes. The result is fries that crunch
on the outside, with a tender (not mushy) zucchini interior.

The photo shows them piled in a small oval dish — a tangle of

make when you want to serve a vegetable without anyone realising
they're eating a vegetable.

## Ingredients

**For the fries:**
- 2 medium zucchini (about 1 lb / 450g), cut into 3-inch sticks,
  ½-inch thick
- 1 tsp kosher salt (for sweating)
- ½ cup all-purpose flour
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 1¼ cups (130g) panko breadcrumbs
- ½ cup (50g) grated parmesan (real)
- 1 tsp dried Italian seasoning
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- Olive oil spray

**To serve:**
- 1 cup warmed marinara sauce
- 2 tbsp fresh basil, torn

## Method

### 01. Cut and salt.
Cut the zucchini into 3-inch sticks, about ½-inch thick. Place
in a colander, sprinkle with 1 tsp kosher salt, toss. Let sit 10
minutes — the salt will draw out moisture.

### 02. Pat dry.
Rinse the zucchini briefly under cold water to remove excess
salt, then pat very dry on a kitchen towel. Press another paper
towel on top. This is the moisture-removal step that prevents
sogginess.

### 03. Set up dredging stations.
Three bowls: flour; beaten eggs; panko + parmesan + Italian
seasoning + garlic powder + salt + pepper, stirred together.

### 04. Dredge.
Working in small batches, dust the zucchini sticks in flour, dip
in egg, then press firmly into the panko-parmesan mixture. Press
hard. Set on a plate.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Spray the breaded sticks
all over with olive oil. Arrange in a single layer in the basket
— it's important they don't touch (work in 2 batches). Cook 9–11
minutes, flipping at the 5-minute mark, until all sides are
deeply golden.

### 06. Plate.
Pile onto a small serving dish or platter. Scatter torn basil
over the top.

### 07. Serve with marinara.
Warm the marinara briefly. Serve in a small bowl alongside for
dipping.

## Notes

For a spicier version, add ½ tsp of cayenne or red pepper flakes
to the breading. For gluten-free, swap panko for gluten-free
panko or crushed cornflakes (the cornflake version is actually
slightly crispier). For a ranch-style dipping sauce instead of
marinara, mix ½ cup mayo + 2 tbsp buttermilk + 1 tsp fresh dill
+ ½ tsp garlic powder + a pinch of salt. The fries don't reheat
well — they go soft. Eat within 15 minutes of cooking. For yellow
squash (summer squash), same recipe works exactly — they're
interchangeable.
