The pork chop has a bad reputation it doesn't deserve. The dry, grey,
shoe-leather chop of the 1990s — overcooked under the broiler because
trichinosis was still on people's minds — gave the cut a reputation
for being boring at best, inedible at worst. The good news: pork
guidelines changed in 2011. The new safe internal temperature is
145°F (63°C), which leaves a slight pink centre and a properly juicy
texture. The bad news: most people still cook them to 165°F out of
habit. Don't be most people.

The air fryer is the natural home of the modern pork chop. Eight
minutes at 400°F gets a ½-inch chop to 145°F internal with deep
browning on both sides. The dry circulating heat develops a real
crust on the surface — no oil splatter, no babysitting the pan. A
simple rub of smoked paprika, garlic powder, brown sugar, salt, and
pepper turns into a caramelised pebbled crust on the outside while
the inside stays moist.

The photo shows what proper pork chops look like: a stack of thin
chops on a white plate, the surfaces visibly seared and crusted with
the paprika rub, fanned slightly to show the slight rosy hue at the
centre. No fancy plating, no garnish — just pork chops, which is the
correct approach. Serve with mashed potatoes, applesauce, or a green
salad on the side.

## Ingredients

- 4 boneless pork chops, ½-inch thick (about 4 oz / 110g each)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp light brown sugar
- ¾ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ¼ tsp dried thyme (optional)
- ¼ tsp onion powder

**To serve:**
- Apple sauce, or mustard, or both

## Method

### 01. Dry the chops.
Pat each pork chop very dry with paper towels. Surface moisture
prevents the seasoning from sticking and the surface from
browning.

### 02. Mix the rub.
In a small bowl, combine the paprika, garlic powder, brown sugar,
salt, pepper, thyme (if using), and onion powder.

### 03. Oil and season.
Rub each chop with a thin coat of olive oil. Sprinkle the spice rub
on both sides, pressing it into the meat.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place the chops in the basket
in a single layer with space between them — they shouldn't touch.
Cook 8–10 minutes, flipping at the 5-minute mark, until the
internal temperature reaches 145°F (63°C) at the thickest part.

### 05. Rest.
Move to a plate and rest 5 minutes — this is non-negotiable. The
juices redistribute, and the carryover heat brings the temperature
up to 148°F. The chops should be juicy with a slight pink centre.

### 06. Serve.
Eat with apple sauce or mustard on the side, alongside mashed
potatoes, a side salad, or air-fryer roasted vegetables.

## Notes

For thicker chops (1-inch), increase the time to 12 minutes total,
flipping at 7 minutes. Bone-in chops work too — same temp, add 2
minutes. **Don't overcook**. 145°F is properly cooked, with a
slight pink centre. If you're squeamish, 150°F is fine; anything
past 155°F is too dry. For a brined version (juicier still), soak
the chops in 2 cups of water with 2 tablespoons salt and 1
tablespoon sugar for 30 minutes; rinse and pat dry before
seasoning.
