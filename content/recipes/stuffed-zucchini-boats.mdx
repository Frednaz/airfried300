The stuffed zucchini boat sits in a class of recipes that solve a
specific problem: what to do with the giant zucchini that
appeared in your CSA box, or that your neighbour left on your
porch in August, or that grew to football-size in your own garden
because you went on vacation for a week. The hollow-out-and-stuff
move turns the zucchini into a vehicle for the actual dinner —
ground meat, spices, cheese — while still letting you claim
you cooked a vegetable.

The filling here is Mediterranean-leaning: ground beef (or lamb,
which is even better) browned with onion, garlic, oregano,
cinnamon (yes, cinnamon — the secret to the right "Greek meat"
flavour), tomato paste, and parsley. The mixture goes into the
hollowed zucchini halves, gets topped with crumbled feta, and
finishes in the air fryer where the cheese melts and the zucchini
softens to just-tender.

The photo shows four zucchini boats lined up in a square white
baking dish, each filled with dark-brown spiced meat with visible
white feta crumbles and torn basil leaves on top. The zucchini
sides have charred slightly at the edges. This is the kind of
dinner that looks like more effort than it took — about 35
minutes start to finish, mostly hands-off.

A note on the zucchini: medium-large is the right size (8–10
inches long, the thickness of a small wrist). Tiny zucchini don't
hold enough filling; giant baseball-bat zucchini are watery and
seedy. The boats are easiest to scoop with a melon baller or a
small spoon.

## Ingredients

**For the boats:**
- 4 medium zucchini (about 8 inches long)
- 1 tbsp olive oil (for brushing)
- ½ tsp kosher salt

**For the filling:**
- 1 lb (450g) ground beef or lamb
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 small yellow onion, finely diced
- 3 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 (14.5 oz) can diced tomatoes, drained
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- ¼ tsp ground cinnamon
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ¼ tsp red pepper flakes
- ⅓ cup fresh parsley, chopped

**For topping:**
- ½ cup crumbled feta cheese
- ¼ cup fresh basil leaves, torn
- Drizzle of olive oil

## Method

### 01. Halve and hollow the zucchini.
Slice each zucchini lengthwise in half. Use a small spoon (or
melon baller) to scoop out a shallow trench down the middle of
each half — keep about ¼-inch of flesh at the bottom and sides.
Finely chop the scooped-out flesh; reserve for the filling.

### 02. Brush and salt.
Brush the cut sides of the zucchini halves with olive oil and
sprinkle with salt.

### 03. Brown the meat.
Heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a skillet over medium-high. Add the
ground meat and cook, breaking it up, until browned, about 6
minutes. Drain off excess fat.

### 04. Aromatics.
Add the chopped onion to the meat. Cook 4 minutes until softened.
Add the garlic and cook 30 seconds.

### 05. Build the filling.
Stir in the reserved chopped zucchini flesh, tomato paste, drained
tomatoes, oregano, cinnamon, salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes.
Simmer 4 minutes to thicken. Off heat, stir in the parsley.

### 06. Stuff the boats.
Spoon the filling into each zucchini half, mounding slightly. Top
each with crumbled feta.

### 07. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 380°F (193°C). Arrange the boats in the
basket in a single layer (work in 2 batches if needed — your
basket likely fits 2–4 at a time). Cook 14–16 minutes, until the
zucchini is tender (pierces easily with a knife) and the feta
has melted slightly and turned golden at the tips.

### 08. Finish and serve.
Scatter torn basil over the top and drizzle with a little olive
oil. Serve immediately.

## Notes

For a vegetarian version, swap the meat for 2 cups of cooked
brown lentils — same seasoning. For a richer version, add ¼ cup
of grated kefalotyri or pecorino along with the feta. The boats
reheat well in the air fryer for 4 minutes at 380°F — they
actually firm up nicely after a night in the fridge. Serve over
rice, with pita and tzatziki on the side, or as-is for a low-carb
dinner.
