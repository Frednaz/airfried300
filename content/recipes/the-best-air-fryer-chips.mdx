The triple-cooked chip is the chip-shop chip elevated to a tiny
edible architectural achievement. Heston Blumenthal popularised
the technique at The Fat Duck two decades ago, and despite its
silly-sounding name, the underlying logic is sound. Each stage
does specific work that the others can't. The result is the
crispest chip you can produce at home — properly crisp, not just
"not soggy."

**Why three stages.** The parboil cooks the inside without
colouring the outside; the first dry-chill develops a starchy
surface skin; the first low-temperature air-fry sets that skin
into a hard shell without browning yet; the second dry-chill
deepens the dehydration; the final high-temperature air-fry
caramelises the outside.

It takes about an hour total. Most of that is unattended chilling.
Active hands-on time is 15 minutes across the stages. The chips
themselves are the same Maris Piper / Yukon Gold approach as the
standard British chip, just executed with more steps. Worth it
for a weekend dinner or a Friday-night-in fish-and-chips meal.

## Ingredients

- 2 lb (900g) Maris Piper potatoes (or Yukon Gold / floury
  potatoes), peeled
- 1 tsp kosher salt (for the boil)
- 3 tbsp sunflower or rapeseed oil
- Flaky sea salt, to finish

**To serve:**
- Mayonnaise
- Ketchup
- Malt vinegar
- (Or all three — let people decide)

## Method

### 01. Cut.
Cut the peeled potatoes into chips, about ½-inch thick on a
side, 3 inches long. Aim for uniform — irregularity is the
enemy.

### 02. Parboil.
Place chips in a large pot of cold salted water. Bring to a boil
and cook 8–10 minutes, until completely tender (a knife slides
straight through) but the chips still hold their shape — barely.
They should look slightly cracked at the edges.

### 03. Drain and first dry-chill.
Drain very gently and lay on a tray or wire rack. Cool to room
temperature, then refrigerate UNCOVERED for at least 30 minutes
(up to overnight is even better). This step is non-negotiable.
The chips need to dry out and develop a thin starch skin.

### 04. First air-fry — low temperature.
Heat the air fryer to 300°F (150°C). Toss the chilled chips
with 1½ tbsp of the oil. Arrange in a single layer in the
basket — work in batches if needed. Cook 12 minutes, shaking
once. The chips should be pale gold and fully cooked but not
browned yet.

### 05. Second dry-chill.
Spread the chips back on the tray. Cool to room temperature.
Refrigerate UNCOVERED again for at least 30 minutes (or up to
6 hours).

### 06. Final air-fry — high temperature.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Toss the chilled chips
with the remaining 1½ tbsp oil. Arrange in a single layer in
the basket. Cook 8–10 minutes, shaking every 3 minutes, until
deeply golden, with proper craggy edges and visible cracking
of the surface.

### 07. Salt and serve.
Tip onto a plate, sprinkle generously with flaky sea salt.
Serve immediately. Eat with both hands.

## Notes

This is the standard against which all other chips are judged.
The chilling steps look long but are completely passive —
chip-prep is done while you do other things. Triple-cooked
chips don't reheat well (the shell goes soft); eat the same
day. For a half-recipe (1 lb potatoes for 2 people), all timings
stay the same — just smaller batches. The blanched chips
(after step 5) can be frozen — bag and freeze; cook from frozen
at 400°F for 12 minutes. Best paired with fish-and-fries, a
proper steak, or just a giant bowl of mayonnaise.
