The British roast potato — "roastie" — is the dish you don't realise
is the centrepiece of Sunday dinner until you don't get any. The
chicken, the gravy, the carrots: all replaceable. The roastie is
non-negotiable. The British way is specific: par-boil potatoes until
the outsides go fluffy, then bash them around in the colander to
rough up the exterior (more surface area = more crisp), dump them
into screaming-hot fat, and roast until they look like geological
formations.

The traditional version uses goose fat or beef dripping in a roasting
tray at 220°C for 50 minutes, turned once. The air-fryer version is
faster, uses less fat, and — controversially — produces a crispier
result. The hot circulating air hits every angle simultaneously,
whereas an oven roasting tray has to be turned by hand.

The two non-negotiable steps: the par-boil and the roughing up. Skip
either and you get smooth, slightly waxy potatoes that don't go crisp.
With both: glass-shattering crust, fluffy interior, the kind of roast
potato that disappears off the plate before anyone notices they got
seconds.

The photo shows them in the natural habitat: a black ceramic bowl
piled with golden roasties, edges visibly craggy and crisp, with a
small jug of gravy alongside on a dark blue lace tablecloth. There's
a piece of parsnip just visible at the edge — the supporting cast.
This is Sunday dinner, executed.

## Ingredients

- 2½ lb (1.1 kg) Maris Piper or Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled
- 1 tsp salt (for the boiling water)
- 3 tbsp goose fat (or duck fat, or beef dripping, or sunflower oil)
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp semolina (optional but gives extra crunch)
- 2 sprigs fresh rosemary (optional)
- 4 cloves garlic, unpeeled (optional)

## Method

### 01. Cut to even sizes.
Cut the peeled potatoes into roughly even chunks — about 2 inches
across. Smaller pieces crisp more; larger pieces stay fluffier in
the middle. Aim somewhere in between.

### 02. Par-boil.
Place the potatoes in a large pot of cold water with the salt. Bring
to a boil and simmer for 10 minutes, until the outsides are starting
to go fluffy but the centres still hold their shape (a knife should
go in with slight resistance).

### 03. Drain and rough up.
Drain the potatoes in a colander. Let the steam off for 2 minutes.
Then — this is the critical step — toss the colander gently a few
times, scraping the potatoes against the sides, to rough up the
fluffy outsides. They should look slightly rugged, almost broken at
the edges. (Don't smash them — just rough.)

### 04. Heat the fat.
Place the goose fat (or oil) in the air-fryer basket. Heat the air
fryer to 400°F (200°C) for 3 minutes — the fat should be very hot
and smoking slightly.

### 05. Add the potatoes.
Carefully (the fat will spit) add the par-boiled potatoes to the hot
fat. Sprinkle with the semolina (if using) and toss to coat. Add
the rosemary sprigs and garlic cloves around them.

### 06. Roast.
Cook at 400°F (200°C) for 24–28 minutes, shaking the basket every 8
minutes, until the potatoes are deeply golden and crisp on every
side. The edges should look craggy and almost burnt — that's the
glass crust.

### 07. Season and serve.
Tip onto a plate or shallow bowl, sprinkle with kosher salt. Eat
immediately — they lose their crackle within 10 minutes.

## Notes

**Why semolina helps**: the gritty texture catches the hot fat and
adds an extra layer of crunch. It's optional — they're still good
without it. Maris Piper is the textbook British roasting potato;
Yukon Gold is the closest American equivalent. Avoid red-skinned or
waxy varieties — they don't go fluffy in the par-boil. For a non-
animal-fat version, sunflower oil works at the same temperature.
Don't skip the par-boil; the inside-fluffy/outside-crisp duality
depends on it. Pair with the Sunday roast chicken or pork joint
recipes — they cook in different baskets if you have two air fryers.
