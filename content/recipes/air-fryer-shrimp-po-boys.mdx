The po'boy is a New Orleans institution: a French-style baguette
loaded with fried shrimp, oysters, or roast beef, dressed with mayo
and pickles, eaten while standing in the corner shop where it was
just made. The "fried" part is what we're approximating here. The
traditional version is deep-fried — the shrimp go in hot oil, come
out gold and crisp, get layered into the bread immediately. The air
fryer at 400°F manages the same crunch with a tablespoon of oil
instead of a quart, and you can pull off the entire dish in 35
minutes flat.

The remoulade is the move that pushes this above an average breaded-
shrimp sandwich. It's mayo-based but pivoted toward Louisiana: lemon
juice, capers, parsley, a touch of Creole mustard, and — the twist —
grated celeriac (celery root), which adds crunch and a subtle
mineral sweetness. If you can't find celeriac, the recipe works with
celery, or even with cabbage. The remoulade is what makes the
sandwich.

## Ingredients

**For the shrimp:**
- 1 lb (450g) large raw shrimp, peeled, deveined, tail-off
- ½ cup (60g) all-purpose flour
- 2 tsp Cajun seasoning
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 1¼ cups (100g) panko breadcrumbs
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- Olive oil spray

**For the celeriac remoulade:**
- ⅓ small celeriac (about 5 oz / 140g), peeled and finely grated
- ½ cup mayonnaise
- 2 tbsp Greek yogurt
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 2 tsp capers, finely chopped
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
- 1 tsp creole mustard (or extra Dijon)
- ¼ tsp kosher salt

**To assemble (per sandwich):**
- 1 sub roll, split
- ½ cup shredded iceberg or romaine lettuce
- 2 tbsp gherkins / cornichons, sliced
- Hot sauce, to taste

## Method

### 01. Make the remoulade.
In a medium bowl, mix the grated celeriac with the mayo, yogurt,
Dijon, lemon juice, capers, parsley, creole mustard, and salt. Cover
and refrigerate while you make the shrimp — it gets better as it sits.

### 02. Set up the dredging stations.
Three bowls: flour mixed with Cajun seasoning and ½ tsp salt; beaten
eggs; panko mixed with paprika and garlic powder.

### 03. Dredge.
Pat the shrimp very dry. Working one at a time: dust in flour, dip
in egg, press firmly into panko to coat. Place on a plate as you go.

### 04. Spray with oil.
Spray the breaded shrimp all over with olive oil — this is what
makes them golden rather than pale.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange the shrimp in a single
layer in the basket. Cook 6–8 minutes, flipping at the 4-minute
mark, until deeply golden and the shrimp are just opaque (don't
overcook — shrimp go from snappy to rubbery fast).

### 06. Assemble.
Split each sub roll. Spread a generous layer of remoulade on both
cut sides. Pile in shredded lettuce, then layer 5–6 shrimp on top,
scatter gherkin slices. Add hot sauce if you want it spicy. Close
and press gently.

### 07. Serve.
Cut on the bias. Eat immediately — the shrimp lose their crackle
within ten minutes of cooking.

## Notes

The remoulade keeps 3 days refrigerated and improves overnight. For
a quick version, swap the celeriac for finely shredded cabbage. For
gluten-free, swap the panko for crushed cornflakes or gluten-free
panko. Don't be tempted to add extra batter to the shrimp — they're
small and overcook fast. The Cajun seasoning is doing real work here;
use a good brand (Tony Chachere's is the classic) or make your own
from paprika, garlic powder, onion powder, oregano, cayenne, salt,
and pepper.
