Salmon is the dish that taught a generation of home cooks they could,
in fact, cook fish. It's forgiving — fattier than white fish, harder to
overcook, doesn't fall apart when you flip it, doesn't need oil because
it brings its own. And lemon-pepper seasoning is the laziest possible
spice rub: pre-mixed, salt-balanced, citrus-forward, you don't need a
second thing.

The photo shows two fillets on a wood cutting board, heavy with seasoning
— the pebbled yellow-and-black crust is what you're going for. Lemon
zest and cracked black pepper are the two ingredients; everything else
(salt, herbs) is supporting. Don't use the lemon pepper from a five-year-
old jar. The lemon oil goes flat. Buy a fresh container or, better,
make it: zest two lemons over a tablespoon of cracked pepper plus a
teaspoon of kosher salt, let it dry on the counter for an hour, store
in a jar. Better than any store-bought one.

The salmon cooks at 400°F for about eight minutes for a 1-inch-thick
fillet. The center finishes at 125°F for medium (still translucent),
135°F for medium-well (just barely flake), 145°F for well (fully opaque,
slightly dry). I pull at 125°F and let the carryover heat finish it.

## Ingredients

- 2 salmon fillets, 6 oz each, skin on or off
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tsp lemon-pepper seasoning (or 1½ tsp cracked black pepper +
  1 tsp lemon zest + ½ tsp kosher salt)
- ½ tsp kosher salt (skip if your seasoning is salt-heavy)
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges (to serve)
- Fresh parsley or dill, chopped, to finish (optional)

## Method

### 01. Dry the salmon.
Pat the fillets very dry with paper towels — surface moisture prevents
the seasoning from sticking and the skin from crisping.

### 02. Oil and season.
Rub each fillet all over with olive oil, then press the lemon-pepper
seasoning into the flesh side (and skin side if skin-on). Sprinkle on
the salt if using.

### 03. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place the fillets flesh-side-up in
the basket (or skin-down if skin-on) with at least an inch of space
between them. Cook 7–9 minutes for medium, until the thickest part
reads 125–130°F (52–54°C). The flesh should flake slightly when nudged
with a fork but still look slightly translucent at the center.

### 04. Rest briefly.
Move to a board or plate and rest 2 minutes — the residual heat finishes
the cooking. Scatter parsley over the top if using and serve with the
lemon wedges.

## Notes

For meal prep: cook two fillets, eat one tonight, flake the second over
a green salad for tomorrow's lunch. The salmon keeps 3 days in the
fridge. If your fillets are very thick (over 1½ inches), increase the
time to 10–11 minutes. For a sweeter glaze, brush with 1 tablespoon of
honey + 1 teaspoon of soy sauce in the last 2 minutes of cooking. For
crispier skin, place skin-side-down on a piece of parchment in the
basket — the skin shatters when it comes out.
