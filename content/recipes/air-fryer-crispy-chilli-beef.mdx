Crispy chilli beef is the dish that defines British-Chinese takeaway
in the same way General Tso's defines American-Chinese: deeply
inauthentic, vaguely Sichuan-inspired, infinitely better than its
purist critics will admit. Deep-fried beef strips, glossy chilli-soy
sauce that's somehow both sweet and aggressive, scattered with red
peppers and scallions, served over a mound of rice. The dish you order
when you're tired and you want crunch and heat in the same forkful.

The home version usually fails on the crisp. Pan-fried, the beef
steams; oven-baked, it stays pale. The air fryer at 200°C (400°F)
finally cracks it — the cornflour coating dries and crisps in eight
minutes, no kitchen-flooding deep-fry session. Then you toss the
crisp beef in a quick stir-fried sauce on the stovetop and serve
immediately. The crispiness lasts about ten minutes — eat it while
it's hot.

## Ingredients

**For the beef:**
- 14 oz (400g) sirloin steak, sliced very thinly against the grain
- 3 tbsp cornflour (cornstarch)
- 1 tsp Chinese five-spice
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp white pepper
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp olive oil

**For the sauce:**
- 3 tbsp light soy sauce
- 2 tbsp rice vinegar
- 2 tbsp ketchup (yes — it's the takeaway secret)
- 1 tbsp dark soy sauce
- 1 tbsp Chinese rice wine (or dry sherry)
- 2 tbsp light brown sugar
- 1 tsp sesame oil
- 1 tsp cornflour mixed with 2 tbsp water (slurry)

**For the stir-fry:**
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 red bell pepper, cut into strips
- 3 cloves garlic, finely sliced
- 1-inch piece ginger, peeled and finely sliced
- 1–2 fresh red chillies, finely sliced (deseed for less heat)
- 4 spring onions, white parts sliced, greens cut into 2-inch pieces

## Method

### 01. Slice and coat the beef.
Slice the steak as thinly as possible against the grain (freezing for
20 minutes makes this easier). Place in a bowl with the soy sauce,
toss to coat, then add the cornflour, five-spice, salt, and pepper.
Drizzle in the oil and toss until each strip is coated.

### 02. Air fry the beef.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Spread the beef in a single
layer (work in two batches if needed) — crowding leads to steam.
Cook 7–9 minutes, shaking the basket twice, until the beef is deep
brown and crisp at the edges.

### 03. Make the sauce.
While the beef cooks, whisk together the soy, vinegar, ketchup, dark
soy, rice wine, brown sugar, and sesame oil. Keep the cornflour
slurry separate for now.

### 04. Stir-fry.
Heat the neutral oil in a wok or large skillet over high heat. Add
the red pepper and stir-fry 2 minutes. Add the garlic, ginger, chilli,
and white parts of the spring onions; stir-fry 30 seconds until
fragrant.

### 05. Sauce and toss.
Pour in the sauce mixture. Bring to a simmer, then stir in the
cornflour slurry. The sauce will thicken in 30 seconds. Add the
crispy beef and the spring onion greens. Toss for 30 seconds — just
long enough to coat — and immediately remove from heat (longer and
the beef goes soft).

### 06. Serve.
Plate over steamed jasmine rice. Eat within 10 minutes for maximum
crispness.

## Notes

**The beef must be very thin** — ⅛-inch slices, max. Freeze the steak
for 20 minutes before slicing if it's slipping under the knife. Swap
the sirloin for flank steak (cheaper, just as good if sliced thin).
For chicken version, use strips of chicken thigh — same coating,
same time. For a sweeter sauce, add an extra tablespoon of brown
sugar. For a hotter version, add 1 tsp chilli oil to the sauce.
