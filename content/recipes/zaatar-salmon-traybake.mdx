Za'atar is the Levantine spice blend that you should already have
in your cupboard — dried thyme, sumac (the lemony-tart red spice),
toasted sesame seeds, oregano, sometimes savory or marjoram. It's
the smell of every street-corner bakery in Beirut, Jerusalem, and
Damascus, where it's brushed on flatbreads with olive oil and
baked. On salmon, it does similar work: the dried thyme infuses,
the sumac brightens, the sesame seeds toast and add nutty crunch.

The traybake structure is the British weeknight invention — protein
and vegetables cooked together on one tray. Adapted to the air
fryer, it becomes faster (no preheat, no big oven) but the same
principle: everything goes into the basket in stages, comes out
together. Potato wedges first (they need more time), then salmon
laid on top, then cherry tomatoes scattered to blister at the end.

The herb-yogurt drizzle is the cooling counterpoint — Greek
yogurt, fresh mint and parsley, a clove of garlic, lemon juice,
olive oil. Stirred together, drizzled in zigzags across the
finished plate. The combination of warm-spice salmon, crisp
potatoes, jammy tomatoes, and cool yogurt is the whole reason
this dish works.

## Ingredients

**For the salmon:**
- 4 salmon fillets (5 oz / 140g each), skin on or off
- 2 tbsp za'atar
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp lemon zest
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper

**For the potato wedges:**
- 1½ lb (700g) baby potatoes, halved or quartered
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp za'atar
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp ground cumin

**For the tomatoes:**
- 12 oz (340g) cherry tomatoes on the vine (2 small clusters)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- ¼ tsp kosher salt

**For the herb-yogurt drizzle:**
- ½ cup (120g) Greek yogurt
- 2 tbsp fresh mint, finely chopped
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, finely chopped
- 1 small clove garlic, grated
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tbsp warm water (to loosen)
- ½ tsp kosher salt

**To serve:**
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges
- Fresh basil or dill sprigs

## Method

### 01. Toss the potatoes.
In a bowl, toss the halved baby potatoes with olive oil,
za'atar, salt, paprika, and cumin.

### 02. Start the potatoes.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Tip the potatoes into the
basket in a roughly even layer. Cook 14 minutes, shaking at
the 8-minute mark.

### 03. Season the salmon.
While the potatoes cook, pat the salmon fillets very dry. Rub
each with olive oil, lemon zest, salt, and pepper. Press the
za'atar generously into the flesh side.

### 04. Add the salmon and tomatoes.
After the potatoes have cooked for 14 minutes, push them to
one side. Place the salmon fillets flesh-side-up in the
basket alongside the potatoes. Drizzle the cherry tomato
vines with olive oil, sprinkle with salt, and lay across the
salmon (or beside it).

### 05. Cook together.
Continue cooking at 400°F for 8–10 minutes more. The salmon
should reach 130°F (54°C) for medium (still translucent), the
potatoes should be deeply golden, and the cherry tomatoes
should be blistered and just bursting.

### 06. Make the herb-yogurt drizzle.
While the bake finishes, whisk yogurt with mint, parsley,
garlic, lemon juice, olive oil, water, and salt until smooth
and drizzleable.

### 07. Plate.
Transfer everything to a large serving platter. Drizzle the
herb-yogurt sauce in zigzags across the top — let it pool
slightly around the salmon. Scatter basil sprigs. Tuck lemon
wedges around the edge.

### 08. Serve.
Bring to the table, family-style. Eat with knives, forks, and
plenty of yogurt sauce.

## Notes

For a meatier version, swap the salmon for boneless chicken
thighs (cook 4 minutes longer than salmon, until 165°F
internal). For a vegan version, swap the salmon for thick
slabs of marinated tofu or large portobello mushrooms (same
timing as salmon). The herb-yogurt drizzle is excellent on
its own — make a double batch and keep in the fridge for use
on grain bowls, with grilled vegetables, or as a sandwich
spread. Without za'atar, mix 1 tbsp dried thyme + 1 tsp sumac
+ 1 tsp toasted sesame seeds + ½ tsp dried oregano + ¼ tsp
salt. The sumac is the key ingredient — don't omit it.
