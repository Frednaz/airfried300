The British pork joint with crackling is one of the great pleasures
of Sunday cooking, and the crackling itself is the most divisive part
— either you crack it beautifully, in which case it's the best thing
on the plate, or it stays leathery and rubbery, in which case the
whole roast feels slightly disappointing.

The air fryer fixes the crackling problem. The conventional oven
struggles because the heat is too diffuse — you can blast the joint
at 220°C and still end up with patchy crackling. The air fryer's
focused, circulating heat at 400°F (200°C) for the first ten minutes
puffs and crisps the skin reliably. Then you drop the temperature
to 320°F (160°C) for the rest of the cook, so the meat finishes
gently without drying out.

The single most important step happens before the joint goes in: the
skin must be deeply scored (¼-inch deep, ½-inch apart, in a tight
crosshatch) and very dry. Pat it for ten minutes with paper towels,
then leave it uncovered in the fridge for an hour to dry further if
you can. The salt does the rest. The photo shows what you're aiming
for: golden-brown crackling with visible salt crystals embedded,
deep scoring lines defining the squares, the meat sliced beneath
showing pale-grey-pink interior.

## Ingredients

- 1 pork loin joint, 2 to 2½ lb (900g–1.1 kg), skin on
- 1 tbsp kosher salt (for the skin)
- 1 tsp fennel seeds, crushed
- 1 tsp coarse black pepper
- 2 tsp olive oil
- 1 tsp dried sage (or 1 tbsp fresh, chopped)
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- 2 cloves garlic, finely grated

## Method

### 01. Score and dry (ideally 1 hour ahead).
Pat the pork skin very dry with paper towels. With a very sharp
knife (or a clean Stanley blade), score the skin in a crosshatch
pattern, ¼-inch deep and ½-inch apart, cutting through the fat
but not into the meat. Place uncovered in the fridge for 1 hour
(or up to overnight) to dry the surface.

### 02. Bring to room temperature.
30 minutes before cooking, take the pork out. Pat the skin dry
again — surface moisture is the enemy of crackling.

### 03. Salt and season.
Sprinkle the kosher salt evenly over the scored skin, pressing it
into the cuts. Mix the fennel seeds, pepper, sage, thyme, and
garlic with the olive oil to form a paste. Rub the paste onto the
flesh sides of the joint (not the skin — the skin only gets salt).

### 04. Blister the crackling (high heat).
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place the joint skin-side-up
in the basket. Cook for 12 minutes — the skin should start to
bubble and crisp.

### 05. Drop the heat (gentle cook).
Reduce the air fryer to 320°F (160°C). Continue cooking for 55–65
minutes more, until the internal temperature reaches 145°F (63°C)
at the thickest part for medium, or 150°F (66°C) for slightly more
well-done. The crackling should be golden and crisp; if it's not
crackling by the end, blast at 400°F for the last 3 minutes.

### 06. Rest.
Move to a board and rest for 10 minutes — this is non-negotiable
for both the meat and the crackling (the skin actually firms up
during the rest).

### 07. Carve.
Cut the crackling off in a single piece if possible, then snap into
pieces (a heavy knife or kitchen shears helps). Slice the meat
across the grain into ¼-inch slices. Serve the crackling on the
side or arranged around the meat.

## Notes

**Why the crackling sometimes fails**: insufficiently dry skin, or
salt added with extra oil rubbed in (the oil stops the crackling —
salt only on the skin). For a stronger flavor on the meat, brine
overnight in a salt-and-sugar brine (1 tbsp each per cup of water,
enough to cover, 12 hours). Pair with the air-fryer roast potatoes
recipe and the same hot sauce in the basket — they cook in 32
minutes while the pork rests. Apple sauce on the side is
non-negotiable.
