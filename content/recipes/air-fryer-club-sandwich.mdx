The club sandwich is one of those dishes that's somehow more than
its parts. The recipe is just bread, chicken, bacon, lettuce,
tomato — ingredients you can find in any kitchen — but the
particular tradition of building it (three slices of toast, two
fillings stacked between, cut into four triangles with a toothpick
through each) elevates it into a specific category of food. The
midcentury hotel lobby sandwich. The 2am diner. The order you
make when you can't decide what else to have.

The air fryer is genuinely useful here for two of the three
critical components. The bacon: 8 minutes at 400°F, no splatter,
no pan to wash. The bread: 2 minutes at 380°F per side gives
proper diner-toast crisp, deeper colour than a toaster manages.
The chicken: use leftover roast chicken if you have it; if not,
season a chicken breast and air-fry it for 12 minutes at 400°F,
then slice. The total operation is twenty-five minutes start to
finish.

The photo shows the textbook stack — tall, dramatic, the layers
visible in cross-section. Bread, chicken, lettuce, tomato, bread,
bacon, cheddar, lettuce, bread. A toothpick spearing the middle.
Cut diagonally. Pickles on the side. This is what a club sandwich
is supposed to look like, made at a kitchen counter at noon on a
Sunday.

## Ingredients

**For the sandwich (per serving):**
- 3 slices good white sandwich bread
- 4 slices streaky bacon
- 4 oz (110g) cooked chicken breast, thinly sliced (or leftover
  roast chicken, shredded)
- 2 slices sharp cheddar
- 4 large leaves butterhead or romaine lettuce
- 1 medium tomato, sliced ¼-inch thick
- 2 tbsp mayonnaise
- ½ tsp Dijon mustard (optional)
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- Pinch of black pepper

**For the optional fresh chicken:**
- 6 oz (170g) chicken breast, pounded ½-inch thick
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp paprika
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp olive oil

**To serve:**
- Cocktail sticks or long toothpicks (4 per sandwich)
- Dill pickle spears
- Crisps (chips, in the American sense)

## Method

### 01. Cook the chicken (if not using leftover).
Rub the pounded chicken with oil, salt, paprika, and pepper.
Air-fry at 400°F (200°C) for 10–12 minutes until the internal
temperature reads 165°F (74°C). Rest 5 minutes, then slice
thinly.

### 02. Cook the bacon.
Arrange bacon strips in a single layer in the air fryer basket.
Cook at 400°F (200°C) for 7–9 minutes until deeply crisp.
Drain on paper towels.

### 03. Toast the bread.
Working in 2 batches if needed, toast the bread slices in the
air fryer at 380°F (193°C) for 2 minutes per side — until golden
and crisp at the edges.

### 04. Mix the mayo.
Stir the mayonnaise with Dijon, salt, and pepper.

### 05. Build the first layer.
Spread mayo on one slice of toast. Layer: sliced chicken, two
lettuce leaves, two tomato slices. Add a second slice of toast,
mayo-side-down.

### 06. Build the second layer.
Spread mayo on the top of the second slice. Layer: crispy bacon,
cheese slice, two lettuce leaves. Top with the third slice of
toast, mayo-side-down.

### 07. Skewer and slice.
Press gently. Insert four cocktail sticks through the sandwich,
forming a square pattern. Cut diagonally twice (corner to corner),
making four triangles, each held together by a stick.

### 08. Plate.
Serve with a pickle spear and a small pile of crisps. Eat
immediately, with both hands.

## Notes

Sourdough or rye instead of white bread is a perfectly acceptable
modernisation — the structural requirement is "sturdy enough to
hold the stack." For a turkey club, swap the chicken for sliced
roast turkey (Thanksgiving leftovers excellent). For a steakhouse
version, swap the cheddar for blue cheese, add a swipe of
horseradish in the mayo. The toothpicks are non-negotiable — the
sandwich collapses without them, and the toothpick-in-corner is
visually part of the club identity.
