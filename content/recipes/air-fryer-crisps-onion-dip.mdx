The crisp-and-dip is the snack format that defines pub culture in
Britain and bar culture everywhere else. Crisps in a basket, a small
ramekin of dip, a pint of beer alongside. The traditional version uses
shop-bought crisps and shop-bought dip — perfectly fine, requires no
effort. The "home version" of this is making crisps from scratch in
the air fryer (which works surprisingly well) and elevating the dip
slightly with caramelised onion chutney.

The crisps are the project. You slice potatoes paper-thin — a mandoline
is non-optional here, unless you have a knife steady enough for 1/16-
inch slices. Then you soak the slices in cold water for ten minutes to
pull starch, dry them aggressively with kitchen towels (water = soggy
crisps), toss with a thin layer of oil and salt, and air fry in batches.
They come out crisp like real crisps — slightly thicker than the
factory ones, slightly more variable in colour, all the better for
it.

The dip is the easy part: soured cream + a few tablespoons of jarred
caramelised onion chutney + chives + cracked pepper. Mix, taste, adjust.
Top with the crispy onions and you have something that looks deeply
considered and took fifteen seconds. The photo shows the whole
operation: skillet of dip in the centre, small wire basket of crisps in
the corner, pints of beer flanking. A small candle for atmosphere. This
is what we're going for.

## Ingredients

**For the crisps:**
- 2 large russet potatoes (about 1 lb / 450g), scrubbed
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- ¼ tsp black pepper

**For the dip:**
- 1 cup (240ml) full-fat soured cream
- ¼ cup (60g) caramelised onion chutney (or red onion marmalade)
- 2 tbsp finely chopped fresh chives, plus extra to garnish
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper, freshly cracked
- 2 tbsp crispy fried onions (the shop-bought kind, e.g. French's), to top

## Method

### 01. Slice the potatoes.
Using a mandoline, slice the potatoes into rounds about 1/16-inch
(1.5mm) thick. You want them paper-thin — if you can almost see
through them, you're there.

### 02. Soak and dry.
Place the slices in a bowl of cold water for 10 minutes — pulls
out surface starch. Drain, rinse once more, then dry very
aggressively with clean kitchen towels. Two rounds of patting; any
remaining moisture will keep them from crisping.

### 03. Toss.
In a large bowl, toss the dry slices with the olive oil, salt,
paprika, and pepper. Use your hands to make sure every slice is
lightly coated.

### 04. Air fry in batches.
Heat the air fryer to 360°F (180°C). Arrange the slices in a single
layer in the basket — don't overlap, they need air circulation. Cook
12–14 minutes, shaking the basket every 4 minutes and rearranging any
that have stuck together. The crisps go from pale to deep golden very
fast in the last 2 minutes — watch them. Tip onto a wire rack to
cool (they crisp further as they sit). Repeat with remaining slices.

### 05. Make the dip.
While the second batch cooks, mix the soured cream, caramelised onion
chutney, chopped chives, salt, and pepper in a serving bowl. Top with
the crispy fried onions and an extra scatter of chives.

### 06. Serve.
Pile the crisps in a bowl or wire basket next to the dip. Eat
immediately — both the crisps and the dip are at their best within
30 minutes.

## Notes

**The mandoline matters**: hand-cut crisps end up varying in thickness,
which means some burn and some stay soft. If you don't have one, use
the slicer attachment of a food processor — close enough. For a sour-
cream-and-onion vibe (more classic chip-shop dip), swap the
caramelised onion chutney for 2 tbsp grated onion + ½ tsp onion powder
+ a squeeze of lemon. Crisps keep 2 days in an airtight container at
room temperature — re-crisp in the air fryer for 90 seconds if they
soften. The dip keeps 3 days refrigerated.
