The yeasted ring doughnut is the foundational form. Soft, slightly
chewy, holes in the middle, glazed with something sweet. Bakery
doughnuts are typically deep-fried; air-fried doughnuts skip the
oil bath and produce something that's slightly less greasy,
slightly more bread-like, but arguably better — the air-frying
gives them a more delicate crumb and a more uniform surface
colour for the glaze.

The yeasted dough is the slightly slower part of this recipe (one
proof, about 30 minutes total). It's enriched with butter, an egg,
and milk — soft and forgiving to handle. Roll out, cut with a
3-inch ring cutter (or a glass and a smaller bottle cap for the
hole), proof briefly, air-fry.

The raspberry glaze is the visual hook. Real raspberries pushed
through a sieve, mixed with icing sugar, becomes a glaze the
colour of a 1950s diner counter. The natural pectin in the
raspberries means it sets to a slightly tacky finish — perfect
for catching sprinkles. Rainbow non-pareils are the only correct
sprinkle topping. (No, you can't use chocolate sprinkles. This is
a raspberry doughnut. Don't.)

The photo shows them cooling on a wire rack: vivid pink-glazed
rings with rainbow sprinkles in irregular constellations, a few
holes (the cutouts, also air-fried) tumbled across, sprinkles
scattered across the dark background. Saturday morning, but make
it photographic.

## Ingredients

**For the dough:**
- 2 cups (250g) plain flour
- 2¼ tsp (1 sachet) instant yeast
- 3 tbsp (45g) caster sugar
- ½ tsp fine salt
- ½ cup (120ml) whole milk, warmed to 100°F (37°C)
- 3 tbsp (45g) unsalted butter, melted
- 1 large egg, room temperature
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- Olive oil spray

**For the raspberry glaze:**
- 1 cup (130g) fresh raspberries (or thawed frozen)
- 1¾ cups (220g) icing sugar (powdered sugar)
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- Pinch of salt

**To finish:**
- ¼ cup (50g) rainbow non-pareils (sprinkles)

## Method

### 01. Mix the dough.
In a stand mixer with a dough hook (or by hand), combine flour,
yeast, sugar, and salt. Add warmed milk, melted butter, egg,
and vanilla. Knead 5 minutes until smooth and slightly tacky.

### 02. Proof.
Place the dough in an oiled bowl, cover, leave in a warm spot
30 minutes — it should rise to about 1½ times its size (not
double; we don't want too much puff).

### 03. Roll and cut.
Turn the dough out onto a lightly floured surface. Roll to
about ½-inch thick. Cut rings with a 3-inch round cutter, then
use a smaller (1-inch) cutter for the centre hole. Re-roll
scraps. Place on parchment.

### 04. Brief second proof.
Cover the cut doughnuts loosely with a tea towel, leave 15
minutes to puff slightly.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 350°F (175°C). Spray the doughnuts with
olive oil. Cook in batches in a single layer for 6 minutes,
flipping at the 3-minute mark, until lightly golden.

### 06. Make the glaze.
Push the raspberries through a fine sieve to extract the juice
and pulp (discard the seeds). Whisk the raspberry puree with
icing sugar, lemon juice, and salt until smooth and pourable.

### 07. Dip and sprinkle.
Dip each cooled doughnut top-down into the glaze, lift,
allow excess to drip off. Place on a wire rack. While the
glaze is still tacky, scatter sprinkles over each.

### 08. Set.
Let the glaze set for 10 minutes before eating — though
they're best the same day.

## Notes

For meal-prep doughnut lovers: the unbaked dough rounds can be
refrigerated overnight after step 4 (slow-proof in the fridge);
let warm 30 minutes at room temp before air-frying. Without
fresh raspberries, use 3 tbsp raspberry jam strained through a
sieve. For a darker pink, add 1 tsp of beetroot powder to the
glaze. The doughnut "holes" (the cutouts) cook in 3 minutes —
roll them in cinnamon sugar after frying for a second batch of
snacks. Day-old doughnuts: split in half, butter, pan-toast in a
skillet for an unconventional but excellent French toast.
