The mixed root-vegetable crisp is one of the smart small uses for
the air fryer. The technique is the same one used for apple
chips: low temperature, long time, paper-thin slices. The
difference is the snacks coming out are savoury — earthy, mineral,
with the kind of textural variety that mass-produced crisps can't
replicate.

The vegetables that work: parsnip (sweet, nutty), beetroot (deep
red, earthy), sweet potato (orange, sweet), carrot (mild, slightly
herbal). Skip waxy potatoes — they're a different snack with a
different technique. The trick is uniform thinness, which means
a mandoline is non-optional unless your knife skills are
extraordinary.

The seasoning is salt-forward but minimal: sea salt, a little
black pepper, maybe a pinch of thyme or rosemary. Don't drown
them in spices — the vegetable flavour is what you're going for.
The colour variation from the multiple roots is the visual
hook.

The photo shows them in a wide white ceramic bowl: a mix of
purple-red beetroot crisps, orange sweet potato, golden parsnip,
amber carrot, all dusted with sea salt. The kind of snack that
photographs better than anything you'd buy in a bag.

## Ingredients

- 1 large parsnip, peeled
- 1 medium beetroot, peeled (raw, not pre-cooked)
- 1 medium sweet potato, peeled
- 2 large carrots, peeled
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp flaky sea salt
- ½ tsp coarse black pepper
- 1 tsp dried thyme (optional)
- ½ tsp smoked paprika (optional)

## Method

### 01. Slice on a mandoline.
Slice each vegetable into 1/16-inch (about 1.5mm) rounds. A
mandoline gives uniform thickness; very sharp knife and steady
hand otherwise. Pat all slices very dry with a clean kitchen
towel — moisture prevents crisping.

### 02. Toss in oil.
In a large bowl, gently toss all the slices with olive oil
until lightly coated. Sprinkle with salt, pepper, and thyme/
paprika if using.

### 03. Air fry — low and slow.
Heat the air fryer to 300°F (150°C). Arrange the slices in a
single layer in the basket — overlap a little is OK, they
shrink. Work in 2–3 batches, keeping each variety separate or
mixed (mixing is faster and looks better).

### 04. Cook with shaking.
Cook each batch for 16–22 minutes, shaking the basket every
5 minutes. The slices should curl at the edges and look
mostly dry. Beetroot takes longest, parsnip shortest.

### 05. Cool on a rack.
Transfer the cooked crisps to a wire rack as they come out.
They firm up to fully crisp within 3–5 minutes of cooling.

### 06. Combine and serve.
Once all batches are done and cool, combine in a single bowl.
Sprinkle extra flaky salt if needed.

## Notes

Store in an airtight container at room temperature for up to 4
days. If they soften, refresh in the air fryer for 2 minutes at
300°F. For a sweet-savoury hit, dust with a small pinch of
maple sugar after cooking. Don't try to do this at higher heat
(400°F) — the slices burn before they crisp. The slow dehydration
is the whole point. For a fancier dip, serve with a small bowl
of crème fraîche mixed with chives, lemon zest, and salt.
