Coconut shrimp lives at the intersection of beach restaurant menus
and freezer-aisle appetizers, and as a result, most people have only
ever had a bad version. The bad version is mealy on the inside, with
a coconut crust that tastes like nothing, served with a syrupy
orange dipping sauce that's mostly sugar. The good version — which
is honestly not hard to make at home — is something else entirely:
plump shrimp with a snappy bite, surrounded by a crust of toasted
coconut that's both sweet and savoury, dipped in something with
actual personality.

The trick is the coconut. Sweetened shredded coconut (not unsweetened
flake) is what you want — it browns into proper colour, gives the
crust the right balance of sweet and toasty. Mixed half-and-half
with panko, it gets you both colour and crunch without going so
sweet it tips over into dessert.

The apricot sauce is the dipping companion that doesn't try too
hard. Apricot preserves, a spoonful of dijon, a splash of rice
vinegar, a teaspoon of chilli paste for heat. Mix in a small bowl,
no cooking. The acid and the chilli cut through the richness of
the fried coconut crust; you don't want a sauce that adds more
sweetness.

The photo shows it set up for sharing: a basket-lined dish piled
with bronze-coloured coconut shrimp, a small teal ramekin of
apricot sauce nestled in the middle. Eat with your hands. Make
double; they go fast.

## Ingredients

**For the shrimp:**
- 1 lb (450g) large shrimp (16/20 count), peeled, deveined,
  tail-on or off
- ½ cup all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 1 cup sweetened shredded coconut
- ¾ cup panko breadcrumbs
- Olive oil spray

**For the apricot dipping sauce:**
- ⅓ cup apricot preserves
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp sambal oelek or sriracha
- 1 tsp soy sauce
- ½ tsp grated fresh ginger

## Method

### 01. Make the sauce.
In a small bowl, whisk together the apricot preserves, Dijon, rice
vinegar, sambal, soy sauce, and ginger until smooth. Set aside —
it gets better as it sits.

### 02. Set up dredging stations.
Three bowls: flour mixed with salt, pepper, and garlic powder; beaten
eggs; coconut mixed with panko.

### 03. Dredge.
Pat the shrimp very dry with paper towels. Working one at a time:
dust in seasoned flour (shake off excess), dip in egg (let drip
off), then press firmly into the coconut-panko mix — turn to coat
every face. Set on a plate. Repeat with all the shrimp.

### 04. Spray with oil.
Spray the coated shrimp lightly all over with olive oil. This is
what makes them golden instead of pale.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange the shrimp in a single
layer in the basket, not touching — work in 2 batches if needed.
Cook 7–9 minutes, flipping at the 4-minute mark, until the coconut
is deep golden and the shrimp are just opaque.

### 06. Serve immediately.
Pile onto a platter or shallow bowl. Set the apricot sauce in the
middle. Eat with your hands while warm — the coconut crust loses
its crunch within 15 minutes.

## Notes

If you can't find sweetened shredded coconut, use unsweetened plus
2 teaspoons of sugar mixed in — the sugar helps browning. For an
easier sauce, store-bought sweet chili sauce works (Mae Ploy is the
benchmark). Don't be tempted to bake these in the oven instead —
the air fryer's hot circulating air is what crisps the coconut
without burning it. Frozen shrimp work — thaw fully under cold
water (15 min) and pat very dry. Tails-on look prettier; tails-off
are easier to eat at a party.
