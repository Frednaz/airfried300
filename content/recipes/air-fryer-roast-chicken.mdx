The whole roast chicken is the entry-level masterpiece of home
cooking. Once you can roast a chicken — really roast it, with the
skin shattering and the breast still moist — you can feed people. You
can host. You can be the person who casually makes "just a chicken"
on a Tuesday and have everyone leave saying it was the best meal of
the week.

The air-fryer version of this dish is, surprisingly, the one a lot of
home cooks now prefer. The conventional oven roast is forty-five
minutes at 425°F plus a rest; the air fryer is fifty minutes at 360°F
with vastly better skin crisping (the smaller chamber and direct heat
crisp the skin like nothing else short of a deep-fry). The catch:
you need a small-to-medium bird (3 to 3½ lb / 1.4–1.6 kg) — anything
bigger won't fit in most baskets.

The single most important step is the dry-salt overnight. Pat the
bird dry, salt it generously inside and out, set it on a rack
uncovered in the fridge for 12–24 hours. The salt seasons deep into
the meat, the skin loses moisture and tightens up, and what you get
the next day is a chicken with shattering, glass-like skin. The
photo shows it: deep amber bird being held on a black plate by hands
in a denim apron, the skin crisp enough that you can almost hear it.

## Ingredients

- 1 whole chicken, 3 to 3½ lb (1.4–1.6 kg)
- 1 tbsp kosher salt (for overnight salting)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp dried thyme
- 1 tsp dried rosemary
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 lemon, halved
- 4 cloves garlic, smashed
- Fresh thyme sprigs (optional)

## Method

### 01. Salt early (12–24 hours ahead).
Pat the chicken dry, inside and out, with paper towels. Sprinkle the
kosher salt evenly all over the skin and inside the cavity. Place
on a rack over a tray and refrigerate, uncovered, for 12–24 hours.
This is the single most important step.

### 02. Bring to room temperature.
About 30 minutes before cooking, take the chicken out of the fridge.
Pat dry again (any surface moisture is the enemy of crisp skin).

### 03. Season.
In a small bowl, combine the thyme, rosemary, paprika, garlic
powder, and pepper. Rub the chicken all over with olive oil, then
sprinkle the herb mix evenly over the skin. Stuff the cavity with
lemon halves, smashed garlic, and fresh thyme sprigs (if using).
Tuck the wing tips under and tie the legs together with kitchen
twine.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 360°F (180°C). Place the chicken
breast-side-down in the basket. Cook for 25 minutes.

### 05. Flip and finish.
Carefully flip the chicken breast-side-up using two pairs of tongs
(or a folded kitchen towel). Continue cooking for 22–28 minutes
more, until the internal temperature reads 165°F (74°C) at the
thickest part of the thigh, and the skin is deeply burnished.

### 06. Rest.
Move the chicken to a board and rest 10 minutes — non-negotiable.
The juices need to redistribute or you'll lose half of them when
you cut.

### 07. Carve.
Remove the twine. Carve into legs, thighs, breasts. Slice the breast
across the grain into ½-inch slices.

## Notes

**Why the overnight salt matters more than anything else**: dry brine
is what turns "homemade roast chicken" into "great roast chicken." If
you only do one thing differently from your usual recipe, do this
one. Don't add extra salt after the dry brine — it's already seasoned.
For gravy, deglaze the basket drippings with a splash of wine and
stock, simmer 5 minutes, finish with a knob of butter. Save the
carcass — Monday's chicken stock starts here.
