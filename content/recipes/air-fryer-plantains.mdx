Tostones (toh-STOH-nez) are the side dish that anyone who's spent
time in Puerto Rico, Cuba, the Dominican Republic, or Colombia
considers a basic right of being alive. Green plantains — firm,
starchy, not sweet like ripe yellow plantains — sliced thick, fried
once to soften, smashed flat with a wooden mallet (or the bottom
of a glass), then fried again to get the edges proper-crisp. Salted
hot, eaten with a garlic dipping sauce, served as a side with
basically every Caribbean meal.

The traditional version is deep-fried in lard or oil. The air-fryer
version uses a tablespoon of oil total, and the result is
genuinely indistinguishable in flavour from the deep-fried version
(slightly less greasy, which some people prefer). The two-cook
sequence is preserved: first a 6-minute soften, then the smash,
then another 6 minutes for the final crisp.

The photo shows the platter: about 20 smashed plantains piled
generously on a white plate, with a bowl of bright green guacamole/
avocado dip in the corner. The plantains have visible char marks
on the edges and salt crystals on top. This is what
weekday-dinner-with-personality looks like.

A note on the plantains: get them GREEN, not yellow. Yellow
plantains are ripe and sweet; they make a different dish (plátanos
maduros — sweet, soft, caramelised, also wonderful but not this).
For tostones you want the skin to be green and the fruit firm
inside.

## Ingredients

**For the tostones:**
- 3 large green plantains
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp kosher salt (plus more for finishing)
- Flaky sea salt, to finish

**For the avocado-garlic dip:**
- 2 ripe avocados, pitted and peeled
- 1 clove garlic, finely grated
- 2 tbsp fresh lime juice
- 2 tbsp fresh cilantro, chopped
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp red pepper flakes (optional)
- 1 tbsp olive oil

## Method

### 01. Peel the plantains.
Cut the ends off each plantain. Score the skin lengthwise (just
the skin, not into the fruit) in 2 or 3 strips down the length —
green plantain skin is thick and hard to peel. Use your thumb to
pry under each scored strip and pull the skin away.

### 02. Slice.
Cut the plantains crosswise into 1-inch thick rounds. You should
get about 8 rounds per plantain.

### 03. First fry (soften).
Heat the air fryer to 380°F (193°C). Toss the rounds with 1 tbsp
olive oil and the kosher salt. Arrange in a single layer in the
basket. Cook 6 minutes, shaking once. They should be soft and pale
golden — not crisp.

### 04. SMASH.
Tip the par-cooked rounds onto a cutting board. Using the bottom
of a glass, a small plate, or the flat of a meat mallet, press
each round flat — to about ¼-inch thick. They shouldn't break
entirely; aim for a flattened disc with the texture intact.

### 05. Second fry (crisp).
Brush or spray the smashed plantains with the remaining ½ tbsp
olive oil. Crank the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange in a
single layer, in 2 batches if needed. Cook 6–8 minutes, flipping
at the 4-minute mark, until deep golden with crisp edges.

### 06. Make the avocado dip.
While the second fry happens, mash the avocados with garlic, lime
juice, cilantro, salt, red pepper flakes, and olive oil in a bowl
until smooth-ish (some chunks are good).

### 07. Finish and serve.
Salt the hot tostones generously with flaky salt. Serve
immediately with the avocado dip alongside.

## Notes

**The smashing is what makes them tostones** — without it, you've
just got fried plantain coins. The smashing increases surface area
and creates ragged edges that crisp deeply. For a quicker dip,
just halve an avocado and drizzle with lime and salt. For a real
flavour upgrade, top the tostones with shredded slow-cooked
ropa vieja or pulled pork to make tostones rellenos (stuffed
tostones). Tostones are best within 10 minutes — they're hard to
revive once they soften.
