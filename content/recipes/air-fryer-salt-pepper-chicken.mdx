Salt-and-pepper chicken is the British-Chinese takeaway dish that
sits next to crispy chilli beef in the canon — same crispy-coated
protein, different finishing logic. Where chilli beef gets a sauce,
salt-and-pepper chicken stays dry: the seasoning is a dust of
five-spice, salt, and ground pepper applied at the end, plus a quick
stir-fry of aromatics tumbled through. The result is crunchier, less
sweet, more aromatic.

The dry seasoning is the soul of the dish. Sichuan peppercorns, if
you have them, lend a faint floral numbness that distinguishes a
"good" version from a "great" one; white pepper adds the heat without
the black-pepper colour. You toast both briefly, grind together with
salt, then dust over the chicken right after it comes out of the
basket. The aromatics — sliced red chilli, garlic, ginger, spring
onions, baby bok choy — get a quick stir-fry in the same pan.

The photo shows the assembled dish in its parchment-lined serving
vessel: chunks of crisp chicken with visible cornflour coating, dark
red chilli slices, pale-green bok choy stems, scallion rounds, all
glistening slightly. There's a side of pickled cucumber for cutting
through the spice. This is the kind of dish you make on a Friday
night when the takeaway costs £15 and you've already paid for the
ingredients.

## Ingredients

**For the chicken:**
- 1¼ lb (570g) boneless, skinless chicken thigh, cut into 1-inch
  pieces
- 1 tsp Chinese five-spice
- 3 tbsp cornflour (cornstarch)
- 1 large egg, beaten
- 1 tbsp light soy sauce
- 1 tbsp neutral oil

**For the seasoning blend:**
- 1 tsp Sichuan peppercorns (or 1 tsp black peppercorns if unavailable)
- 1 tsp white peppercorns (or extra black)
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp Chinese five-spice

**For the stir-fry:**
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 3 cloves garlic, finely sliced
- 1-inch piece ginger, finely sliced
- 1 red chilli, finely sliced (deseeded for less heat)
- 1 red bell pepper, cut into ½-inch strips
- 2 heads baby bok choy, halved lengthwise
- 4 spring onions, sliced (whites and greens separated)

## Method

### 01. Marinate the chicken.
In a bowl, combine the chicken with the five-spice, beaten egg, and
soy sauce. Toss to coat. Sprinkle in the cornflour and toss again
until each piece is coated. Drizzle in the neutral oil.

### 02. Toast and grind the seasoning.
In a dry skillet over medium heat, toast the Sichuan and white
peppercorns for 90 seconds until fragrant. Tip into a mortar (or
spice grinder), add the salt and five-spice, grind to a coarse
powder. Set aside.

### 03. Air fry the chicken.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange the chicken in a single
layer in the basket — work in two batches if needed. Cook 11–13
minutes, shaking the basket every 4 minutes, until deeply crisp and
the internal temperature reads 165°F (74°C).

### 04. Stir-fry the aromatics.
While the chicken cooks, heat the neutral oil in a wok or large
skillet over high heat. Add the garlic, ginger, chilli, and white
parts of the spring onions. Stir-fry 30 seconds until fragrant. Add
the red pepper and bok choy. Stir-fry 2 minutes — vegetables should
still have bite.

### 05. Combine and season.
Add the hot crispy chicken to the wok. Sprinkle generously with the
seasoning blend (start with 2 tsp, taste, add more) and the spring
onion greens. Toss for 20 seconds — just to distribute, don't let
the chicken sit and soften.

### 06. Serve.
Plate immediately over steamed jasmine rice. Eat within 10 minutes
of cooking.

## Notes

The seasoning blend keeps in a small jar for 2 months — make a
double batch. For a more authentic version, add 1 tablespoon of
chilli oil drizzled over before serving. For a tofu version (very
good), use extra-firm tofu cut into 1-inch cubes — same dredging
and cooking method, 12 minutes in the air fryer. Without baby bok
choy, use sliced cabbage or broccolini. Don't skip the high-heat
stir-fry step — the aromatics need to char slightly to release
their full flavour.
