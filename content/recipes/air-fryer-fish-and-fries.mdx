The classic British fish and chips relies on a deep fryer and a wet
batter — neither of which the home cook particularly wants to deal
with on a Wednesday. The air-fryer version skips the batter
entirely and goes with a dry crumb crust made from crushed
cornflakes and seasoned flour. The cornflakes are the trick: they
give a thicker, crackier exterior than panko, with enough internal
texture to mimic the shatter of beer batter.

The fries are the same recipe as the Maris Piper chips published
earlier in this collection — chunky, soaked in cold water, dried,
oiled, then cooked in two stages (low for tenderness, high for
crisp). The fish and fries cook in series, not in parallel: fries
first (they take longer), set aside under foil to keep warm, then
fish cooks for the last 9 minutes. Both arrive on the plate at the
same time.

The photo shows the British pub plating: two steel-and-paper-lined
baskets, one with golden-bronze fish portions and chunky chips, a
quartered lemon wedge, a small bowl of tartar sauce, glass of pale
ale just out of frame. This isn't sophisticated. It's not trying
to be. It's Friday night, a glass of something cold, and dinner
that justifies eating with your hands.

## Ingredients

**For the fries:**
- 1½ lb (700g) Maris Piper or Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled, cut into
  ½-inch chips
- 2 tbsp sunflower oil
- 1 tsp kosher salt

**For the fish:**
- 1¼ lb (570g) cod or haddock fillets, cut into 4 portions
- ½ cup (60g) all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 2 cups (60g) cornflakes, crushed to coarse crumbs
- Olive oil spray

**To serve:**
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges
- Tartar sauce or malt vinegar
- Flaky sea salt
- Mushy peas (optional, traditional)

## Method

### 01. Prep the fries.
Cut potatoes into ½-inch chips, soak in cold water 20 minutes,
drain, pat very dry, toss with the sunflower oil and salt.

### 02. First fry (low heat, fries only).
Heat the air fryer to 320°F (160°C). Cook the fries 12 minutes,
shaking every 4 minutes. They should be tender but still pale.
Tip onto a plate, set aside.

### 03. Dredge the fish.
While fries cook, set up three bowls: flour mixed with salt,
pepper, paprika, and garlic powder; beaten eggs; crushed
cornflakes. Pat the fish dry, then dredge each portion in flour,
then egg, then cornflakes — press the crumbs in firmly. Set on
a plate.

### 04. Spray the fish with oil.
Spritz the breaded fish all over with olive oil for colour.

### 05. Second fry (high heat — fish and fries together).
Crank the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Return the fries to the
basket and lay the fish on top (or in a single layer alongside
if it fits). Cook 9–11 minutes — flip the fish at the 5-minute
mark, shake the fries underneath at the same time. Fish should
flake when nudged, crust deep golden; fries should be crisp on
every edge.

### 06. Salt and serve.
Pile onto plates or in newspaper-lined baskets. Squeeze lemon over
the fish, scatter flaky salt over the fries. Serve with tartar
sauce, malt vinegar, and mushy peas on the side.

## Notes

The cornflakes vs. panko choice matters — cornflakes have a coarser,
craggier texture that gives more crackle. Crush them in a zip-top
bag with a rolling pin; coarse fragments are good. For a beer-
battered approximation, you can mix 2 tbsp beer into the egg wash
— gives a subtle yeasty flavour. For thicker fillets (over 1 inch),
add 3 minutes to the fish time. Frozen fish works if fully thawed
and very dry; pat aggressively. Don't try cod from the freezer
section that's already breaded — make your own crust, it's
indisputably better.
