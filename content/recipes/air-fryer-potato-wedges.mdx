There's a kind of restaurant potato — thick-cut, mahogany on the outside,
fluffy as a baked potato inside, the skin still on — that for a long time
you couldn't really make at home unless you owned a deep fryer or were
willing to fight a sheet pan and 400 degrees of oven for an hour. The air
fryer changed that, almost by accident. The combination of dry circulating
heat and a shallow oil coat does the same thing a deep fry does, in twenty
minutes, with a tablespoon of oil instead of a quart.

These are that wedge. Skin-on for grip, soaked in cold water before they
go in (the only step you might be tempted to skip — don't, it's the
difference between crisp and crackling), tossed with smoked paprika so
they pick up the russet edge you see in the photo, then air-fried at 400°F
until they sound hollow when you tap them on the plate. Serve them while
they're hot enough to make you breathe through your mouth. Ketchup is
allowed and encouraged, but they're salty enough to stand on their own.

A note on quantities: the photo is what you get from two big russets —
four servings as a side, or one and a half if it's just you and a movie.
They don't keep, so don't bother saving leftovers; just make less next
time, or eat what you don't finish cold from the fridge the next morning
(I do this, I'm not ashamed).

## Ingredients

- 2 large russet potatoes (about 1.5 lb), scrubbed
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- ¼ tsp black pepper, freshly cracked
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped (to finish)
- Ketchup, for serving

## Method

### 01. Cut and soak.
Slice each potato lengthwise in half, then each half into 4 wedges (8 per
potato). Submerge in cold water for 20 minutes — this pulls surface starch
so the wedges crisp instead of steam.

### 02. Dry and season.
Drain and pat very dry with a clean kitchen towel (water is the enemy of
crispness). Toss with the olive oil, then sprinkle on the salt, paprika,
garlic powder, and pepper. Toss again to coat every face.

### 03. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange wedges in a single layer with
space between them — work in two batches if your basket is small. Cook
18–22 minutes, shaking every 7 minutes, until deep golden on every edge.

### 04. Finish.
Tip the wedges onto a plate, scatter the parsley, and serve immediately
with ketchup. They lose their crackle if they sit, so eat them hot.

## Notes

The soak is optional — skip it and you'll still get good wedges, just
slightly less shattering. The smoked paprika is what gives them the rusty
edge you see; sweet paprika keeps it brighter.
