The beef fajita (the OG fajita — the chicken version is a later
adaptation) traces back to ranch hands in South Texas in the
1930s, who were given the cheap skirt-steak cut as part of their
wages and figured out it tasted better than its price suggested
if you marinated it heavily, grilled it hot, and sliced it thin
across the grain. The dish has evolved from there into a
restaurant standard, but the home version is genuinely close to
the ranch-hand original.

The cut matters. Skirt steak (the traditional choice) or flank
steak (more available, slightly less flavourful) are the right
cuts — both are thin, deeply flavoured, and benefit from quick
high-heat cooking. Don't use ribeye or sirloin; they're too
thick for fajitas and the wrong shape for slicing.

The marinade is straightforward: lime juice, olive oil, soy sauce
(the umami component that pushes this above plain Tex-Mex),
garlic, chili powder, cumin, smoked paprika. The acid in the lime
breaks down the surface of the meat so it cooks tender; the soy
adds salt-and-umami; the spices give it the colour and flavour
that distinguish fajita meat from generic grilled steak.

The cook is short — about 10 minutes in the air fryer for a
1-inch flank, which gets you medium-rare. The slicing is the most
important step: thin (¼-inch) across the grain (perpendicular to
the long fibres). Slice with the grain and you get chewy, ropey
fajitas; slice across the grain and you get tender, melt-in-the-
mouth slices.

## Ingredients

**For the marinade:**
- 1½ lb (700g) flank or skirt steak
- ¼ cup lime juice (about 3 limes)
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp soy sauce
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tsp chili powder
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp brown sugar

**For the vegetables:**
- 1 red bell pepper, sliced into ½-inch strips
- 1 yellow onion, sliced into half-moons
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp kosher salt

**To serve:**
- 8 small flour tortillas, warmed
- 1 ripe avocado, sliced
- ¾ cup pico de gallo (or salsa)
- ¼ cup fresh cilantro, chopped
- 2 limes, cut into wedges

## Method

### 01. Marinate the steak.
In a zip-top bag or shallow dish, combine the lime juice, olive
oil, soy sauce, garlic, chili powder, cumin, paprika, salt, and
brown sugar. Add the steak, turn to coat. Marinate at room
temperature for 30 minutes, or refrigerated up to 8 hours.

### 02. Season the vegetables.
In a bowl, toss the peppers and onion with olive oil and salt.

### 03. Cook the vegetables first.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Add the peppers and onion
to the basket in a single layer. Cook 8 minutes, shaking once,
until softened and charred at the edges. Tip onto a plate, tent
with foil to keep warm.

### 04. Cook the steak.
Lift the steak from the marinade (let excess drip off), pat dry
slightly with paper towels. Place in the empty basket. Cook
8–10 minutes for medium-rare on a 1-inch-thick steak — internal
temperature 130°F at the thickest part. Flip at the 4-minute
mark.

### 05. Rest the steak.
Move steak to a cutting board, tent loosely with foil, rest 5
minutes. Don't skip this — slicing right away loses all the
juice.

### 06. Slice across the grain.
Look at the steak — you'll see the long fibres running in one
direction. Slice the steak across (perpendicular to) those
fibres, into ¼-inch slices.

### 07. Warm the tortillas.
Stack tortillas, wrap in a damp paper towel, microwave 30
seconds. Or warm over a gas flame for char.

### 08. Assemble.
Pile the steak and vegetables onto a serving platter. Squeeze a
lime wedge over the top. Scatter cilantro. Serve family-style
with tortillas, avocado, and pico de gallo alongside — let
people build their own.

## Notes

For a charred-edge effect that mimics the grill, brush the steak
with an extra teaspoon of olive oil halfway through cooking. The
marinade can be made up to 3 days ahead. Leftover steak slices
are excellent over rice the next day, or on a torta with mayo,
lettuce, tomato, avocado. **Don't over-marinate**: more than 12
hours and the lime breaks down the meat to a mushy texture. 30
minutes to 8 hours is the sweet spot.
