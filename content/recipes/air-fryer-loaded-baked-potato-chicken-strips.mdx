The "loaded baked potato" flavour profile — sour cream, bacon,
cheddar, chives — has spread across American food in the last
decade like a virus. It's appeared in chips, pretzels, dips,
mashed potatoes, and now, here, applied to a chicken strip. The
coating is what carries the flavour: dehydrated potato flakes
(the kind you'd use for instant mashed potatoes) mixed with finely
crumbled cooked bacon, grated parmesan, chopped chives, and salt.
Press it onto egg-dipped chicken strips, air-fry, and the result
is a chicken strip that tastes specifically of a loaded baked
potato. Improbable but excellent.

The trick is the potato flakes. They give the coating a unique
texture — coarser than panko, lighter than regular breadcrumbs,
with a faint potato sweetness that you don't get from any other
breading. The bacon mixed in is what gets you the smoky-savoury
note that defines the loaded-baked-potato flavour family. Real
parmesan, not the green can stuff.

## Ingredients

**For the chicken:**
- 1½ lb (700g) chicken tenderloins (or chicken breast cut into ¾-
  inch strips)
- ½ cup all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 2 large eggs, beaten

**For the loaded coating:**
- 1 cup (60g) dehydrated potato flakes (instant mashed potato
  flakes)
- ½ cup (50g) finely crumbled cooked bacon (about 4 slices —
  use the air-fryer-bacon method)
- ⅓ cup (35g) finely grated parmesan
- 2 tbsp fresh chives, finely chopped
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- Olive oil spray

**To serve:**
- ½ cup sour cream
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh chives (to finish)
- Mixed greens salad

## Method

### 01. Cook the bacon first.
If you don't have cooked bacon ready, air-fry 4 slices at 400°F
for 9 minutes. Cool fully, then crumble very finely with your
hands.

### 02. Set up dredging stations.
Three bowls: seasoned flour (mixed with salt, pepper, garlic
powder); beaten eggs; the loaded coating (potato flakes, bacon
crumbs, parmesan, chives, salt, and paprika).

### 03. Dredge.
Pat the chicken dry. Working one at a time, dust in flour, dip in
egg, then press firmly into the loaded coating, packing it on. Set
on a plate.

### 04. Spray.
Spray the coated strips all over with olive oil for colour.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange in a single layer in
the basket. Cook 10–12 minutes, flipping at the 6-minute mark,
until the coating is deeply golden and the chicken reaches 165°F
internal.

### 06. Plate.
Move to a serving platter. Scatter additional fresh chives over
the top. Serve with a small bowl of sour cream for dipping and a
green salad on the side.

## Notes

For a heavier flavour, double the bacon and parmesan in the
coating. For a vegetarian "loaded" coating using the same flavour
profile, swap the bacon for finely chopped sun-dried tomatoes
(unusual but works). Don't substitute regular breadcrumbs for the
potato flakes — they're the unique ingredient here. The cooked
strips freeze well: cool fully, freeze in a single layer, then bag.
Reheat from frozen at 380°F for 8 minutes.
