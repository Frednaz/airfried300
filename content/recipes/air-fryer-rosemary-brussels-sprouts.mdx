This is the second brussels sprouts recipe in the collection — and
intentionally so. The first version (the chilli-honey-soy glaze) is
the modern, slightly sweet, Asian-influenced take. This one is the
traditional savoury Italian-American treatment: garlic, rosemary,
parmesan, breadcrumbs. Both work. They're for different moods and
different meals.

The technique is the same as any roasted-brussels-sprouts recipe:
halve, toss with oil and seasoning, air-fry until the cut sides
caramelise and the outer leaves char. The finishing step is what
distinguishes this from a plain version. While the sprouts are
still hot, you toss them with a mixture of grated parmesan, panko
breadcrumbs, and the residual oil from the basket. The cheese
melts into the surface; the breadcrumbs cling and toast on
contact. The result is a sprout that's part-vegetable, part-
casserole-top.

The photo shows them served family-style — a small terracotta-
rimmed bowl piled with sprouts, all visibly coated in golden
breadcrumbs and visible parmesan, with charred green leaves
poking through. A spoon stuck in the bowl. The kind of side dish
people fight over at the table.

Rosemary is the herb that makes this feel like Thanksgiving. If
you're making this in summer when fresh rosemary is everywhere,
use it generously. If you're making it in February when you only
have dried, halve the quantity (dried rosemary is much more
concentrated).

## Ingredients

**For the sprouts:**
- 1½ lb (700g) Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 3 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 1 tbsp fresh rosemary, finely chopped (or 1 tsp dried)
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- ¼ tsp red pepper flakes (optional)

**For the topping:**
- ⅓ cup (35g) grated parmesan (real Parmigiano-Reggiano)
- ¼ cup (25g) panko breadcrumbs
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp lemon zest
- 1 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped

## Method

### 01. Prep the sprouts.
Trim woody bottoms and remove any loose outer leaves (save them;
they crisp like chips). Halve through the stem.

### 02. Season.
In a bowl, toss the halved sprouts with olive oil, minced garlic,
rosemary, salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes.

### 03. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place sprouts cut-side-down
in the basket in a single layer. Cook 12–14 minutes, shaking
twice, until the cut sides are deeply browned and outer leaves
are charred.

### 04. Toast the breadcrumbs.
While the sprouts cook, heat 1 tbsp olive oil in a small skillet
over medium heat. Add the panko and toast 2 minutes, stirring
often, until deep golden. Off heat, stir in the parmesan and lemon
zest. The cheese will melt slightly from the residual heat.

### 05. Toss and finish.
Tip the hot sprouts into a serving bowl. Sprinkle the parmesan-
breadcrumb mixture over the top and toss gently to coat. The
cheese clings to the heat; the breadcrumbs distribute throughout.
Scatter parsley over the top.

### 06. Serve immediately.
These are best within 10 minutes of finishing — the breadcrumb
crust loses its crisp over time. If serving family-style at a
holiday table, hold the topping until just before serving.

## Notes

For a thicker breadcrumb coating, double the panko-parmesan
mixture. For Thanksgiving's specific aesthetic, add ¼ cup of
crisp-cooked diced bacon (from the air-fryer bacon recipe). For
a vegan version, swap parmesan for nutritional yeast (use 2
tablespoons) and skip the breadcrumb-toasting butter step (use
olive oil only). The leftover sprouts reheat passably in the air
fryer for 3 minutes at 380°F, but they really are best fresh.
