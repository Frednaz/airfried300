There's a category of healthful snack that is, in fact, just dessert
that's been retitled. Energy balls are one of these. You take dates
(which are 73% sugar by weight), nuts (which are mostly fat), maybe
some chocolate or other concentrated sweetness, blitz it in a food
processor, and call them "energy balls" or "bliss balls" or "wellness
bites." They taste like candy. They are nutritionally not that
different from candy. We continue with the fiction because it makes us
feel better. Fine.

These are the pecan-pie variant — pecans and dates blended with a
splash of maple syrup, vanilla, and a pinch of salt. The flavour is
remarkably close to actual pecan pie filling, minus the corn syrup and
the butter and the eggs. They roll up into golf-ball-sized rounds and
get coated in chopped pecans, which gives them visible texture and
makes them photograph well (see: photo).

A note on the dates: Medjool dates are non-negotiable for this recipe.
The drier Deglet Noor dates won't blend properly and won't bind. You
want Medjools — large, sticky, sweet, the consistency of caramel when
pitted. Twelve makes a batch; a one-pound bag of Medjools has roughly
30, so this is a small commitment.

## Ingredients

- 1 cup pecan halves, plus 2 tbsp finely chopped (for coating)
- 12 Medjool dates, pitted (about 1 cup pitted)
- 1 tbsp pure maple syrup
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon

## Method

### 01. Toast the pecans.
For extra depth (optional but worth it), toast the 1 cup of pecans in
a dry skillet over medium heat for 4 minutes, shaking the pan, until
fragrant. Cool to room temperature.

### 02. Process.
In a food processor, pulse the toasted pecans alone first, about 8
pulses, until coarsely chopped (don't go to powder — leave some
texture). Add the pitted dates, maple syrup, vanilla, salt, and
cinnamon.

### 03. Blend until it holds.
Process for 60–90 seconds, stopping to scrape the sides once, until
the mixture forms a sticky paste that holds together when pressed.
The dates and pecans should be fully incorporated; you should see
small pecan pieces but no large date chunks.

### 04. Roll.
Scoop the mixture by the tablespoon and roll between your palms into
balls (slightly damp hands help — the mixture is sticky). You should
get 12 balls.

### 05. Coat.
Roll each ball in the chopped pecans, pressing gently so they stick.
The photo shows what you're going for: visible pecan pieces clinging
to the dark date interior.

### 06. Chill and store.
Refrigerate for 30 minutes to firm up. Store in an airtight container
in the fridge for up to 2 weeks, or freeze up to 3 months.

## Notes

For variations: swap pecans for walnuts (closer to a brownie flavour),
or almonds (richer, slightly drier). Add 2 tablespoons of unsweetened
cocoa powder for a chocolate-pecan-pie version. For salted-caramel
vibes, top with a pinch of flaky salt after rolling. These keep
beautifully in the freezer — eat them straight from the freezer for a
fudgey-cold texture. Good with coffee, after dinner, at 4pm when you
feel a snack coming on.
