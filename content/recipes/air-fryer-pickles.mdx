The fried pickle is one of those Southern-American bar appetisers
that travelled from a few specific roadside restaurants in
Arkansas (where the recipe is generally credited to a place called
the Duchess Drive-In, 1963) to nearly every chain-restaurant
appetizer menu in the country, and now to home air fryers, in the
space of about six decades. The journey was justified. A properly
fried pickle is one of the great salty-sour-crunchy snacks ever
invented.

The technique is straightforward but has one critical step: drying
the pickles before breading. Pickle slices straight out of the jar
are dripping with brine, and that brine prevents the breading from
sticking. Pat each slice on both sides with paper towels until they
look matte — like, really commit to the patting. Then dredge in
flour-egg-panko (the standard breading), spray with oil, and air
fry until the crust crackles.

The dipping sauce is non-negotiable: ranch dressing. Homemade if
you're being ambitious (Greek yogurt, mayo, buttermilk, dill,
chives, garlic powder, lemon), bottled if you're not (Hidden
Valley is the cultural reference, but any quality ranch works).

## Ingredients

**For the pickles:**
- 1 (16 oz / 450g) jar dill pickle chips (pre-sliced), drained
- ½ cup all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp cayenne pepper (optional)
- 2 large eggs, beaten
- 1 tbsp dill pickle brine
- 1½ cups (150g) panko breadcrumbs
- ½ tsp dried dill
- Olive oil spray

**For the ranch:**
- ½ cup mayonnaise
- ⅓ cup buttermilk (or plain Greek yogurt)
- 1 tbsp fresh dill, chopped
- 1 tbsp fresh chives, chopped
- 1 tsp lemon juice
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper

## Method

### 01. Make the ranch.
Whisk all ranch ingredients together in a small bowl. Refrigerate
while you make the pickles — it improves as it sits.

### 02. DRY THE PICKLES.
This is the most important step. Drain the pickle chips, spread
them on a clean kitchen towel in a single layer, and pat
aggressively on both sides until they no longer look wet. Let
sit 5 minutes. Pat again. They should be matte.

### 03. Set up dredging stations.
Three bowls: flour mixed with paprika, garlic powder, salt, and
cayenne; eggs whisked with the tablespoon of pickle brine; panko
mixed with dried dill.

### 04. Dredge.
Working in small batches: dust pickles in flour, dip in egg, press
firmly into panko. Set on a plate.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Spray the breaded pickles
all over with olive oil. Arrange in a single layer in the basket
(work in 2 batches). Cook 6–8 minutes, flipping at the 4-minute
mark, until both sides are deep golden and crisp.

### 06. Serve immediately.
Pile into a basket. Serve with the ranch alongside. Eat within
10 minutes — they soften as they cool.

## Notes

**Use chip-cut pickles, not spear-cut** — the round shape is the
correct one for this dish, and they cook evenly. If you only have
spears, slice them into ¼-inch rounds first. For an extra crispy
result, double-dredge: egg again, panko again. **Why fried pickles
sometimes turn out soggy**: insufficient drying. The brine is the
enemy. Dry, dry, dry. For a Buffalo-style twist, toss the cooked
pickles with 2 tablespoons of buffalo sauce just before serving —
serve with blue cheese instead of ranch.
