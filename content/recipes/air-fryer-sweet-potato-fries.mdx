Sweet potato fries are the side dish that gets ordered in the spirit of
healthful intent and arrives, disappointingly, soft. The problem is
that sweet potatoes have more sugar and more moisture than regular
russet potatoes, which means they want to steam rather than crisp.
Restaurants get around this by deep-frying them, which works but is
beyond what most home cooks will do for a Tuesday. The air fryer plus
one specific trick — a coating of cornstarch — gets you to actual
crisp at home.

The cornstarch is critical. A tablespoon tossed with the cut fries
before cooking forms a dry coating that wicks away surface moisture
and crisps into a thin shell as it cooks. Without it: soft fries.
With it: properly crisp fries that hold their structure for a good
fifteen minutes after coming out of the basket.

The photo is the finished plate: deeply orange-tan fries on a ceramic
plate, with a small ramekin of ketchup at the edge. They've been
seasoned with smoked paprika (you can see the slight darker tint) and
a generous pinch of salt. The cuts are deliberately uneven — slimmer
ones get crispier, fatter ones stay tender in the middle. Variety is
good.

## Ingredients

- 2 large sweet potatoes (about 1½ lb / 700g total), peeled
- 1 tbsp cornstarch (cornflour, in UK terms)
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- ¾ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- Pinch of cayenne (optional)

**To serve:**
- Ketchup, sriracha mayo, or chipotle aioli

## Method

### 01. Cut.
Peel the sweet potatoes. Cut each into ½-inch-thick planks, then cut
the planks into ½-inch-wide fries. Aim for roughly uniform thickness
but don't agonise over it.

### 02. Soak (optional but recommended).
Place the cut fries in a bowl of cold water for 15 minutes. This pulls
out excess starch and helps with crisping. Drain and dry very well
with a clean kitchen towel.

### 03. Toss with cornstarch.
In a large bowl, sprinkle the cornstarch over the dry fries and toss
until each is lightly coated. Add the olive oil, paprika, garlic powder,
salt, pepper, and cayenne (if using). Toss again to coat evenly.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Spread the fries in a single
layer in the basket — don't crowd. Work in two batches if your basket
is small. Cook 14–16 minutes, shaking the basket every 5 minutes,
until deeply golden and crisp on the outside.

### 05. Serve immediately.
Sweet potato fries are at their crispest within 5 minutes of cooking.
Move to a plate, hit with a final pinch of salt, serve with ketchup.

## Notes

**Why they go soft at restaurants**: not the cornstarch step. Skip it
and you'll get the same outcome. The other crisping trick is to make
them thinner — ⅓-inch fries crisp more than ½-inch ones, but they
fall apart easier in the basket. For a sweet version, swap the paprika
for cinnamon and add a teaspoon of brown sugar — serve with a drizzle
of maple syrup. For a spicy version, double the cayenne. Standard
russet fries work with the same method; reduce the cooking time to 13
minutes.
