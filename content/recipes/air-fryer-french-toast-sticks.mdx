There's a family of breakfast recipes that exist primarily to
become kid food — chicken nuggets, dinosaur-shaped chicken nuggets,
pancakes shaped like Mickey Mouse, French toast sticks. The
fascinating thing about French toast sticks specifically is that
they're better than regular French toast in a structural sense:
more surface area, so more crust-to-interior ratio; sized for
dipping rather than knife-and-fork; portable enough to eat with one
hand. They're upgrade-shaped, not downgrade-shaped.

This version goes one step further with a cornflake crust. The
standard French toast batter (egg, milk, vanilla, cinnamon) gets
applied as usual; then before the sticks go in the air fryer, they
get rolled in crushed cornflakes. The result is a French toast
stick with two textures: a properly crackling outside and a soft,
custardy inside. The cornflakes also save you from the floppy
sogginess that bedevils oven-baked French toast.

## Ingredients

- 8 slices thick-cut brioche or challah bread (slightly stale is
  ideal), cut into 1-inch strips (about 24 sticks)
- 4 large eggs
- ½ cup whole milk
- 2 tbsp maple syrup
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 1½ tsp ground cinnamon
- Pinch of salt
- 2 cups (60g) cornflakes, crushed to coarse crumbs
- 2 tbsp granulated sugar (to mix with the cornflakes)
- Olive oil spray
- Powdered sugar, to dust

**To serve:**
- Maple syrup
- Fresh strawberries, quartered
- A scoop of plain yogurt (optional)

## Method

### 01. Make the custard.
Whisk the eggs with milk, maple syrup, vanilla, cinnamon, and salt
in a wide bowl until smooth.

### 02. Prep the cornflake coating.
Crush the cornflakes in a zip-top bag with a rolling pin (you want
coarse fragments, not powder). Tip into a shallow bowl and mix in
the granulated sugar.

### 03. Dip and coat.
Working with a few at a time: dip the bread sticks in the custard
(30 seconds — they should be moist but not soggy), let excess
drip off, then roll firmly in the crushed cornflakes to coat all
sides. Set on a plate.

### 04. Spray and air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 360°F (180°C). Arrange the sticks in a
single layer (work in 2 batches), spritz with olive oil. Cook
7–9 minutes, flipping at the 4-minute mark, until both sides are
deep golden.

### 05. Dust and serve.
Move to a plate, dust with powdered sugar. Serve immediately with
a small bowl of maple syrup for dipping and fresh strawberries on
the side.

## Notes

For meal prep, the sticks freeze beautifully after step 4 (cool
fully first). Reheat from frozen in the air fryer for 4 minutes at
350°F. Day-old bread works better than fresh — the dryer it is, the
more custard it can absorb without going to mush. Without
cornflakes, panko + an extra teaspoon of sugar gives a similar
result. The plain yogurt on the side cuts the sweetness if you want
to balance the breakfast out.
