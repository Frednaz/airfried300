The American diner-style meatloaf — beef, ketchup glaze, served
with peas and curly fries — is one of those dishes that traces a
direct line from a 1950s Sunday-supper magazine spread to a
contemporary midwest diner menu. The whole point of it is the
glaze: ketchup mixed with brown sugar and mustard, brushed onto
the top, caramelised in the oven into something between BBQ sauce
and candied tomato. The meat is the vehicle; the glaze is the
soul.

The classic version is baked in a loaf pan, which produces a
problem: the bottom doesn't get any crust because it's sitting in
its own rendered fat, and the glaze on top is the only proper
caramelised surface. The free-form version (no pan, shaped by hand
into football-shaped patties) fixes this. Every surface caramelises;
the rendered fat drips into the basket below; the glaze caramelises
on multiple faces.

The photo shows the platonic 1950s plate: a single mini-meatloaf
centre-plate with a glossy ketchup-brown-sugar crust visible from
the angle, a pile of curly fries and a small heap of bright green
peas alongside, on a plain white plate. This is what nostalgia
looks like, served on a Wednesday.

A note on the meat: 80/20 beef is non-negotiable. Leaner blends
(85/15 or 90/10) make a dry meatloaf. The fat is what keeps it
juicy through the cook.

## Ingredients

**For the meatloaf:**
- 1 lb (450g) ground beef (80/20)
- ⅓ cup (35g) panko breadcrumbs
- ¼ cup whole milk
- 1 large egg, beaten
- 1 small yellow onion, very finely diced (about ½ cup)
- 2 cloves garlic, finely minced
- 2 tbsp ketchup
- 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp dried Italian herbs

**For the glaze:**
- ⅓ cup ketchup
- 2 tbsp brown sugar
- 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- A few dashes of hot sauce (optional)

**To serve:**
- Curly fries (air-fried from frozen, 15 min at 400°F)
- Steamed green peas

## Method

### 01. Hydrate the breadcrumbs.
In a small bowl, combine the panko and milk. Let sit 3 minutes —
the panko softens into a paste. This step is what keeps the
meatloaf tender; skip it and you'll get a dense, dry result.

### 02. Mix the meatloaf.
In a larger bowl, combine the beef, hydrated panko mixture, egg,
onion, garlic, 2 tbsp ketchup, Worcestershire, mustard, salt,
pepper, and Italian herbs. Mix with your hands until just combined
— don't overwork it.

### 03. Shape.
Divide the mixture in half. Form each into a football-shaped loaf
about 4 inches long. Place on a plate.

### 04. Make the glaze.
Whisk together the ketchup, brown sugar, vinegar, Dijon, paprika,
and hot sauce in a small bowl.

### 05. First cook (no glaze yet).
Heat the air fryer to 360°F (180°C). Place the meatloaves in the
basket with space between them. Cook 15 minutes.

### 06. Glaze and finish.
Brush the tops and sides generously with the glaze. Return to the
air fryer for another 8–10 minutes, until the internal temperature
reaches 160°F (71°C) and the glaze is caramelised and bubbling.

### 07. Rest and serve.
Rest 5 minutes on a plate. Serve each meatloaf whole with curly
fries and peas on the side.

## Notes

For 4 servings, scale the recipe up and shape 4 loaves — cook them
in two batches in the basket. For a turkey or chicken version
(leaner but still good), swap the beef for ground dark-meat turkey
and add 1 extra tablespoon of olive oil to compensate. Leftover
meatloaf cold the next day on a sandwich (with mayo and pickles,
on white bread) is one of the great unappreciated lunches. The
glaze recipe also works on pork chops, salmon, or even cottage pie.
