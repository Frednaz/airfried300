The fudgy brownie versus cakey brownie debate has been carried out
in cooking magazines, on internet message boards, and across
kitchen tables for at least fifty years. The fudgy-brownie partisan
position is correct: a brownie should be dense, slightly chewy, and
have a glossy crackled top that comes from emulsifying sugar into
melted butter and chocolate. A cakey brownie is just a brown cake,
and we already have cakes for that.

The air fryer is unexpectedly good at fudgy brownies. The
ceiling-heat effect that worked for the cookie pie applies here
too: the top sets quickly into a thin crackled meringue layer
while the middle stays barely-set and ultra-fudgy. The cook time
is shorter (about 20 minutes vs. 30+ in the oven), which makes
fudgy brownie territory easier to hit because you've got less
margin between underdone and overcooked.

The photo shows the test of a properly made brownie: nine cut
squares arranged in a 3x3 grid on parchment, each one with a
visibly cracked top (the meringue layer), shiny in places, with
fudgy dark interiors visible at the cut edges. The crackled top is
the signature; if your brownies don't crack, you didn't beat the
sugar into the wet ingredients long enough.

A note on the chocolate: use real chocolate (a 70% dark bar,
chopped) plus cocoa powder. Cocoa-only brownies don't have the
fudgy density; chocolate-only brownies don't have the deep cocoa
flavour. The combination is non-negotiable.

## Ingredients

- ½ cup (115g) unsalted butter
- 4 oz (115g) dark chocolate (60–70%), chopped
- 1 cup (200g) granulated sugar
- 2 large eggs, at room temperature
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- ⅔ cup (85g) all-purpose flour
- ⅓ cup (30g) unsweetened cocoa powder
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ⅓ cup (60g) chocolate chips (optional)

## Method

### 01. Line a small pan.
Line a 6-inch (15 cm) square baking pan or a 7-inch round with
parchment paper, letting it overhang the sides for easy lifting.

### 02. Melt butter and chocolate.
In a heatproof bowl set over a pot of barely-simmering water (or
in 20-second microwave bursts), melt the butter and chopped
chocolate together until smooth. Off heat.

### 03. Beat in the sugar.
While the chocolate mixture is still warm, whisk in the sugar
vigorously for 90 seconds. The sugar partially dissolves into the
fat — this is what creates the crackled top. Don't skip this step.

### 04. Add the eggs.
Let the mixture cool 1 minute, then whisk in the eggs one at a
time, then the vanilla.

### 05. Fold in the dries.
Sift together the flour, cocoa, and salt. Fold gently into the wet
mixture — just until no streaks of flour remain. Don't overmix.
Fold in chocolate chips if using.

### 06. Pour and air fry.
Pour into the prepared pan, smooth the top. Heat the air fryer to
320°F (160°C). Cook 18–22 minutes — a toothpick inserted should
come out with a few moist crumbs (not wet batter, not clean). The
edges should be set, the middle slightly underdone.

### 07. Cool.
Lift out using the parchment, cool on a rack for at least 30
minutes before cutting (they firm up as they cool). Cut into 9
squares with a hot knife (run under hot water, wipe dry, slice).

## Notes

These are best after a full hour of cooling, when the texture
settles. They keep 3 days in an airtight container at room
temperature, or up to a week in the fridge (microwave 12 seconds
to refresh). For a salted version, sprinkle flaky sea salt on top
before air-frying. For a more dramatic version, add a swirl of
peanut butter (warmed slightly) over the top of the batter before
cooking — drag a toothpick through for swirls. Don't use this
recipe in a square 8x8 pan — too thin, they overbake. Stick to
the small (6-inch) pan or scale up the recipe by 1.5×.
