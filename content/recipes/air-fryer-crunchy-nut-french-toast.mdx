Crunchy Nut cornflakes are one of those cereal products that ruined
breakfast cereals for everyone — once you've had honey-coated golden
cornflakes, plain cornflakes feel like a punishment. This recipe
takes the Crunchy Nut box and uses it as a breading for French toast.
The result is a triangle of soaked bread with a coating that crackles
like real fried chicken, sweet from the cereal honey, with the warm
custardy interior of French toast underneath.

The technique is "double dredge" — bread dipped in egg custard, then
pressed into crushed cornflakes, then back in custard, then back in
cornflakes. The double coat catches the maximum amount of cereal and
gives you the crunchiest result. Air-fry at 360°F for 8 minutes per
side, basting halfway with a brush of melted butter to deepen the
colour.

Served with whipped cream and a generous drizzle of golden syrup
(British/Australian inverted-sugar syrup; substitute pure maple if
unavailable). The photo shows two triangles fanned across a dark
blue plate: deeply golden cornflake crust visible, syrup oozing
across, white whipped cream piled on the side. The kind of breakfast
that justifies a 6am wake-up because you got to eat it.

## Ingredients

**For the French toast:**
- 4 thick slices brioche or challah (about 1-inch thick)
- 3 large eggs
- ⅓ cup (80ml) whole milk
- 2 tbsp double cream (heavy cream)
- 1 tbsp caster sugar
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon
- Pinch of salt

**For the crunchy coat:**
- 3 cups (90g) Crunchy Nut cornflakes (regular cornflakes work but
  add 2 tbsp brown sugar and 1 tbsp honey to the dredge)
- 2 tbsp melted unsalted butter (for brushing)

**To serve:**
- ½ cup whipped cream
- ¼ cup golden syrup (or maple syrup)
- Fresh berries (optional)

## Method

### 01. Crush the cornflakes.
Place the cornflakes in a zip-top bag and crush with a rolling pin
to small pieces (not powder). Tip into a shallow bowl.

### 02. Whisk the custard.
In a wide bowl, whisk eggs, milk, cream, sugar, vanilla, cinnamon,
and salt until smooth.

### 03. Cut and dip.
Cut each slice of bread diagonally into 2 triangles (8 total). Dip
each triangle in the custard, letting it soak briefly (10 seconds
per side — don't oversoak).

### 04. Double-coat.
Press each soaked triangle firmly into crushed cornflakes, turning
to coat all sides. Return to the custard for a brief dip, then back
into the cornflakes for a second coat. Set on a plate.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 360°F (180°C). Brush triangles with melted
butter. Cook in a single layer 6–8 minutes, flipping at the 4-minute
mark and brushing with more butter. They should be deeply golden on
both faces.

### 06. Serve.
Plate 4 triangles per serving, fanned across the plate. Top with a
generous dollop of whipped cream and a heavy drizzle of golden syrup.
Add berries if using.

## Notes

For a coconut-flake variation: swap half the cornflakes for
unsweetened coconut flakes. For meal-prep: the cornflake mix can be
prepped the night before; the assembled triangles can be coated and
refrigerated up to 6 hours before air-frying. Don't oversoak the
bread — it goes mushy and the crust falls off. The custard ratio is
important: too eggy and it tastes like an omelette; this ratio
(3 eggs + ⅓ cup milk + 2 tbsp cream) is the right balance.
