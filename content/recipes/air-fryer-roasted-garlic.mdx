There's a class of pantry ingredient that does not exist commercially
in any form approaching the real thing, and roasted garlic is the
patient zero. The jarred version in oil tastes like one note (acidic,
faintly metallic). The fancy gel-tubed version is closer but still
shy. Roasted garlic — actually roasted, with caramelised edges and
that almost-spreadable softness through the middle — is what they're
trying to be. Twenty minutes at 380°F gets you there.

The classic version is a whole head, top sliced off, oiled and wrapped
in foil in a 400°F oven for an hour. The air fryer beats this in every
practical way: faster (20 minutes vs. an hour), less foil, more even
caramelisation. The downside is you have to peel the cloves first —
the air fryer doesn't roast whole heads well because the papery skin
blocks the convection. Five minutes of peeling for a jar of liquid
gold; fine trade.

The photo shows the finish: peeled cloves in a small ramekin, deeply
golden at the tips, glassy in the middle. The texture goes from raw-
firm-crunchy to soft-spreadable-sweet, with the bite gone entirely.
Each clove is now usable as either a half-teaspoon of garlic punch (one
clove smashed into a dressing) or a base for something bigger (six
cloves whipped into mashed potatoes, a head's worth folded into
softened butter for compound butter).

## Ingredients

- 2 heads of garlic, cloves separated and peeled (about 20 cloves)
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- Sprig of fresh thyme or rosemary (optional)

## Method

### 01. Peel.
The faster way to peel garlic: smash each clove with the flat of a
knife — the skin will pop off. Or shake all the cloves in a sealed jar
for 30 seconds — the skins separate. Either is faster than picking at
them with your fingernails.

### 02. Toss.
In a small heat-safe bowl or a folded square of foil, toss the peeled
cloves with the olive oil and salt. If using herbs, tuck the sprig
underneath.

### 03. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 380°F (193°C). Place the bowl or foil packet in
the basket. Cook 18–22 minutes, shaking once halfway through, until
the cloves are deeply golden at the tips and soft to the press of a
fork.

### 04. Cool and store.
Let cool in the bowl — the cloves firm up slightly as they release
steam. Use immediately, or transfer to a jar, top with olive oil to
cover, and refrigerate up to 2 weeks.

## Notes

The oil the garlic was roasted in is itself a small treasure — use it
in vinaigrettes, drizzle over a pizza after baking, or to start a soup.
For confit-style (fully submerged, even softer), use ½ cup of olive
oil and cook 25 minutes — the cloves go almost gel-like. Don't be
tempted to roast whole heads in the air fryer; the air circulation
doesn't penetrate the papery layers and the cloves stay raw at the
center while the outside scorches.
