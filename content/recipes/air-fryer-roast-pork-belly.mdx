The pork-belly roast is the British Sunday-dinner standard that
intimidates many home cooks because of the crackling. The skin can
go from raw to leathery to perfect to burned in a small window of
heat. The air fryer makes this easier — focused high heat blisters
the crackling reliably, and the lower-heat finish renders the fat
without overcooking the meat.

The technique: score the skin in tight crosshatch, salt heavily,
let it sit uncovered in the fridge overnight to dry the surface (or
1 hour minimum), then air-fry at 200°C for 15 minutes to puff the
crackling, drop to 160°C for 50–60 more minutes to render the fat.

The dry overnight rest is critical for proper crackling. Skip it and
you get leathery skin instead of glass-shatter. Salt the skin only
— no oil — the salt draws out moisture and the rendered fat does
the rest.

## Ingredients

- 2 lb (900g) pork belly, skin on
- 2 tbsp kosher salt (for the skin)
- 1 tsp fennel seeds, crushed
- 1 tsp coarse black pepper
- 1 tsp dried sage (or 1 tbsp fresh, chopped)
- 2 tsp olive oil (for the flesh side only)
- 1 tsp flaky sea salt (to finish)

**To serve:**
- Apple sauce
- Roasted vegetables
- Mashed potato

## Method

### 01. Score and dry.
Pat the pork-belly skin very dry with paper towels. Score the skin
in a tight crosshatch pattern, ¼-inch deep and ½-inch apart, cutting
through the fat but not into the meat. Place uncovered in the fridge
for at least 1 hour (overnight is much better).

### 02. Salt the skin.
Pat the skin dry again. Sprinkle the kosher salt evenly over the
skin, pressing it into the cuts.

### 03. Season the flesh side.
Mix fennel seeds, pepper, and sage with the olive oil. Rub onto the
flesh side of the pork only — not the skin.

### 04. Blister the crackling.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place the pork skin-side-up in
the basket. Cook 15 minutes — the skin should bubble and puff.

### 05. Drop the heat.
Reduce the air fryer to 320°F (160°C). Continue cooking for 55–65
minutes more, until the meat reads 145°F (63°C) at the thickest
part for medium, or 150°F (66°C) for slightly more well-done.

### 06. Rest.
Move to a board and rest 10 minutes — non-negotiable, both for the
meat and the crackling (the skin firms up further as it cools).

### 07. Carve.
Cut the crackling off in one piece if possible, snap into shards.
Slice the meat across the grain into ½-inch slices.

### 08. Serve.
Plate the meat with shards of crackling on top. Add apple sauce on
the side.

## Notes

If the crackling isn't quite shatter-crisp by the end: blast at 400°F
for 3 more minutes (watching closely). For a stronger flavour on the
meat: brine overnight in 1 cup water + 1 tbsp salt + 1 tbsp sugar +
crushed garlic. The bones (if your belly has them) make excellent
stock — simmer with onion, carrot, peppercorns for 2 hours. Apple
sauce on the side is non-negotiable.
