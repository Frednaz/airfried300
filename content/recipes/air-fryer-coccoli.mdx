Coccoli are the small fried-dough rounds that line every Tuscan
aperitivo platter. They're traditionally deep-fried — hot oil,
spluttering, dough balls floating to the surface and turning golden
in 90 seconds — then split open and stuffed with stracchino (a soft,
spreadable cow's-milk cheese from northern Italy) and prosciutto
crudo. The combination of warm puffed dough, cool creamy cheese, and
salty ham is exact.

The air fryer version skips the deep-fry while keeping the structural
result. Small balls of yeasted dough (the same dough you'd use for
focaccia) get sprayed with oil and cooked at 200°C until they puff
golden. They come out slightly less crisp than the deep-fried version
but still pillowy inside.

The dough is the slowest part — about an hour of proofing total.
Everything else is quick. The assembly is theatre: split coccolo,
spoon cheese onto bottom half, drape with prosciutto, replace top.
Eat with fingers.

The photo shows them assembled on a ceramic plate: golden split dough
rounds with white stracchino visible through the gap and pink
prosciutto draped across, alongside a bowl of stracchino with olive
oil and pepper for additional spreading. The aperitivo standard.

## Ingredients

**For the coccoli dough:**
- 2 cups (250g) plain flour
- 1½ tsp instant yeast
- 1 tsp salt
- 1 tsp caster sugar
- ¾ cup (180ml) warm water
- 2 tbsp olive oil, plus more for spraying

**For the stracchino mix:**
- 250g stracchino (or substitute: 200g fresh ricotta + 50g cream cheese)
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp lemon zest
- ¼ tsp flaky salt
- Cracked black pepper

**To serve:**
- 100g prosciutto crudo, thinly sliced
- Cracked black pepper
- Flaky salt
- Extra olive oil for drizzling

## Method

### 01. Make the dough.
In a stand mixer with a dough hook, combine flour, yeast, salt, and
sugar. Add warm water and olive oil. Knead 6 minutes until smooth
and slightly tacky.

### 02. Proof.
Place in an oiled bowl, cover, leave in a warm spot 45 minutes until
nearly doubled.

### 03. Shape.
Tip dough onto a lightly floured surface. Divide into 18 equal
pieces. Roll each into a tight ball.

### 04. Second proof.
Place balls on parchment, cover loosely with a tea towel, leave 15
minutes to puff slightly.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Spray the balls generously with
olive oil. Cook in batches in a single layer for 8–10 minutes, until
deeply golden and puffed.

### 06. Prepare the cheese.
In a bowl, stir stracchino with olive oil, lemon zest, salt, and a
generous grind of pepper.

### 07. Split and stuff.
Slice each coccolo in half horizontally (or split apart with thumbs
while still warm). Spread the bottom half generously with stracchino
mix. Drape a piece of prosciutto across.

### 08. Plate.
Replace tops loosely (or serve open-faced). Pile onto a platter,
finish with cracked pepper and a drizzle of olive oil. Eat
immediately.

## Notes

Without stracchino: ricotta + cream cheese (4:1) approximates the
texture. Other classic fillings: salami, mortadella, fig jam, or
sautéed greens with garlic. The dough can be made the night before
and slow-proofed in the fridge — bring to room temperature 30
minutes before shaping. Best eaten within 2 hours of cooking; the
dough rounds go stale quickly. For a vegetarian version: skip the
prosciutto, add a thin slice of marinated artichoke or sundried
tomato.
