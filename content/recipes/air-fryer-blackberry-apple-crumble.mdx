The fruit crumble is one of those British puddings that's so
deeply embedded in the national culinary memory that arguing for
its excellence feels redundant. Apple crumble. Rhubarb crumble.
Plum crumble. The recipe is genuinely simple: chopped fruit on
the bottom, oat-and-butter rubble on top, baked until the fruit
bubbles and the topping crisps. The air fryer does the same job
faster — 18 minutes instead of 35 — and produces a topping
slightly crunchier than the oven version because the dry heat
circulates aggressively.

Blackberry-and-apple is the autumn pairing. Bramley apples
(British cooking apples — Granny Smith works as substitute)
break down into sweet-sharp purée when cooked. Blackberries
hold their shape until the juices burst and stain everything
purple-red. The combination is the classic British hedgerow
pudding — both fruits available at the same time, both at their
best when slightly overripe.

The crumble itself: flour, butter, sugar, oats, a pinch of
salt, a flutter of cinnamon. Rub the butter into the flour with
your fingertips until it looks like coarse breadcrumbs, then
stir in the oats and sugar. The oats are non-negotiable for
British crumble — they give the topping its characteristic
shaggy texture.

## Ingredients

**For the fruit:**
- 1 lb (450g) Bramley apples (or Granny Smith), peeled, cored,
  cut into 1-inch chunks
- 8 oz (225g) blackberries (fresh or frozen)
- 3 tbsp caster sugar
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- ½ tsp ground ginger (optional)
- 1 tbsp plain flour (to thicken juices)

**For the crumble topping:**
- 1 cup (140g) plain flour
- ½ cup (110g) cold unsalted butter, cubed
- ½ cup (90g) demerara sugar (or dark brown sugar)
- ½ cup (50g) rolled oats
- ½ tsp ground cinnamon
- ¼ tsp fine sea salt

**To serve:**
- Vanilla ice cream
- Or custard
- Or pouring cream

## Method

### 01. Prepare the fruit.
In a bowl, toss the apple chunks and blackberries with sugar,
lemon juice, cinnamon, ginger (if using), and flour. Tip into
a small ovenproof dish that fits your air fryer (round 7-inch
cake tin or similar).

### 02. Make the crumble topping.
In a separate bowl, rub the cold butter into the flour with
your fingertips until the mixture resembles coarse breadcrumbs.
Stir in the demerara sugar, oats, cinnamon, and salt.

### 03. Top the fruit.
Scatter the crumble mixture evenly over the fruit, covering it
completely. Don't pat down — keep the topping shaggy and loose
for maximum crisp.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 350°F (175°C). Place the dish in the
basket and cook 18–22 minutes, until the topping is deep
golden and you can see fruit juices bubbling up at the edges.

### 05. Rest.
Pull from the air fryer and rest 5 minutes — the juices thicken
slightly as it cools.

### 06. Serve.
Spoon into bowls. Add a scoop of vanilla ice cream (or a glug
of custard, or pouring cream). Eat warm.

## Notes

For frozen blackberries: no need to defrost, just add 2 more
minutes to the cooking time. Pear-and-blackberry works the same
way. For a gluten-free version, swap the flour for almond flour
in both the fruit (omit the thickening) and the topping. For
make-ahead: assemble through step 3, refrigerate up to 24 hours,
cook from cold (add 4 minutes). Leftovers reheat in the air fryer
for 4 minutes at 350°F.
