There's a small culinary swindle that pre-bagged croutons get away with,
and it's that they exist at all. Croutons are stale bread, oil, and
heat. The recipe has not changed in five hundred years. And yet a bag
of "Caesar croutons" costs five dollars and weighs less than a CD, and
they taste like dust and the inside of a glove compartment. Make them
yourself; it takes six minutes.

The trick is that they need to be cubed evenly so they crisp at the same
rate. Tear them — don't cube them with a knife — irregular edges catch
more oil and brown harder, which is exactly what you want for the
texture. The photo is what you get: not perfectly uniform, which is the
point, with the deep mahogany corners that say "I got this hot oil and
I held it."

The bread matters less than you'd think. Sourdough is best because the
crust adds structure, but you can do this with grocery-store white,
day-old baguette ends, the heels of whatever loaf has been judging you
from the breadbox for a week. You're going to add garlic powder and
salt; the underlying bread is mostly the carrier.

## Ingredients

- 4 cups stale bread, torn into 1-inch pieces (sourdough, white, baguette,
  anything sturdy)
- 3 tbsp olive oil (or melted butter, or a mix)
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp dried oregano (or Italian seasoning)
- ¾ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper, freshly cracked
- 2 tbsp grated parmesan (optional, but recommended)

## Method

### 01. Tear.
Tear (don't cube) the bread into rough 1-inch pieces. The irregular
edges crisp better than clean knife cuts.

### 02. Toss.
In a large bowl, drizzle the oil over the bread and toss until each
piece has a sheen. Sprinkle on the garlic powder, oregano, salt, pepper,
and parmesan if using. Toss again to distribute.

### 03. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 380°F (193°C). Spread the bread in the basket in
a single layer — work in two batches if your basket is small. Cook 6–8
minutes, shaking the basket every 3 minutes, until deeply golden and
crackling-crisp.

### 04. Cool to crisp.
Tip onto a plate and let cool 5 minutes — they crisp further as they
release steam. Eat warm or store in a sealed jar.

## Notes

These keep at room temperature for a week in an airtight container —
they'll soften slightly, just retoast for 2 minutes at 350°F to refresh.
For Caesar-specific croutons, swap the parmesan for 2 teaspoons of
anchovy paste whisked into the oil and double the garlic powder. For
sweet croutons (yes, on ice cream — try it), use butter, cinnamon, and
sugar instead of oil, salt, and herbs.
