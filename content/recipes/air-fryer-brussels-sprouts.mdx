The brussels sprout problem of the past forty years has been one of
cooking method. Boiled brussels sprouts (the British school dinner of
the 1970s and 80s) are appalling — sulphurous, soggy, the colour of
old felt. Roasted brussels sprouts (the answer of the 2010s) are good
— properly browned, slightly bitter, fundamentally interesting. Air-
fried brussels sprouts are roasted brussels sprouts, but better and
faster: same caramelisation, half the time, no sheet pan.

The glaze here is the move that pushes them from "side dish" to
"thing people actually fight over at the table." Soy sauce, honey,
rice vinegar, chili flakes — sticky, salty-sweet, with enough heat to
balance the natural sweetness of the caramelised sprouts. You toss the
sprouts in the warm glaze right after they come out of the basket, so
the surface stays crisp but every leaf gets coated.

The photo is the result — dark green sprouts with charred outer
leaves, glossy with glaze, dotted with red chili flakes and visible
sesame seeds, served in a deep blue ceramic bowl against a navy
napkin. The glaze has pooled slightly at the bottom of the bowl, which
is fine — that's the sauce you want to spoon over rice later.

## Ingredients

**For the sprouts:**
- 1 lb (450g) Brussels sprouts, trimmed and halved (large ones quartered)
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper

**For the glaze:**
- 2 tbsp soy sauce (or tamari for gluten-free)
- 1½ tbsp honey (or maple syrup for vegan)
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
- ½ tsp red chili flakes (more if you like it hot)
- 1 clove garlic, finely grated
- 1 tsp finely grated fresh ginger
- 1 tsp sesame seeds (to finish)

## Method

### 01. Trim and halve.
Trim the woody bottom off each sprout and remove any loose outer
leaves. Cut each in half through the stem (quarters for very large
ones). Save the loose leaves — they crisp up like chips in the basket.

### 02. Oil and season.
In a bowl, toss the halved sprouts and any loose leaves with the
olive oil, salt, and pepper.

### 03. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place the sprouts cut-side-down
in the basket in a single layer. Cook 12–14 minutes, shaking the
basket every 4 minutes, until the cut sides are deeply browned and
the outer leaves are crisp and charred at the tips.

### 04. Make the glaze.
While the sprouts cook, whisk together the soy sauce, honey, rice
vinegar, sesame oil, chili flakes, garlic, and ginger in a small
bowl.

### 05. Toss and serve.
Move the hot sprouts to a wide serving bowl. Pour the glaze over
them and toss gently — the residual heat will tighten the glaze into
a sticky coating. Scatter sesame seeds over the top. Serve
immediately.

## Notes

For a smokier version, swap the chili flakes for chipotle in adobo
(1 tsp finely minced). For more contrast, top with finely sliced
scallions or quick-pickled red onion. Without the glaze, the sprouts
work as a plain side — same method, finish with lemon juice and
shaved parmesan. They reheat passably in the air fryer for 3 minutes
at 380°F but they're really best within 10 minutes of cooking. Serve
over a bowl of jasmine rice with some peanuts for a quick weeknight
dinner.
