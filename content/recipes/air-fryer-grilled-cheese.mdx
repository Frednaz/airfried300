The grilled cheese sandwich is so universal that arguing for any
single "correct" version is a fool's errand. There are people who
believe the right cheese is American singles; people who believe
the right bread is white sandwich bread; people who believe
mayo-on-the-outside beats butter-on-the-outside. All of these
people are right, in the same way that there is no wrong way to
order coffee.

The version pictured here is one specific direction within that
spectrum: sourdough bread, sharp cheddar plus melting mozzarella
(or fontina), a thick slice of tomato, three strips of crispy
bacon. Butter on the outside, not mayo. Cooked in the air fryer
because — and this is the part the skillet can't match — the heat
hits both sides at the same time, so the bread browns evenly
without you watching it. You walk in 5 minutes after putting it in
the basket and find a sandwich that looks like the photo: stacked
in a three-high tower, deeply golden crust, cheese spilling out
the sides, the tomato just barely visible under the orange ooze.

The photo's plating is unfussy — a wooden cutting board, a piece
of butcher paper, a pickle spear on the side, a bottle of San
Pellegrino just out of focus in the background. This is what
weekday lunch looks like when you want it to feel slightly
elevated.

A note on cheese: the magic ratio is one melty cheese (mozzarella,
fontina, monterey jack) plus one flavour cheese (sharp cheddar,
gruyere, aged provolone). Two melty cheeses gets you texture but
no flavour. Two flavour cheeses gets you flavour but a gritty
sandwich. The combination is the answer.

## Ingredients

**Per sandwich (recipe makes 2):**
- 2 slices sourdough bread (½-inch thick, slightly stale is ideal)
- 1 tbsp salted butter, softened (¾ tbsp for the bread + ¼ for
  finishing)
- 1 oz (28g) sharp cheddar, sliced thin
- 1 oz (28g) low-moisture mozzarella or fontina, sliced thin
- 1 thick slice (¼-inch) ripe tomato
- 3 slices crispy cooked bacon (see the air-fryer-bacon recipe)
- A pinch of kosher salt and black pepper
- ½ tsp Dijon mustard (optional, spread thinly inside)

## Method

### 01. Butter the bread.
Spread softened butter generously on one side of each slice of
bread — these are the outsides of the sandwich. Use enough that
the bread shines slightly.

### 02. Build the sandwich.
Flip one slice butter-side-DOWN. If using mustard, spread thinly
on the now-up surface. Layer: half the cheddar, half the
mozzarella, the tomato slice (pat it dry with paper towel first
to prevent sogginess), the bacon, the remaining cheese. Top with
the second slice of bread, butter-side-UP.

### 03. Season.
Sprinkle the top butter with a small pinch of salt and a crack of
pepper. Not strictly necessary but elevates the surface.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 380°F (193°C). Place the sandwich in the
basket. Cook for 5–6 minutes, flipping at the 3-minute mark, until
both sides are deep golden and the cheese has melted enough to
spill at the edges.

### 05. Rest 1 minute.
Move to a board and rest 1 minute — the cheese sets enough not to
ooze entirely out when you cut.

### 06. Slice and serve.
Cut on the diagonal (always diagonal, this is a structural
preference) and serve immediately with a pickle and a glass of
something cold.

## Notes

**For a classic plain version**: skip the bacon and tomato, use 2
oz total cheese (1 cheddar + 1 mozzarella). For a French-onion
version: add caramelised onions, swap mozzarella for gruyere, add
a tiny smear of Dijon. For a tomato-soup pairing (the canonical
combo): make the soup while the sandwich cooks — Campbell's plus a
splash of cream and a knob of butter is honestly fine. Don't try
to make 4 sandwiches at once in the air fryer; the crowded basket
steams them. Two at a time, max.
