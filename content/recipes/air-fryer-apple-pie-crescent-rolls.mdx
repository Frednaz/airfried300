There is a class of dessert that exists somewhere between "I baked
something" and "I bought something," and crescent-roll desserts are the
queen of that class. The dough comes out of a refrigerated tube. It has
no shame about it. You're not making puff pastry from scratch and you're
not pretending to — you're using a shortcut that happens to bake into
something flaky and gold-brown that nobody at the table will complain
about.

These are apple pie in eight bites. Granny Smith because you want the
tartness against the cinnamon sugar — Honeycrisp goes too soft, Pink
Lady would work in a pinch. You sauté the chopped apple briefly in
butter and cinnamon until the kitchen smells like a fall candle, roll
each crescent triangle around a tablespoon of filling, brush the tops
with more butter, dust them in cinnamon sugar, and air fry at 350°F for
seven or eight minutes. They come out the color of toast and they fall
apart in your hands.

The photo shows them stacked on parchment, slightly leaking apple at the
ends, which is the correct way for them to look. If yours seal up
perfectly, you didn't put in enough filling. Serve while still warm,
with a scoop of vanilla ice cream if you want to be serious about it, or
on their own if it's a Wednesday and nobody's looking.

## Ingredients

- 1 can (8 oz) refrigerated crescent roll dough (8 triangles)
- 1 large Granny Smith apple, peeled and finely chopped (about 1 cup)
- 3 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
- 3 tbsp cinnamon sugar (2 tbsp granulated sugar + 1 tsp ground cinnamon)
- 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
- Pinch of salt
- Vanilla ice cream, to serve (optional)

## Method

### 01. Sauté the apple.
Melt 1 tablespoon of the butter in a small skillet over medium heat. Add
the chopped apple, lemon juice, salt, and 1 tablespoon of the cinnamon
sugar. Cook, stirring, for 3–4 minutes — you want the apple soft but not
mushy. Slide into a bowl to cool slightly.

### 02. Roll.
Unroll the crescent dough and separate into 8 triangles along the
perforations. Spoon about a tablespoon of apple filling onto the wide
end of each triangle. Roll from wide end to point, tucking as you go.

### 03. Butter and sugar.
Melt the remaining 2 tablespoons of butter. Brush each crescent all over
(top and bottom). Place them on a plate and roll each one in the
remaining cinnamon sugar to coat.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 350°F (175°C). Arrange the crescents seam-side
down in a single layer, leaving space between them — they puff. Cook for
7–8 minutes, until deep golden.

### 05. Dust and serve.
Move to a plate. If there's any cinnamon sugar left, dust the tops while
they're still warm. Serve immediately, with ice cream if you're in that
kind of mood.

## Notes

These reheat passably in the air fryer (3 minutes at 300°F) but they're
best within an hour of cooking — after that the crescent dough goes from
flaky to slightly tough. The filling can be made the day before; warm it
gently before rolling. If you want to gild the lily, drizzle with a
two-ingredient glaze of powdered sugar + milk after they come out.
