The roasted chickpea is one of those pantry recipes that's
nutritionally responsible AND actually good — a rare alignment.
A can of chickpeas, twenty minutes in the air fryer, and you
have something between a snack and a topping that has the
crackle of a corn nut and the slight chew of a candied legume.

The savoury version (paprika, garlic, salt) is the classic. This
maple-cinnamon version is the sweet-snacky variant — pumpkin-pie
adjacent, almost like a candied chickpea or a small protein-rich
gingerbread crumb. The combination of toasted chickpea + warm
spice + caramelised maple is more interesting than any single
ingredient suggests.

The technique requires patience on the drying step. Drain the
chickpeas thoroughly, then pat them very dry on a kitchen
towel. Wet chickpeas steam instead of crisping. Roll them
gently to remove any loose skins (these burn). Toss in oil
first, air-fry for 12 minutes plain, then add the maple-cinnamon
glaze for the final 4 minutes. If you add the maple too early,
it burns; too late, it doesn't caramelise.

The photo shows a small ceramic bowl piled with the finished
chickpeas: deeply amber, glazed and glossy, visible cinnamon
flecks. The kind of thing you reach for on the counter when
walking past, until they're somehow all gone.

## Ingredients

- 2 (14 oz / 400g) cans chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- ¾ tsp kosher salt
- 1½ tbsp pure maple syrup
- 1½ tsp ground cinnamon
- ¼ tsp ground ginger (optional)
- Pinch of cardamom (optional)
- Pinch of flaky sea salt, to finish

## Method

### 01. Dry the chickpeas.
Drain and rinse the chickpeas. Spread on a clean kitchen towel,
pat very dry, roll gently — discard any skins that come off.
Let air-dry 10 minutes for maximum crispness.

### 02. First fry — plain.
In a bowl, toss the chickpeas with olive oil and kosher salt.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Cook 12 minutes, shaking
the basket every 4 minutes.

### 03. Glaze.
In a small bowl, mix maple syrup, cinnamon, ginger, and
cardamom (if using). Pause the air fryer, pour the maple
mixture over the chickpeas, toss gently to coat.

### 04. Second fry — to caramelise.
Return to the air fryer at 380°F (193°C) for 4–5 minutes more,
shaking at the halfway point. The chickpeas should be deeply
glossy and the maple should look matte-glazed (not wet).

### 05. Cool on a tray.
Tip onto a parchment-lined baking sheet in a single layer.
Cool 10 minutes — they crisp up further as they cool.

### 06. Finish.
Sprinkle with flaky sea salt. Eat as snack, or scatter over
porridge, yogurt bowls, or roasted squash salads.

## Notes

Store in an airtight container at room temperature for 3 days
— they soften over time, so they're best within 24 hours.
Refresh in the air fryer for 3 minutes at 350°F if they've gone
chewy. For a savoury version: skip the maple and cinnamon, toss
with 1 tsp smoked paprika + 1 tsp garlic powder + ½ tsp cumin
+ ½ tsp salt before air-frying. For a curry version: 1 tbsp
curry powder + 1 tsp salt + 1 tsp lemon zest. The leftover skins
go in the compost — don't eat them, they're papery and bitter.
