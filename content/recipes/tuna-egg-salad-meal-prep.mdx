The compromise nobody asked for and everybody should embrace: tuna
salad and egg salad combined into one bowl. The egg adds richness
that tuna alone lacks, the tuna brings depth that egg alone misses,
and the result is more interesting than either parent. Plus the
hardboiled eggs add textural variety — small soft bites in the middle
of the flake-and-chunk tuna.

The meal-prep version goes in a three-compartment container: salad in
the largest compartment, fresh berries (strawberries plus blueberries)
in the next, crackers in the third. The crackers are critical — they
become the vehicle, since you're not going to spoon the salad straight
into your mouth at the office (probably). Wheat Thins, Triscuits,
Multigrain Crisps, anything sturdy.

## Ingredients

**For the tuna-egg salad:**
- 2 cans (5 oz each) chunk-light tuna in water, drained well
- 3 large hard-boiled eggs, finely chopped
- ⅓ cup mayonnaise
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 2 tbsp dill pickle, finely chopped (or sweet relish)
- 1 stalk celery, finely diced
- 2 tbsp red onion, finely minced
- 1 tbsp fresh dill or parsley, chopped
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper

**For each meal-prep box (3 boxes):**
- ½ cup mixed berries (strawberries quartered, blueberries whole)
- 8–10 multigrain or wheat crackers

## Method

### 01. Hard-boil the eggs.
If you don't have boiled eggs ready: place 3 eggs in a saucepan,
cover with cold water by 1 inch, bring to a boil, then cover and turn
off the heat. Let sit 10 minutes. Drain and run under cold water to
stop the cooking. Peel.

### 02. Drain the tuna.
Open the cans, press the lid down to drain, then press the tuna
against a sieve with the back of a spoon. Wet tuna salad is the
difference between a sandwich filling and a soup.

### 03. Mix.
In a bowl, combine the drained tuna, chopped eggs, mayo, Dijon,
pickle, celery, red onion, dill, salt, and pepper. Stir until the
mixture is uniform but still chunky.

### 04. Portion.
Divide the salad evenly among three meal-prep containers, in the
largest compartment. Fill the second compartment with mixed berries.
Fill the third with crackers (keep separate so they stay crisp).

### 05. Refrigerate.
Seal and refrigerate. The salad keeps up to 4 days. The crackers in
the closed container will stay crisp for the full week.

## Notes

For a lighter version, swap half the mayo for plain Greek yogurt
(loses a bit of richness, gains tang). For more vegetables, add
finely chopped bell pepper or grated carrot. The crackers can be
swapped for cucumber slices (lower carb), pita chips (more substantial),
or pretzel thins. If you want a hot lunch component, swap the
crackers for a cup of soup in a thermos — tomato or chicken noodle
both work.
