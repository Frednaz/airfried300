The turkey croquette is one of those legacy dishes from the era
when home economics columns in newspapers gave you ideas for using
up the third day of Thanksgiving leftovers. The principle is
sound: shred cold turkey, bind it with mashed potato (also
leftover), add seasoning, form into discs, bread and fry. The
result is hot, crisp, savoury — a totally different dinner from
the cold-turkey-sandwich track that most leftovers default to.

The air fryer is genuinely the right tool for these. Pan-fried
croquettes require a layer of oil in the bottom of a skillet and
careful flipping; deep-fried croquettes are even more involved.
The air fryer crisps both sides at once with a tablespoon of oil
sprayed on, and the cook time is short enough that the inside
stays creamy.

The croquette mix here is engineered to bind without being dense:
shredded turkey + cooled mashed potato + an egg + scallions + sage
+ a small amount of breadcrumbs. The mashed potato is the binder;
the egg holds everything together; the breadcrumbs add structure.
You shape the patties, dredge them in flour-egg-panko, refrigerate
to firm up, then air-fry.

The photo shows the plated finish: about 10 golden-bronze
croquettes scattered on a parchment-lined oval black plate, with
a small ceramic bowl of sour-cream-and-chive dip alongside. A
glass of white wine in the background. This is a dinner-party
appetiser, a meal-prep lunch, or the second-best use of leftover
turkey (the best being a sandwich).

## Ingredients

**For the croquettes:**
- 2 cups shredded cooked turkey (white and dark mixed)
- 1 cup cooled mashed potatoes (leftover is ideal)
- 1 large egg, beaten
- 2 scallions, finely sliced
- 1 tbsp fresh sage, finely chopped (or 1 tsp dried)
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- ⅓ cup panko breadcrumbs (mixed into the patty)

**For the breading:**
- ½ cup all-purpose flour
- 1 large egg, beaten
- 1½ cups panko breadcrumbs
- ½ tsp dried sage
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- ¼ tsp kosher salt
- Olive oil spray

**For the dip:**
- ½ cup sour cream
- 2 tbsp fresh chives, chopped
- 1 tsp lemon juice
- A pinch of salt

## Method

### 01. Mix the croquettes.
In a bowl, gently combine the shredded turkey, mashed potatoes,
egg, scallions, sage, Dijon, salt, pepper, and the ⅓ cup of
panko. Don't overwork — just mix until uniform.

### 02. Shape.
Divide into 10 portions. Form each into a flat disc about 2½
inches across and ½-inch thick. Place on a plate.

### 03. Set up dredging stations.
Three bowls: flour; beaten egg; panko mixed with dried sage,
garlic powder, and salt.

### 04. Bread.
Working one at a time, dust the patty in flour, dip in egg, then
press firmly into the seasoned panko. Return to the plate.

### 05. Chill briefly.
Refrigerate the breaded croquettes 10 minutes — this firms them
up before cooking.

### 06. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Spray each croquette with
olive oil. Place in a single layer (work in 2 batches). Cook 8–9
minutes, flipping at the 5-minute mark, until both sides are
deeply golden.

### 07. Make the dip.
While the croquettes cook, stir together the sour cream, chives,
lemon juice, and salt.

### 08. Serve.
Plate the croquettes on a platter with the dip alongside. Garnish
with extra chives.

## Notes

For non-Thanksgiving cooking, use rotisserie chicken instead of
turkey — same recipe, same technique. The croquettes freeze well
breaded but uncooked: lay on a sheet pan, freeze solid, bag. Cook
from frozen at 400°F for 12 minutes. For a richer dip, swap sour
cream for crème fraîche. The mashed potato is critical — without
it, the croquettes don't bind. If you don't have leftover mash,
boil 2 small potatoes, mash with 2 tbsp butter and 2 tbsp milk,
cool fully before using.
