The tuna burger is the recipe that takes canned tuna out of the
sandwich category and puts it in the burger category — same
ingredient, completely different dish. The shift makes a real
difference: a tuna burger feels like dinner, not lunch. It comes
on a bun. It has lettuce and tomato. It needs a fork-and-knife
because it doesn't quite hold together perfectly. It tastes
distinctly tuna-y in the way the can-opened-at-9am-and-mixed-with-
mayo version doesn't.

The mix is similar to the salmon-patties published earlier but
with a different proportional balance: more tuna, less binder
(the salmon recipe leans heavier on panko because salmon flakes
larger). Canned tuna in water (drained well, pressed dry), beaten
egg, panko, finely diced celery and red bell pepper, scallions,
dijon, lemon zest, salt. Formed into 4 proper burger-sized
patties (about 4 inches across, ½-inch thick), air-fried.

The photo shows them in their natural habitat: assembled burger
on a brioche bun with lettuce, a thick slice of tomato, on a
white scalloped plate with shoestring fries on the side. Three
more burgers staged behind on a tray. This is what summer
weekday-lunch-or-dinner looks like.

A note on the tuna: use tuna packed in WATER, not oil. Oil-packed
tuna is too greasy for this; the water-packed gets fully drained
and pressed dry, then absorbs the egg and panko into a cohesive
patty. The "premium" tuna brands (Tonnino, Wild Planet) make a
real difference; cheap supermarket tuna works but tastes
distinctly less.

## Ingredients

**For the patties:**
- 2 (5 oz / 142g) cans tuna in water, drained AND pressed dry
- 1 large egg, beaten
- ½ cup (50g) panko breadcrumbs
- 1 celery stalk, very finely diced
- 2 tbsp red bell pepper, very finely diced
- 2 scallions, finely sliced
- 2 tbsp mayonnaise
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp lemon zest
- 1 tbsp fresh dill, chopped (or 1 tsp dried)
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- Olive oil spray

**To serve (per burger):**
- 1 brioche bun, split and lightly toasted
- 1 thick slice ripe tomato
- 2 leaves butter lettuce
- 1 tbsp mayonnaise (or a mix of mayo + Dijon)
- A squeeze of lemon juice
- Shoestring fries on the side

## Method

### 01. Drain and DRY the tuna.
Drain both cans, then press the tuna into a sieve with the back
of a spoon to remove every drop of liquid. Then transfer to a
clean kitchen towel and press a second time. Wet tuna won't bind.

### 02. Mix.
In a bowl, flake the dried tuna with a fork. Add the egg, panko,
celery, red pepper, scallions, mayo, Dijon, lemon zest, dill,
salt, and pepper. Mix gently with a spatula until uniform.

### 03. Shape.
Divide into 4 equal portions. Form each into a patty about 4
inches across and ½-inch thick. Place on a plate.

### 04. Chill briefly.
Refrigerate the shaped patties for 10 minutes — they hold
together better when slightly chilled.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 380°F (193°C). Spray the patties on both
sides with olive oil. Place in a single layer in the basket
(work in 2 batches if needed). Cook 9–11 minutes, flipping at
the 5-minute mark, until both sides are deep golden.

### 06. Toast the buns.
While patties cook, lightly toast the brioche buns (cut side up
in the air fryer for 90 seconds, or in a toaster).

### 07. Assemble.
Spread mayo (or mayo + dijon) on both cut sides of the bun.
Layer: bottom bun, lettuce, tomato slice, tuna patty, lemon
juice, top bun.

### 08. Serve.
Plate with shoestring fries (air-fry from frozen for 10 minutes
at 400°F). Eat immediately.

## Notes

For an Asian-leaning version, swap dill for ½ tsp grated ginger
and add 1 tsp soy sauce + 1 tsp sesame oil to the mix. Serve on
a rice bun with wasabi mayo and pickled cucumber. For a low-carb
version, skip the bun and serve the patty over a green salad
with extra mayo-Dijon dressing. The cooked patties keep 3 days
refrigerated and are excellent crumbled into a salad cold.
