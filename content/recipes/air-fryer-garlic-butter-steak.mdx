This is the steak that proves the air fryer can do more than just
chicken tenders. A 1-inch ribeye or New York strip, properly
seasoned and pulled at the right temperature, comes out with a
dark mahogany crust on every face and a rosy-pink medium-rare
interior. The cook time is short — about 8 minutes for a
1-inch-thick steak — but unattended, which means you can be making
the compound butter on the cutting board while the air fryer
handles the actual cooking.

The compound butter is what makes this dish. Soft butter mashed
with finely minced garlic, chopped parsley, lemon zest, salt, and
cracked pepper, formed into a small disc and slapped onto the hot
steak right after it comes out of the basket. The butter melts on
contact, pooling around the meat with garlic-flecked golden
streams. Spoon the juices back over the steak before each bite.

The photo shows the plating that earned this dish its reputation
as a date-night standard: sliced ribeye fanned across a white
speckled plate, butter melting in glistening pools on top, steamed
asparagus alongside, a loaded baked potato with sour cream and
chives, a glass of red wine just visible in the corner. It looks
like the kind of dinner you'd pay $50 for. Made at home it costs
about $14 in ingredients and takes 22 minutes.

The most important thing to get right is **bringing the steak to
room temperature first**. A cold steak from the fridge cooks
unevenly — the outside overcooks before the inside warms up. Take
it out 30 minutes before cooking, no shortcuts.

## Ingredients

**For the steak:**
- 2 ribeye or NY strip steaks, 1 inch thick (10–12 oz / 280–340g
  each)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp coarse black pepper
- 1 tsp garlic powder

**For the compound butter:**
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
- 3 cloves garlic, very finely minced
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, finely chopped
- 1 tsp lemon zest
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp cracked black pepper

**To serve:**
- Steamed asparagus
- Baked potato (see air-fryer-baked-potatoes recipe)
- Flaky sea salt, to finish

## Method

### 01. Bring steaks to room temp.
Take steaks out 30 minutes before cooking. Pat very dry with paper
towels. Wet meat doesn't sear; it steams.

### 02. Make the compound butter.
Mash the softened butter with garlic, parsley, lemon zest, salt,
and pepper using a fork until uniform. Scoop onto a piece of
plastic wrap, roll into a log, and refrigerate 10 minutes (or
press into a small ramekin to set).

### 03. Season the steaks.
Rub all over with olive oil, then season generously with salt,
pepper, and garlic powder, pressing the seasoning into the meat.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place steaks in the basket
with space between them. Cook 7–9 minutes for medium-rare (130°F
internal at the thickest part), 9–11 for medium (140°F). Flip at
the 4-minute mark.

### 05. Rest with butter.
Move steaks to a board. Immediately top each with a thick coin of
compound butter. Tent loosely with foil and rest for 5 minutes —
this is non-negotiable. The butter melts; the steak's juices
redistribute.

### 06. Slice and serve.
Slice each steak across the grain into ½-inch slices. Spoon the
melted butter and juices from the board over the slices. Serve
immediately with asparagus and a baked potato.

## Notes

For a thicker steak (1½-inch), add 2–3 minutes to the cook time.
Doneness chart: 125°F rare, 130°F medium-rare, 140°F medium, 150°F
medium-well. **An instant-read thermometer is the single best $15
upgrade for cooking steak** — the air fryer is precise, but
thermometer-confirmed precision is the only way to nail doneness
every time. The compound butter keeps a week refrigerated and is
also excellent on grilled fish, baked potatoes, or steamed
vegetables. For a flavoured variation, add 1 teaspoon of fresh
thyme leaves or a finely chopped anchovy to the butter mix.
