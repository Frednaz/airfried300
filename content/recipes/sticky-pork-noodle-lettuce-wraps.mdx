The lettuce wrap is one of those dishes that's structurally a salad
but eats like fancy finger food. You put the components on a platter,
let everyone build their own, and watch a normal Tuesday dinner become
slightly festive. This particular version — pork belly, glazed sticky,
rice noodles for substance, baby gem lettuce as the wrapper —
borrows the bones of Vietnamese bún chả without the precision of the
actual dish. It's an approximation, gestured-toward, and that's
honest.

The pork is the work. You start with pork belly chunks (the fatty
cut — don't substitute leaner pork or the glaze won't have anything
to cling to), toss in a marinade of soy, honey, ginger, garlic, and
five-spice. Air fry at high heat for 15 minutes, brushing with extra
marinade halfway, until the edges go properly lacquered — sticky,
caramelised, slightly crisp at the corners.

The photo shows the platter: pale-green lettuce cups arranged on a
coral plate, each filled with rice noodles, fresh herbs, julienned
carrot, and a few chunks of glazed pork on top. They look almost
flower-like, with the pork glowing dark brown against the white
noodles. There's no formal "recipe" for the assembly — you just put
the components out and let people make their own.

## Ingredients

**For the pork:**
- 1¼ lb (570g) pork belly, cut into 1-inch chunks
- 3 tbsp light soy sauce
- 2 tbsp honey
- 1 tbsp dark soy sauce
- 1 tbsp rice vinegar
- 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
- 3 cloves garlic, finely grated
- 1 tsp Chinese five-spice
- 1 tsp toasted sesame oil
- ½ tsp white pepper (or black pepper)

**To serve:**
- 4 oz (110g) dry rice noodles (vermicelli or thin rice stick)
- 2 heads baby gem lettuce, leaves separated
- 2 medium carrots, julienned (or coarsely grated)
- 1 cup fresh coriander leaves
- ½ cup fresh mint leaves
- 1 fresh red chilli, finely sliced (optional)
- 2 limes, cut into wedges
- 2 tbsp roasted peanuts, chopped (optional)

## Method

### 01. Marinate the pork.
In a bowl, whisk together the soy sauces, honey, rice vinegar,
ginger, garlic, five-spice, sesame oil, and pepper. Add the pork
chunks, toss to coat, and let sit for at least 15 minutes (or up
to 24 hours in the fridge).

### 02. Cook the noodles.
Bring a pot of water to the boil. Add the rice noodles, turn off
the heat, and let sit for 4–5 minutes (or per package). Drain,
rinse with cold water, drain again. Drizzle with a teaspoon of
sesame oil to prevent sticking.

### 03. Air fry the pork.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Lift the pork chunks out of
the marinade with a slotted spoon (reserve the marinade). Arrange
in a single layer in the basket. Cook for 12 minutes, shaking the
basket once at 6 minutes.

### 04. Glaze and finish.
Pour the reserved marinade into a small saucepan and simmer for 3
minutes over medium heat until thickened slightly. Brush the pork
chunks generously with the reduced glaze. Return to the basket and
cook 3–4 minutes more at 400°F, until the edges are dark and
lacquered.

### 05. Set up the platter.
Arrange the baby gem leaves on a serving plate. Place a small mound
of rice noodles in each cup. Top with julienned carrot, then a few
chunks of glazed pork. Scatter coriander, mint, and chilli over
the top. Add lime wedges to the side, and sprinkle peanuts if using.

### 06. Eat with hands.
Fold each lettuce cup like a small taco. Squeeze lime over before
eating. The wraps are best eaten the moment they're assembled —
let people build their own.

## Notes

For a leaner version, use pork shoulder instead of belly (cook 3
minutes longer in the air fryer; it crisps less but works). For
chicken: boneless thighs cut into 1-inch chunks, same marinade,
same cook time. The marinade also works on tofu — extra-firm tofu
cubes, press dry first, marinate 30 minutes, air fry 14 minutes.
Don't skip the herbs — they're 30% of the dish, structurally. Make
a double batch of glaze and use the extra as a dipping sauce.
