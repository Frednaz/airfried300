The crispy chicken wing is one of those recipes that hides a
counter-intuitive trick. You'd think the secret to crisp wings would
be more oil, or higher heat, or coating, or some kind of cornstarch
dredge. The actual answer is **baking powder**. A teaspoon of plain
baking powder tossed with dry wings raises the pH of the skin
slightly, breaking down proteins and pulling moisture to the
surface where it evaporates quickly. The result is wings with
shattering crackle, no batter or coating involved.

The technique has two parts. First, dry the wings — pat them with
paper towels until they no longer feel damp, then sit them
uncovered in the fridge for at least 30 minutes if you have time
(the colder, drier surface crisps better). Then toss with baking
powder, salt, garlic powder, and smoked paprika. Air fry at 400°F
for 22 to 25 minutes, flipping once. The skin comes out the colour
of dark amber, crackling on contact.

The photo shows them naked — just the seasoning, no sauce, in a
dark navy bowl. That's how I prefer them, with sauces on the side
to dip into so each wing keeps its crispness. The bowl has buffalo
sauce in the corner; in the photo there's a jar of honey too,
suggesting the hot-honey treatment that became popular around
2018 and has only gotten better since.

A note on the wings: get the "wingettes plus drumettes" mix (or
whole wings cut into 3 pieces, with the tip removed). Frozen wings
work but must be thawed fully and patted very dry.

## Ingredients

**For the wings:**
- 2 lb (900g) chicken wings, separated into drumettes and wingettes
  (tips removed, save for stock)
- 1 tbsp baking powder (NOT baking soda — different thing)
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 1 tsp olive oil

**For serving (sauces on the side):**
- Hot sauce (Frank's RedHot, Crystal, or sambal)
- Honey
- Ranch or blue cheese dressing
- Celery and carrot sticks

## Method

### 01. Dry the wings.
Pat the wings very dry with paper towels — really aggressively dry.
If you have 30 minutes, arrange on a rack over a tray and leave
uncovered in the fridge to dry further. Skip if you're in a hurry.

### 02. Season.
In a large bowl, toss the wings with baking powder, salt, paprika,
garlic powder, pepper, and olive oil until evenly coated.

### 03. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange the wings in a single
layer in the basket — work in two batches if needed. Cook 22–25
minutes, flipping at the 12-minute mark. They should be deeply
amber and the skin should crackle when poked.

### 04. Rest briefly.
Pull the wings onto a paper-towel-lined plate to drain off any
excess rendered fat. Rest 2 minutes.

### 05. Serve.
Pile into a bowl. Serve with hot sauce, honey, ranch, and
celery/carrot sticks on the side. Let people sauce their own.

## Notes

For **hot-honey wings**: mix 3 tablespoons of hot sauce with 2
tablespoons of honey and 1 tablespoon of melted butter — toss the
hot wings in just before serving. For **buffalo wings**: 4 tbsp
melted butter + ½ cup Frank's RedHot, same toss. For
**lemon-pepper**: skip the smoked paprika in the rub, finish with
lemon zest and extra cracked pepper. Don't pre-toss the wings in
sauce before air-frying — the sauce burns and the wings end up
soft. Sauce after cooking, or serve plain with sauce on the side
(my preference).
