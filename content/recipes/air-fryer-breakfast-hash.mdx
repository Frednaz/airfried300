The breakfast hash is one of those dishes that's structurally simple
and emotionally complicated. It's diner food, it's hangover food, it's
the meal you have on Sunday morning when there's nothing in the house
but two potatoes and an egg. The traditional version is made in a
cast-iron skillet on the stovetop, which works but requires babysitting
— you stand over the pan flipping potato cubes around for twenty
minutes while the pepper softens and the egg cooks on a second burner.
The air-fryer version makes all of that simultaneous and unattended.

The potatoes are the project. Baby potatoes (the small, golden,
thin-skinned kind) work best — they're starchy enough to go fluffy
inside but small enough to dice quickly. Quarter the small ones,
sixteenth the bigger ones, you want roughly ½-inch chunks. Toss with
the diced red pepper and a quarter of a yellow onion, add olive oil,
smoked paprika, salt, garlic powder. Eighteen minutes in the basket
at 400°F. By the time you've made the coffee they're done.

The egg is the easy part. Crack one into a small oiled ramekin or
silicone mold, slide it into the basket for the last 4 minutes of the
potato cook. Comes out white-set with a slightly runny yolk, ready to
be tipped onto the hash. The photo is the assembled bowl: turquoise
ceramic, golden hash with red pepper flecks underneath, the fried
egg dominating the centre. A blue-and-white kitchen towel in the
corner, a slight wood-grain background. Brunch.

## Ingredients

**For the hash:**
- 1 lb (450g) baby potatoes (or 2 large russets), diced ½-inch
- 1 red bell pepper, diced ½-inch
- ¼ yellow onion, finely chopped
- 1½ tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp garlic powder
- ¾ tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper

**For the eggs:**
- 2 large eggs
- 1 tsp olive oil (for the ramekins)
- Pinch of salt

**To finish:**
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped (optional)
- Hot sauce (Cholula, Tabasco, or sriracha)

## Method

### 01. Dice the vegetables.
Cut the potatoes into ½-inch dice (uniform sizes cook evenly). Dice
the red pepper to the same size. Chop the onion finely.

### 02. Toss.
In a bowl, combine the potatoes, pepper, and onion. Drizzle with
olive oil, sprinkle with paprika, garlic powder, salt, and pepper.
Toss until everything has a sheen.

### 03. Air fry the hash.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Tip the hash into the basket
in a roughly even layer. Cook 14 minutes, shaking every 5 minutes,
until the potatoes are crisp at the edges and tender in the centre.

### 04. Add the eggs.
Lightly grease 2 small ramekins or silicone egg molds with the
olive oil. Crack an egg into each. Sprinkle with a pinch of salt.
Push the hash to one side of the basket, place the ramekins in the
empty space. Cook 4–6 minutes more, until the whites are fully
set but the yolks are still soft.

### 05. Plate.
Divide the hash between two shallow bowls. Carefully tip an egg
on top of each pile. Scatter parsley and serve with hot sauce on
the side.

## Notes

For a heartier version, add chunks of cooked bacon or breakfast
sausage in the last 5 minutes of cooking. For a vegetarian
upgrade, add a handful of chopped spinach and a sprinkle of feta
after plating. The hash works for meal prep: cook a double batch
of just the hash, refrigerate up to 4 days, reheat in the air
fryer for 4 minutes and add a fresh egg each morning. For yolks
slightly runnier, pull the eggs at 4 minutes; for fully set, go
7 minutes.
