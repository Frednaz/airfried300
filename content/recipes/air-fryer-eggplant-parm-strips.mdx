Eggplant parmesan, the dish, is an Italian-American tradition: slabs
of eggplant breaded and fried, layered with marinara and mozzarella,
baked into a casserole. It's great. It's also an hour-long project
involving multiple sheet pans and a 13×9 dish. This recipe is the
deconstructed appetiser version — eggplant cut into finger-shaped
strips, breaded the same way, air-fried instead of pan-fried, served
with marinara for dipping. Same flavour profile, ten times the
practical convenience.

The trick with eggplant is the moisture. Eggplant is 92% water, and
if you don't pull some of that water out before breading, the
finished pieces go soggy in the middle. The fix is the salt-and-rest
trick: cut your strips, salt them on a wire rack for fifteen minutes,
pat dry with paper towels. The salt draws moisture to the surface,
which you wipe away before breading. This single step is the
difference between properly crisp strips and pale, limp ones.

The breading is the second variable. Panko (Japanese-style
breadcrumbs) plus grated parmesan plus dried herbs — not regular
breadcrumbs, which get heavy and pasty. A quick dredge through
flour, egg, then panko-parm mix. Air fry at 400°F until deeply
golden. The photo shows what you're going for: a stack of breaded
strips on a pink plate with a small ramekin of marinara sauce and a
fork resting in it.

## Ingredients

**For the eggplant:**
- 1 large eggplant (about 1¼ lb / 570g), cut into ½-inch strips
- 1 tsp kosher salt (for the salt-rest)
- ½ cup (60g) all-purpose flour
- 2 large eggs, beaten with 1 tbsp water
- 1½ cups (120g) panko breadcrumbs
- ⅓ cup (30g) finely grated parmesan
- 1 tsp Italian seasoning
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- Olive oil spray

**To serve:**
- 1 cup marinara sauce (jarred is fine), warmed
- 2 tbsp fresh basil, torn (optional)
- Extra grated parmesan (optional)

## Method

### 01. Salt the eggplant.
Trim the ends and cut the eggplant lengthwise into ½-inch slabs,
then cut each slab into ½-inch strips (you want them roughly
finger-shaped). Place on a wire rack over a tray. Sprinkle with the
kosher salt and let sit 15 minutes — moisture will bead on the
surface.

### 02. Pat very dry.
Wipe each strip with paper towels, pressing firmly to remove
moisture and excess salt. This is the most important step.

### 03. Set up the dredging stations.
Three shallow bowls: flour in one; beaten egg and water in the
second; panko, parmesan, Italian seasoning, garlic powder, oregano,
salt, and pepper mixed in the third.

### 04. Dredge.
Working one piece at a time: dust in flour, dip in egg, press
firmly into the panko mixture so it's fully coated. Place on a
plate as you go.

### 05. Spray with oil.
Spray (or brush) the breaded strips all over with olive oil — this
is what makes them golden rather than pale.

### 06. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange the strips in a single
layer with space between them — work in two batches if needed.
Cook 10–12 minutes, flipping at the 6-minute mark, until deeply
golden and crisp.

### 07. Serve.
Stack the strips on a plate. Scatter basil and a final dusting of
parmesan if using. Serve immediately with warm marinara for
dipping.

## Notes

These don't reheat well — the breading softens. Eat within 15
minutes of cooking. For a heartier dish, layer the cooked strips
in a baking dish with marinara and mozzarella, broil 4 minutes —
classic eggplant parm in a fraction of the time. For gluten-free,
swap the panko for gluten-free panko or crushed cornflakes. The
salt-rest trick works for zucchini and yellow squash too — same
breading, slightly shorter cook time (8 minutes).
