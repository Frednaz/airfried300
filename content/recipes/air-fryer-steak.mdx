The pan-seared steak is the classic. The grill-marked steak is
summer. The air-fryer steak is the steak you didn't know was a
contender — and it's better than both of those at one specific thing:
the perfectly even, edge-to-edge medium-rare interior. No undercooked
band near the centre, no overcooked grey ring around the edge. The
hot circulating air cooks the steak from all sides simultaneously,
which is exactly what you want.

The technique is the same as for any good steak. Salt heavily and
early (overnight is best, but 40 minutes on the counter works). Dry
the surface before cooking. Use a thick cut — 1¼ to 1½ inches — and
pull it at 130°F (55°C) for medium-rare, letting carryover heat
finish it during the rest.

The photo shows the result: two ribeyes carved into ½-inch slices on a
wooden board, the pink-to-pinker gradient through the slices, with
the dark crust on top. Off to the side: a small bowl of smoked-
paprika aioli with a spoon in it — the dipping sauce that elevates
plain steak to dinner. A blue linen napkin in the corner, sea salt
in a small dish.

## Ingredients

**For the steaks:**
- 2 ribeye steaks, 12 oz (340g) each, 1¼-inch thick
- 1½ tsp kosher salt (for salting)
- 1 tsp olive oil
- ½ tsp coarse black pepper

**For the smoked-paprika aioli:**
- ½ cup mayonnaise
- 2 cloves garlic, finely grated
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tsp lemon juice
- Pinch of cayenne (optional)
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- 1 tbsp olive oil

**To finish:**
- Flaky sea salt
- Fresh black pepper

## Method

### 01. Salt early.
At least 40 minutes ahead — ideally overnight — pat the steaks dry,
sprinkle generously with the kosher salt all over, and place on a
rack uncovered in the fridge (or on the counter for shorter rest
times). This pulls moisture from the surface, seasons deeper, and
sets you up for proper browning.

### 02. Bring to room temperature.
40 minutes before cooking, take the steaks out and let them come to
room temperature. Pat dry again — any surface moisture is the enemy
of a crust.

### 03. Make the aioli.
While the steaks rest, whisk together the mayo, garlic, paprika,
Dijon, lemon juice, cayenne (if using), salt, and olive oil in a
small bowl. Cover and refrigerate — flavours meld in 30 minutes.

### 04. Oil and pepper.
Rub each steak with a tiny amount of olive oil (just enough to slick
the surface — the steak's own fat does most of the work). Sprinkle
with coarse black pepper.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place the steaks in the basket
with space between them. Cook 4 minutes, then flip. Cook 4–6 minutes
more depending on thickness, until the internal temperature reads
130°F (55°C) for medium-rare, 140°F (60°C) for medium.

### 06. Rest.
Move the steaks to a board and rest 8 minutes — non-negotiable. The
juices need to redistribute or you'll lose half of them when you
slice.

### 07. Slice and serve.
Slice across the grain into ½-inch slices, fanning them out on the
board. Sprinkle with flaky salt. Serve with the aioli for dipping.

## Notes

**Temperatures by doneness**: 125°F rare / 130°F medium-rare /
140°F medium / 150°F medium-well / 160°F well (please don't). Carryover
heat adds about 5°F during the rest — pull 5°F below your target.
For thinner steaks (under 1 inch), reduce time to 6–8 minutes
total. Sirloin and strip steaks work with the same technique — same
times. The aioli is genuinely good as a dipping sauce for fries,
roasted vegetables, or sandwiches; make a double batch.
