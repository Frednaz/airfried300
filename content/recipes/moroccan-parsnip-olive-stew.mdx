The Moroccan vegetable tagine is one of those dishes that proves
the case for spice doing serious heavy lifting. There's nothing
fancy in the pot — parsnips, butternut squash, an onion, some
chickpeas, olives — but the combination of ras-el-hanout, saffron,
cinnamon, and preserved lemon turns it into something that smells
like a souk and tastes like a proper meal.

Ras-el-hanout is the North African spice blend that does most of
the flavour work. The name means "head of the shop" — historically,
the best spices the seller had. A good blend has cumin, coriander,
ginger, cinnamon, cardamom, allspice, paprika, mace, nutmeg, and
sometimes rose petals. You can buy excellent ones at any
supermarket; making your own from scratch is satisfying but not
necessary.

Preserved lemons are the second key ingredient. The fermented
saltiness and floral citrus quality are impossible to substitute.
Use just the rind, finely chopped — the flesh is too salty. If
unavailable, lemon zest plus a teaspoon of salt is the closest
substitute.

The technique is "everything in one pot." Soften the onion, bloom
the spices in the oil, add the vegetables and stock, simmer 35
minutes until the parsnips are sweet and tender. Stir in the
olives and preserved lemon at the end so they retain their bright
punctuation. Finish with mint and parsley.

## Ingredients

**For the stew:**
- 3 tbsp olive oil
- 1 large yellow onion, finely chopped
- 4 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 thumb fresh ginger, grated
- 2 tbsp ras-el-hanout
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- ½ tsp saffron threads (or ½ tsp turmeric as substitute)
- 1 lb (450g) parsnips, peeled and cut into 1-inch chunks
- 1 lb (450g) butternut squash, peeled and cut into 1-inch
  chunks
- 1 (14 oz / 400g) can chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 (14 oz / 400g) can chopped tomatoes
- 3 cups (700ml) vegetable stock
- 1 cup (130g) pitted green olives (Castelvetrano or similar)
- 1 preserved lemon, rind only, finely chopped (or zest of 1
  lemon + ½ tsp salt)
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp black pepper
- 2 tbsp honey or maple syrup

**For the couscous:**
- 1½ cups (250g) couscous
- 1¾ cups (420ml) boiling vegetable stock
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- ½ tsp kosher salt

**To finish:**
- ¼ cup fresh mint leaves, torn
- ¼ cup fresh parsley, chopped
- ⅓ cup toasted slivered almonds
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges
- Greek yogurt or vegan yogurt, to serve (optional)

## Method

### 01. Bloom the spices.
Heat olive oil in a large heavy pot over medium heat. Add the
onion and cook 6 minutes until softened. Add garlic and ginger,
cook 1 minute. Stir in ras-el-hanout, cinnamon, cumin, and
saffron, cook 60 seconds until fragrant.

### 02. Build the stew.
Add the parsnips, squash, and chickpeas. Stir to coat in the
spices. Pour in the chopped tomatoes and stock. Add salt,
pepper, and honey.

### 03. Simmer.
Bring to a boil, then reduce to a gentle simmer. Cover and cook
30–35 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the parsnips and
squash are tender but still hold their shape.

### 04. Add the olives and preserved lemon.
Stir in the olives and preserved lemon rind. Simmer uncovered
5 more minutes to reduce slightly. Taste and adjust salt.

### 05. Make the couscous.
While the stew finishes, place the couscous in a large bowl
with the olive oil and salt. Pour over the boiling stock,
cover with a tight lid or cling film, leave 5 minutes. Fluff
with a fork.

### 06. Plate.
Spoon couscous into wide bowls, ladle the stew over the top.
Scatter mint, parsley, and almonds. Serve with lemon wedges
and yogurt on the side.

## Notes

For a meat version, add 1 lb of diced lamb shoulder at the start
(brown, then continue with the recipe — simmer time becomes 90
minutes total). For more heat, add ½ tsp of cayenne or harissa
paste with the spices. The stew is better the next day — make
ahead, refrigerate, reheat gently. Freezes well 3 months. Without
ras-el-hanout, use 1 tbsp each of cumin and coriander + 1 tsp
each of cinnamon, ginger, and paprika.
