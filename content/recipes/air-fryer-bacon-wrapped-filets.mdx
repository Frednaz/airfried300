The bacon-wrapped filet is the dish that says "this is a special
occasion" without being so fussy that the cook can't actually enjoy
the occasion. It's a steakhouse classic for a reason: filet mignon
is lean, tender, and slightly forgiving on doneness; bacon adds the
salt, fat, and crust that filet itself lacks. The whole thing is
twenty minutes of attention and produces a plate that looks like
a $58 entrée.

The air fryer is the right tool for this for two reasons. First,
its high circulating heat sears the bacon properly all the way
around the filet — something that's hard on a pan (you have to keep
rotating). Second, the precision of pulling at the right
temperature is easier when the cook time is shorter. A 1½-inch
filet at 400°F reaches medium-rare (130°F / 54°C) in about 10
minutes. Use an instant-read thermometer if you have one; if not,
the 10-minute mark is the safe pull-time.

The mushroom sauce is the optional flourish that pushes this from
"steak on a plate" to "actual steakhouse meal." You make it in 5
minutes in a skillet while the steaks rest: brown butter,
mushrooms, garlic, thyme, a glug of red wine, beef broth, simmer
to thicken, finish with another knob of butter. Spoon over the
plated filet.

## Ingredients

**For the filets:**
- 2 filet mignon steaks, 1½ inches thick (6–8 oz / 170–225g each)
- 2 slices thick-cut streaky bacon
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- ½ tsp coarse black pepper

**For the mushroom sauce:**
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, divided
- 8 oz (225g) cremini mushrooms, sliced
- 2 cloves garlic, minced
- 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves
- ⅓ cup dry red wine (or 2 tbsp balsamic + ¼ cup water)
- ½ cup beef broth
- ½ tsp Dijon mustard
- Salt and pepper, to taste

**To serve:**
- Mashed potatoes
- Steamed or buttered green beans
- Fresh parsley, chopped (to garnish)

## Method

### 01. Bring filets to room temp.
Take the filets out 30 minutes before cooking. Pat very dry with
paper towels.

### 02. Wrap with bacon.
Wrap a slice of bacon around the circumference of each filet,
overlapping the ends slightly. Secure with a toothpick if needed
(remove before serving).

### 03. Season.
Rub each filet all over with olive oil. Sprinkle the top, bottom,
and side (not the bacon-wrapped side) with salt and pepper.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Place the filets standing
upright in the basket so the bacon hits the air directly. Cook
9–11 minutes for medium-rare (130°F internal), 11–13 for medium
(140°F), depending on thickness.

### 05. Rest.
Move to a board and rest for 5 minutes, tented loosely with foil.
Resting is critical — the juices need to redistribute or they
pour out when cut.

### 06. Make the mushroom sauce.
While the filets rest, melt 1 tbsp butter in a skillet over
medium-high heat. Add mushrooms and cook 6 minutes, undisturbed
for the first 3 minutes to brown. Add garlic and thyme, cook 30
seconds. Pour in the wine to deglaze — scrape any browned bits —
then add the broth and mustard. Simmer 3–4 minutes to reduce by
half. Off heat, swirl in the remaining 1 tbsp butter. Salt and
pepper to taste.

### 07. Plate.
Place each filet on a plate, spoon the mushroom sauce over and
around it. Add a mound of mashed potatoes and a small pile of
green beans. Scatter parsley over the top.

## Notes

For a medium-well finish (160°F), add 3 more minutes. The
mushroom sauce can be made up to 2 days ahead and reheated.
Without red wine, use 2 tablespoons of balsamic vinegar + ¼ cup
of water — different flavour profile but still excellent. The
bacon won't get fully crispy in this short cook time — that's the
trade-off for keeping the steak at medium-rare. If you want
crispier bacon, partially cook it first (3 min in the air fryer
alone before wrapping), then wrap and finish.
