Bagged kale chips have an unearned bad reputation. The mass-
produced versions are usually dehydrated rather than baked or
fried, which makes them chewy rather than crisp; they're also
generally coated in cashew "cheese" sauces that make them gummy.
The home version — kale leaves torn into chip-sized pieces,
tossed with olive oil and seasoning, then air-fried at low heat
— is something else entirely. Properly made, kale chips shatter
when you bite them. They taste like the green plant they came
from, intensified by salt and spice.

The seasoning here is Old Bay — the Maryland-Chesapeake spice mix
that contains celery salt, paprika, mustard, bay leaves, and a
couple of dozen other ingredients in proportions the McCormick
company has kept secret since 1939. It's traditionally used on
seafood (Maryland crab boils, shrimp), but it makes an
extraordinary seasoning for vegetables. The celery salt and the
bay leaf together give kale a flavour that's somewhere between
"healthy snack" and "actual crab boil." It's distinctive and
slightly addictive.

The photo shows them simply plated — a white round shallow bowl
piled with darkened-green kale fragments, on a blue-and-white-
striped napkin. A second pile of chips on a small piece of
parchment in the background. This is what a kale chip should
look like: deep green, slightly translucent at the edges, with
visible seasoning specks. Not bagged. Not chewy. Crisp.

A note on the kale: curly kale (the green leafy ribboned kind)
works best for chips. Lacinato (dinosaur kale, dark and flat)
also works but is denser and takes a minute longer. Don't try
this with baby kale — too thin, burns instantly.

## Ingredients

- 1 large bunch curly kale (about 6 oz / 170g)
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1½ tsp Old Bay seasoning
- ¼ tsp kosher salt (more if your Old Bay is low-salt)
- ½ tsp lemon zest (optional)

## Method

### 01. Strip the kale.
Tear the leaves off the stems by running your hand down each
stalk — the leaves come off in chip-sized pieces. Discard the
stems (or save for stock).

### 02. Wash and DRY.
Rinse the torn kale leaves in cold water, then spin dry in a
salad spinner. Then pat between paper towels until completely
dry. **Wet kale won't crisp** — this step matters.

### 03. Massage with oil.
In a large bowl, drizzle olive oil over the kale. Use your hands
to massage the oil into every leaf, for about 30 seconds. This
helps the oil distribute and breaks down the leaves slightly so
they crisp more evenly.

### 04. Season.
Sprinkle Old Bay, salt, and lemon zest (if using) over the kale.
Toss to coat.

### 05. Air fry low and slow.
Heat the air fryer to 300°F (150°C) — lower than usual. Place
the kale in the basket in a roughly even layer — some overlap
is fine, they shrink dramatically. Cook 6–8 minutes, shaking
every 2 minutes, until the leaves are crisp to the touch and
slightly papery. They should rustle when you shake them.

### 06. Cool briefly.
Tip onto a plate — they crisp up further as they cool. Eat within
an hour of cooking.

## Notes

Without Old Bay, use 1 tsp smoked paprika + ½ tsp celery salt +
½ tsp garlic powder. For a cheese version, sprinkle 2 tablespoons
of nutritional yeast over the kale before air-frying — gives a
faintly cheesy flavour without dairy. The chips lose their crisp
within a few hours of cooking, so they're really a "make and eat"
recipe rather than a "make and store" one. For a salt-and-vinegar
version, replace the Old Bay with 1 teaspoon of white vinegar
powder + 1 tsp salt. Don't try to make these at higher heat — the
edges burn before the centres crisp.
