The apple fritter is the pastry that exists at the intersection of
"breakfast" and "dessert" and steadfastly refuses to commit to
either side. At a roadside diner in Pennsylvania it shows up at
breakfast next to the eggs. At a bakery in suburban California it
shows up at 3pm next to the donuts. In a home kitchen, it can be
whatever you want it to be.

The traditional version is deep-fried, which is part of what gives
the fritter its craggy, irregular crust — a deep-fried batter
spreads and crisps unevenly, creating dozens of little ridges and
craters. The air-fryer version doesn't quite replicate that
craggy texture (the convection heat is more even, so the crust
sets more uniformly), but what it loses in surface drama it gains
in being doable on a Tuesday morning without a vat of oil.

The technique: cinnamon-spiced batter made with self-rising flour
(or all-purpose plus baking powder), diced apples folded in, dropped
by the heaping tablespoon onto parchment in the basket, air-fried
at 350°F for 8 minutes. Then drizzled with a simple powdered-sugar
glaze that sets to a thin white crackled shell.

The photo shows them tucked into a small basket — about 8 fritters,
golden-bronze with visible glaze drizzles. Tight, irregular shapes.
This is what you want — they shouldn't look like donuts. They should
look like accidents.

## Ingredients

**For the fritters:**
- 1 cup (130g) all-purpose flour
- 2 tbsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- ¼ tsp ground nutmeg
- 1 large egg
- ⅓ cup (80ml) whole milk
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, melted
- 1 large Honeycrisp or Granny Smith apple, peeled, cored, finely
  diced (about 1 cup)

**For the glaze:**
- 1 cup (120g) powdered sugar
- 2 tbsp whole milk (or apple cider)
- ½ tsp vanilla extract
- Pinch of salt

## Method

### 01. Mix the dry ingredients.
In a bowl, whisk together flour, sugar, baking powder, salt,
cinnamon, and nutmeg.

### 02. Mix the wet ingredients.
In a separate bowl, whisk the egg, milk, vanilla, and melted
butter until smooth.

### 03. Combine.
Pour the wet into the dry. Stir gently with a wooden spoon — just
until no dry streaks remain. Lumps are fine. Fold in the diced
apple.

### 04. Drop onto parchment.
Cut a piece of parchment to fit your air fryer basket. Drop the
batter by heaping tablespoons onto the parchment, leaving about
1 inch between each fritter. You should get 8 mounds.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 350°F (175°C). Place the parchment in the
basket. Cook 7–9 minutes — flip at the 5-minute mark — until both
sides are deep golden and a toothpick comes out clean.

### 06. Cool and glaze.
Lift the fritters onto a wire rack (with parchment underneath to
catch drips). Whisk the powdered sugar, milk, vanilla, and salt
into a smooth pourable glaze. Drizzle generously over the warm
(not hot) fritters. Let set 5 minutes.

### 07. Serve.
Best within 30 minutes of glazing. Eat warm with coffee.

## Notes

For an apple-cider glaze (more autumnal), swap the milk in the
glaze for reduced apple cider — simmer 1 cup of cider down to ¼
cup, cool, use 2 tablespoons. The fritters lose their crisp within
a few hours; not a great make-ahead recipe but the batter can be
refrigerated for a few hours before cooking. For a denser, more
fritter-like texture, use bread flour instead of all-purpose. For
a pumpkin version (in October), swap the apple for ½ cup of
pumpkin purée and add an extra ½ teaspoon of pumpkin pie spice.
