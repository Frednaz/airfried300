The appeal of bacon-wrapped asparagus is mathematical. You take a
vegetable that some adults still treat as an imposition (asparagus,
fine, virtuous) and you wrap it in a substance literally nobody
disputes (bacon, joy itself). The net effect is that asparagus
becomes the most popular vegetable in the room — pulled off platters
by people who would otherwise leave it on the plate. It is a small
deception, but a useful one.

The technique is straightforward. Trim the asparagus, take a slice
of streaky bacon, wrap it in a tight spiral up the length of the
spear, then air-fry at 400°F. The bacon crisps and shrinks, locking
itself in place; the asparagus inside steams just enough to lose its
woody crunch but stay snappy. Twelve minutes, no flipping necessary.
The photo shows the finished platter on a yellow grid linen — about
ten spears, fanned out, with a small bowl of dukkah-style spice mix
on the side for dipping.

The dukkah is the optional flourish that pushes this from "side dish"
to "the thing people make conversation about." It's a mix of toasted
sesame seeds, cumin, coriander, and pistachios — Egyptian in origin,
endlessly snackable, the kind of thing you make once and then put on
everything for a week.

## Ingredients

**For the asparagus:**
- 1 lb (450g) fresh asparagus, woody ends snapped off (about 20–24
  spears)
- 8–12 slices thin-cut streaky bacon
- 2 tsp olive oil
- ½ tsp black pepper, freshly cracked
- ¼ tsp red pepper flakes (optional)

**For the dukkah (optional):**
- 2 tbsp sesame seeds, toasted
- 1 tbsp pistachios, toasted and roughly chopped
- 1 tsp coriander seeds, toasted and crushed
- 1 tsp cumin seeds, toasted and crushed
- ½ tsp flaky sea salt

## Method

### 01. Prep the asparagus.
Hold each spear with both hands and snap the bottom — it'll break
where the woody part ends. If the spears are very thick, peel the
bottom inch lightly with a vegetable peeler.

### 02. Wrap with bacon.
Starting at the bottom of each spear, wrap a slice of bacon in a
tight spiral up to just below the tip. The bacon should overlap
slightly with each turn. For thick asparagus, one slice per spear;
for thin, one slice per two spears (wrap them together).

### 03. Season.
Place the wrapped spears on a plate. Drizzle with olive oil,
sprinkle with cracked pepper and red pepper flakes if using. The
bacon brings the salt — don't add more.

### 04. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange the spears in a single
layer in the basket, leaving a little space between them. Cook
10–12 minutes — the bacon should be deeply golden and crisp, the
asparagus tip just slightly browned. No flipping needed.

### 05. Make the dukkah (optional).
While the asparagus cooks, combine the toasted sesame, pistachios,
coriander, cumin, and salt in a small bowl. Roughly crush with the
back of a spoon for a textured mix.

### 06. Serve.
Transfer to a platter and serve immediately with the dukkah on the
side for dipping. Eat with your hands.

## Notes

For a sweeter version, brush the bacon with maple syrup in the last
2 minutes — they go from snackable to candy. For thicker asparagus
(over ½-inch), add 2 minutes to the cook time. The leftover dukkah
keeps in a jar for a month and is excellent over labneh, soft-boiled
eggs, or sliced cucumber. Don't bother prepping these ahead — the
bacon goes pale and rubbery when refrigerated and reheated.
