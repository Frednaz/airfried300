There's a class of food that exists primarily so that adults can eat
something children would eat without feeling like they've made a
mistake. Chicken tenders sit at the top of that list. The version you
got at airports, the version your friend's mom kept in the freezer for
emergencies, the version that comes in dinosaur shapes — all of those
are descended from a real, good idea that just got progressively diluted
on its way to the freezer aisle.

This is the original idea. A buttermilk brine, which both seasons the
chicken and tenderizes it (the lactic acid breaks down a bit of the
protein structure, leaves you with something nearly impossible to
overcook). A breading made with panko — Japanese-style breadcrumbs that
stay crisp because their cell structure is more open than regular
crumbs — boosted with parmesan and smoked paprika for color and depth. A
spritz of oil before the air fryer, which is what makes them brown
instead of just dry out. Twelve minutes at 400°F and you have something
that shatters when you bite it and tastes like the chicken inside.

You can skip the brine if you're in a hurry — they'll still be good,
just less juicy. You can't skip the panko (don't substitute regular
breadcrumbs; the texture isn't the same), and you can't skip the spritz
of oil (without it the breading looks raw and pale, which is the
saddest possible chicken tender outcome). Serve with ketchup, honey
mustard, or ranch — there is no wrong answer.

## Ingredients

**Brine:**
- 1½ lb chicken tenders (or chicken breast cut into ¾-inch strips)
- ½ cup buttermilk
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1 tsp garlic powder

**Breading:**
- 1 large egg, beaten
- 1 cup panko breadcrumbs
- ¼ cup finely grated parmesan
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp kosher salt
- ¼ tsp black pepper
- Olive oil spray (or 2 tbsp olive oil in a spritzer)

**To serve:**
- Fresh parsley, chopped
- Ketchup, honey mustard, or ranch

## Method

### 01. Brine.
In a bowl or zip-top bag, combine the chicken with the buttermilk,
salt, and garlic powder. Toss to coat. Refrigerate for at least 30
minutes — overnight is better, up to 24 hours.

### 02. Set up dredging.
Drain the chicken (don't rinse). In one shallow bowl, beat the egg.
In another, combine the panko, parmesan, paprika, garlic powder, salt,
and pepper.

### 03. Coat.
Working one piece at a time: dip in egg, let excess drip off, then
press firmly into the panko mixture, turning to coat all sides. Set
on a plate. Repeat with all the chicken.

### 04. Spritz.
Spray or brush the coated tenders all over with olive oil. This is
the difference between pale and golden — don't skip it.

### 05. Air fry.
Heat the air fryer to 400°F (200°C). Arrange the tenders in a single
layer, not touching — work in two batches if needed. Cook 10–12
minutes, flipping at the 6-minute mark, until deeply golden and
crisp.

### 06. Rest and serve.
Let them rest for 2 minutes (the inside finishes carrying-over). Scatter
parsley over the top and serve with the dipping sauce of your choice.

## Notes

These freeze beautifully after breading, before air-frying — lay them on
a sheet pan, freeze solid, then bag. Cook from frozen at 400°F for 14
minutes. The brine is the most important step; if you're going to skip
something, skip the parmesan instead. For gluten-free, swap the panko
for gluten-free panko or crushed cornflakes — the cornflake version is
arguably better.
